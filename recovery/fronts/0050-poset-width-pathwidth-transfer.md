## question

For the antichain root A_n, does every optimal comparison strategy induce a poset/state graph of bounded pathwidth or treewidth independent in some way, or can adversarial states on optimal paths force super-polynomial pathwidth for all-n optimal strategies?

## basis

General poset complexity uses treewidth/pathwidth ideas but transfer from arbitrary posets to antichain is not automatic; need all-n antichain-specific argument.

## falsifier

Exhibit an infinite family of n with all height-S(n) optimal strategies producing states whose comparability/extension graphs have pathwidth growing super-polynomially in n, or prove bounded pathwidth for some canonical optimal strategy.

## implication

Bounded pathwidth would suggest dynamic programming by separators is possible; super-polynomial would block separator-based exact algorithms and relate to tree decomposition complexity.

**Findings (deduction/experiment from literature in repo):**
- Winning frontier (barrier doc): (s×L) grid of chains + extra between-level edges gives 2^((1/4-o(1))n(log n)^2) nonisomorphic winning states at budget k=S(n)+o(n) (extends to exact S(n) by Sergeev). These are enumeration barriers, not runtime lower bounds.
- Structure: Hasse/comparability graphs are “path-like” layered (s chains, height L). Width >= s; pathwidth of s-wide layered graphs is Θ(s). For the large-count balanced regime s=L=√n gives m ~ n log n << M ~ n^(3/2), so states are Θ(√n)-pathwidth (polynomial), not super-polynomial.
- Permutohedron has treewidth Θ(n log n). Comparability graph doesn’t determine F(n=7); transfer from arbitrary posets to antichain is not automatic (repo notes). Optimal strategy is a decision tree over states.
