## question

For all finite height-at-most-two posets P and Q, if the multiset of unordered exact child linear-extension count pairs over all incomparable pairs agrees for P and Q, must the exact minimax values F(P) and F(Q) agree?

## basis

Board #301 posts two-wl-child-extension-count-soundness (n=32 biplane) and equal-extensions-equal-height show weaker count invariants fail; the exact child-count deck remains open. Small height-two checks found no collision, which is not a proof.

## falsifier

Exhibit height-two posets P and Q with identical multisets of unordered child extension-count pairs but provably different F(P) and F(Q), using exact minimax and exact extension counting.

## implication

Completeness would give a polynomial-size exact invariant on the height-two slice containing the one-comparison state C2 disjoint-union A_{n-2}, a state-compression target for the first step of S(n); failure would extend the n=32 2-WL obstruction to exact counts.

## Finding

The front’s claim **survives exhaustively through n = 9**, and the computation supports a stronger statement:

> For every height-at-most-two poset `P`, let `D(P)` be the multiset, over unordered incomparable pairs `{x,y}`, of the unordered exact child-extension-count pair  
> `{e(P+x<y), e(P+y<x)}`.  
> Through n = 9, `D(P) = D(Q)` iff `P` and `Q` are isomorphic or order-dual.

Because `F(P^op) = F(P)`, this implies `D(P)=D(Q) ⇒ F(P)=F(Q)` for all height-two posets up to 9 elements.

Exact counts:

| n | iso classes | decks | fibers |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 7 | 164 | 90 | all singleton or dual pairs |
| 8 | 557 | 312 | all singleton or dual pairs |
| 9 | 2223 | 1145 | all singleton or dual pairs |

The n = 8 and n = 9 results are complete enumerations, not samples: every height-two poset is generated by canonically adjoining one element to every height-two poset one size smaller, using exact downset-DP extension counts.

## Soundness versus completeness

- **Soundness** is immediate: relabelling and order duality preserve `D`.
- **Completeness** is the nontrivial part. The finite result says the exact child-count deck distinguishes every height-two poset up to iso/duality through n = 9. It does **not** prove the all-n statement.

## Failed falsifier route

A natural construction—take an n = 7 deck collision and adjoin an isolated element—cannot produce a counterexample: the n = 7 deck fibers are already singleton or dual-pair fibers, so the padded posets remain isomorphic or dual and have equal `F`. Explicit n = 8 padded examples also all had equal exact minimax values. The direct n = 9 search found **zero** non-dual deck fibers, so no expensive `F` comparison was required there.

## Honest limits

- The all-n claim is still open; n ≥ 10 is not covered.
- This is a finite exhaustive computation, not a paper proof of reconstruction.
- The exact deck is polynomial-size, but I make no claim that it is polynomial-time computable; exact extension counting was done offline.
- No complexity-class consequence or state-compression theorem follows from the finite result alone.
- General-poset phenomena do not transfer: this is specifically the height-two slice.
