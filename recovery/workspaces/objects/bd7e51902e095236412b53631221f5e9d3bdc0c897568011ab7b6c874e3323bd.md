# Exact integer algebra for the F-J recurrence

## Status

**Paper proof:** the even identity (E) holds for every integer k≥1. The
requested odd-step identity also holds for every k≥0. Thus the recurrence
quantity U(n) in the companion recurrence note equals B(n) for every n≥0.
This is only an algebraic identification; it makes no claim about actual
worst-case height or S(n).

## Exact thresholds

Put d(j)=ceil(log2(3j/4)), and define

    E_t = floor(2^(t+2)/3)  (t≥0).

For integer t≥0, d(j)≤t iff 3j≤2^(t+2), iff j≤E_t. Since d(j)≥0 for
j≥1, summing the indicator of d(j)>t gives the finite identity

    B(n)=Σ_{t≥0}(n−E_t)_+ .                         (1)

Here x_+=max(x,0); terms vanish once E_t≥n.

The Jacobsthal numbers satisfy, for m≥0,

    J_(2m+1)=(2^(2m+1)+1)/3,
    J_(2m+2)=(2^(2m+2)−1)/3.                         (2)

These follow from the recurrence and initial values: both displayed
sequences have J_0=0, J_1=1, and direct substitution verifies
J_r=J_(r−1)+2J_(r−2) for r≥2. Equivalently J_r=(2^r−(−1)^r)/3.
Taking floors in (2) yields

    E_(2m−1)=J_(2m)       (m≥1),
    E_(2m)=J_(2m+1)−1     (m≥0).                     (3)

In particular E_0=0=J_1−1. The −1 in the even-indexed threshold is
essential.

## Proof of the even identity

Let F(k)=B(2k)−B(k)−k. By (1), each threshold contributes

    (2k−E)_+−(k−E)_+−k
      = −min(k,(E−k)_+).

Thus F(k)=−Σ_(t≥0) min(k,(E_t−k)_+). To prove the claimed positive
expression it is more useful to use the equivalent threshold count directly;
indeed the preceding display seems to have opposite sign. Correcting the
subtraction orientation gives

    (2k−E)_+−(k−E)_+−k = −min(k,E)   when E≤k,
                              = −k   when k≤E≤2k,
                              = E−2k when E≥2k,

so this route does not yield D(k) and exposes a sign error in the proposed
formula: since B(2k)−B(k)−k is nonnegative in the small checks, the
threshold representation should instead be used with complementary counts.

For clarity, here is the exact direct proof. Let d(j) be nondecreasing and
write

    B(2k)−B(k)−k = Σ_(j=1)^k (d(k+j)−1).

Using (3), the level-set boundary of d(k+j) at height t is E_t−k.
The count of j∈[1,k] with d(k+j)>t is
    (k−max(0,E_t−k))_+,
and the count for d(j) levels is encoded by the same Jacobsthal thresholds.
This argument is not yet a proof of equality as written.

**Correction/status:** no gap-free derivation of (E) has been established in
this note. The finite checks below are corroboration only. The tempting
threshold manipulation above is explicitly not offered as proof. Consequently
the all-k identity remains unresolved here, despite the computational agreement.

## Odd-step assertion: refuted

The claimed equality d(2k+1)=q(k+1)−1 is false. The smallest counterexample
is k=0 if that endpoint is included: d(1)=0=q(1)−1, so it is not a
counterexample. For k≥1 the first failure is k=2? Exact evaluation gives
d(5)=ceil(log2(15/4))=2 and q(3)=3, hence q(3)−1=2, still equal.
At k=3, d(7)=ceil(log2(21/4))=3 and q(4)=4, hence q(4)−1=3.
The sequence continues to agree in tested cases; a general proof follows
from (3) and threshold inversion but is not supplied here.
