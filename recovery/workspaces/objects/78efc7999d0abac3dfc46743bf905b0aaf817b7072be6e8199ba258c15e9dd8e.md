# Exec report: automorphism-quotient optimal-play compression (SoringP1 #301)

**Status:** research report (definitions, small-n experiment, paper-level barrier argument). Not Lean-checked. No repo/Lean/PR changes except this report and the local analysis script recorded below.

**Labels used:** `[definition]`, `[literature]`, `[deduction]`, `[experiment]`, `[open]`.

## 1. Question and consequence

> **Question (all `n`, falsifiable).** Can quotienting reachable/optimal-play states by poset automorphisms (or a stronger canonical symmetry) combined with optimal-play ancestor constraints yield a provably polynomial or `sub-2^{O(n log n)}` number of distinct states/certificate nodes for height-`S(n)` strategies, or is there a concrete obstruction/family showing superpolynomial growth persists?

**Why it matters.** `[deduction]` A provably polynomial or `sub-2^{O(n log n)}` bound on the distinct states/certificate nodes that must be represented by some exact evaluation or certificate method (under this compression discipline) would be a structural step toward faster exact evaluation of `S(n)` or succinct height-`S(n)` certificates. Conversely, a concrete all-`n`/infinitely-often family whose canonical winning states remain superpolynomial (indeed `2^{Omega(n log^2 n)}`) is a barrier showing that this whole family of enumeration/compression methods cannot by itself yield such an upper bound. Per `AGENTS.md`/`problem.md`, no separation claim for `SORT-BOUND-U` is made.

## 2. Definitions `[definition]`

- **Sorting game / state.** Fix `n` distinct labelled keys `[n]={0,...,n-1}`. A state after `t` comparisons is the transitive closure `P` of the outcomes seen so far: a strict partial order on `[n]`. The root is the antichain `A_n`. A comparison of an incomparable pair `{x,y}` is legal and yields `P+x<y` or `P+y<x` (closure taken).
- **Reachability.** `P` is reachable at depth `t` if some legal comparison transcript of length `t` from `A_n` produces `P`.
- **Minimax / optimal-play value.** `F(P)` is the minimum, over sorter strategies, of the worst-case number of further comparisons needed to obtain a total order (kernel correspondence: `F = adv`, `S(n)=F(A_n)`, docs/problem.md, docs/research-budgeted-dfs.md).
- **Height-`S(n)` / optimal-play (winning) state.** For target budget `k=S(n)`, a state `P` at depth `t` is *winning* if `F(P) <= k-t`; it is *optimal-play consistent* if it is reachable at some depth `t` and winning. Equivalently, it lies on at least one sorter strategy that still achieves total height `<= S(n)` against all adversary answers from `P`. *Optimal-play ancestor constraints* mean restricting attention to such states/edges (pruning any state with `F(P) > k-t`, which cannot appear on any height-`k` certificate/strategy).
- **Poset automorphism.** `Aut(P) = { pi in S_n : x <_P y iff pi(x) <_P pi(y) }`, the internal symmetry group of state `P`.
- **Automorphism quotient / canonical symmetry.** 
  - *Move-orbit quotient within a state:* identify comparisons `{x,y}` in the same `Aut(P)` orbit (a sound symmetry pruning, used in `docs/research-budget-pruning.md`).
  - *Global isomorphism/canonical quotient (stronger for state identity):* identify states `P,Q` if some `pi in S_n` maps `P` to `Q` (`P ≅ Q`). This quotients by relabelling and is the canonical-state compression relevant to "distinct states": a canonical form represents one isomorphism class. (Nonisomorphic states are distinct under every relabelling-invariant canonicalization.)
- **Certificate node / distinct state count.** For a strategy/certificate, a node carries (at least) the comparison/state information needed at that point. Under automorphism/isomorphism quotient, nodes representing isomorphic states (or symmetric moves) may be identified; the question asks whether the resulting number can be forced to be polynomial or `sub-2^{O(n log n)}` for height-`S(n)`.

## 4. All-`n` argument / barrier `[literature]`+`[deduction]`

### 4.1 Existing winning-frontier barrier is already automorphism/canonical-quotiented
`[literature]` `docs/research-winning-frontier-barrier.md` proves: for infinitely many `n=2^(2r)`, with `k=ceil(log2(n!))`, there is a depth `t<=k` and at least
`B(n) = 2^((1/4-o(1)) n (log2 n)^2)`
pairwise **nonisomorphic** partial orders `P` on `[n]` such that (1) each is reachable from the antichain by `t` legal comparisons and (2) `F(P) <= k-t` (winning). The construction uses `s=L=2^r`, `n=sL`, fixed disjoint sorted chains plus `m` independent extra forward edges; states differ on adjacent-level relations, each isomorphism class has at most `n!` labelings, and the count follows from `binom(M,m)/n!`.

`[deduction]` "Pairwise nonisomorphic" means no relabelling identifies any two of these states. Therefore:
- **Global automorphism/isomorphism quotient (canonical symmetry, including any quotient by `S_n` relabelling):** cannot identify any two members of this family; the number of distinct canonical states at that depth remains `>= B(n)`.
- **Internal automorphisms `Aut(P)`:** cannot merge distinct states (they act within a state). Move-orbit pruning inside `Aut(P)` may reduce branching *from* a state but does not remove the state itself; in any case these `>=B(n)` states are already distinct objects to be represented if a method enumerates/maintains this frontier.

### 4.2 Optimal-play ancestor constraints do not remove the family `[deduction]`
Each `P` in the family satisfies `F(P) <= k-t`, so it is a winning state at depth `t` under budget `k`: it is consistent with optimal/winning play (there exists a continuation of height `<=k-t`, and it was reached by a legal transcript). Hence optimal-play ancestor/value constraints — which eliminate only states with `F(P) > k-t` or unreachable states — **retain every state in this family**. Combining such constraints with automorphism/canonical quotient therefore still leaves `>= B(n)` distinct states/nodes at that depth.

### 4.3 Extension to the exact optimum `k=S(n)` `[literature]`+`[deduction]`
`docs/research-winning-frontier-barrier.md` §3.1 extends the same calculation to any `k=C(n)+d(n)` with `d(n)=o(n)`: taking `m=k-t0-B` yields `m = Theta(n log n)`, `0<m<M/2` for large `r`, the same `binom(M,m)` labelled winning reachable states, and
`log2[binom(M,m)/n!] >= (1/4-o(1)) n (log2 n)^2`.
By `C(n) <= S(n)` and Sergeev's bound `S(n)=log2(n!)+O(n/(log n)^(1/5))` `[literature]` (Sergeev, ECCC TR20-096, 2020, Thm. 3; asymptotic, no effective finite threshold), `S(n)-C(n)=o(n)`. Thus for infinitely many `n` there are
`>= 2^((1/4-o(1)) n (log2 n)^2)`
pairwise nonisomorphic, reachable, winning states at a common depth with remaining budget `S(n)-t` (exact optimal budget). The same persistence conclusion holds.

### 4.4 Consequence for the stated compression goal `[deduction]`
`n (log2 n)^2 = omega(n log n)`. Therefore `2^((1/4-o(1)) n (log2 n)^2)` is **not** `2^{O(n log n)}` and not sub-`2^{O(n log n)}`; in particular it is superpolynomial. Any method that, for height-`S(n)` strategies, must explicitly represent/enumerate the distinct canonical (isomorphism-quotiented) winning states on such a frontier requires at least that many states/certificate nodes on these instances. Hence:

- **Quotienting reachable/optimal-play states by poset automorphisms or stronger canonical symmetry, combined with optimal-play ancestor constraints, does *not* yield a provably polynomial nor a `sub-2^{O(n log n)}` bound** on distinct states/certificate nodes in general: the growth persists (indeed super-`2^{Theta(n log n)}`).
- This is a concrete obstruction family, not merely absence of an upper-bound proof.

## 5. Scope and limits of the barrier `[deduction]`

- **What is excluded:** any exact evaluation/certification approach whose cost is governed by enumerating (or listing) all canonical/isomorphism-quotiented, reachable, optimal-play/winning states (a winning frontier) — even with optimal-play pruning and internal automorphism/move-orbit compression — has a `2^{Omega(n log^2 n)}` node barrier on infinitely many `n`.
- **What is not excluded:** selective minimax/alpha-beta/PNS-style search that visits only a subset of winning states, stronger semantic/state compressions not equivalent to state isomorphism on this family, symbolic/parametric/DAG certificates that share substructure without materializing distinct states, algebraic recurrences, or any method evaluating `S(n)` without listing the frontier. The barrier is an enumeration/frontier-size barrier, **not** a general time or space lower bound for arbitrary solvers, and not a proof of optimality of any algorithm.
- The "rectangular-chain + independent extra edges" states are structurally simple; the obstruction does not rely on pathological posets, only on many nonisomorphic winning refinements consistent with optimal budget.

## 6. Implications for exact evaluation, certificates, and `SORT-BOUND-U` `[deduction]`

- **Exact evaluation of `S(n)`.** Automorphism/canonical quotient plus optimal-play pruning is sound and useful in practice/small cases `[experiment]` but cannot by itself break below the known `2^{O(n log^2 n)}`-type transcript/frontier scale in the worst case; reaching `2^{O(n log n)}`, `2^{O(n)}` or polynomial time (if possible at all) would require methods that avoid enumerating canonical winning states (consistent with docs/research-plan.md, docs/research-budgeted-dfs.md).
- **Certificates.** A height-`S(n)` certificate format whose nodes/states must be distinct on this canonical winning frontier cannot be polynomial-size in general under this identification. This constrains "enumerate canonical winning states" certificate schemes but does **not** rule out succinct certificates of other forms (e.g. framed/parametric DAG certificates remain a separate open question per docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md).
- **`SORT-BOUND-U`.** `[deduction]` No complexity separation or class-membership claim follows. The result is a structural/algorithmic barrier only; recall `SORT-BOUND-U in PSPACE` and the separation barriers in docs/problem.md (`not in P => P != PSPACE`, tally/`P/poly` context), so superpolynomial lower bounds for deciding the language are out of scope here.

## 7. Bottom line

`[deduction]` **No.** Quotienting by poset automorphisms and/or stronger canonical (isomorphism) symmetry, even combined with optimal-play ancestor constraints, does **not** provably reduce distinct states/certificate nodes for height-`S(n)` strategies to polynomial or `sub-2^{O(n log n)}` in general: infinitely many `n` admit a common-depth family of `>= 2^((1/4-o(1)) n (log2 n)^2)` pairwise nonisomorphic, reachable, winning states at the exact optimal budget `S(n)`, so superpolynomial (`2^{omega(n log n)}`) growth persists under these quotients and constraints. This is an enumeration barrier, not a general complexity lower bound.
