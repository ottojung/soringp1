## 1. Executive summary

**[open]** The question remains open. No all-n construction of a polynomial-size
permutation-framed certificate for *exact* optimal sorting, and no proof that
none exists, was found.

**[deduction]** The natural exact optimal strategies that are currently known
and polynomially certifiable are the Ford–Johnson strategies. They are exact
for all `n` for which `S(n)` equals the Ford–Johnson bound (in particular for
`n <= 22` and `n = 20,21`; `S(23)` is open). Ford–Johnson certificates have
`O(n^2)` comparison nodes and are accepted by the existing framed checker.
Thus polynomial certificates are known in the *near-optimal / Ford–Johnson*
regime.

**[deduction]** For exact optimality in the asymptotic regime, Ford–Johnson
has a `Theta(n)` comparison surplus over the information bound, whereas
Sergeev gives `S(n)-log2(n!) = O(n/(log n)^(1/5)) = o(n)`. Any exact
certificate must therefore operate with only `o(n)` bits of total
imbalance/forgetting per path (certificate entropy identity, §4 of
`research-framed-certificate-frontier.md`).

**[deduction]** A straightforward join-based compression of the optimal
decision tree is blocked: the single-policy optimal DAG has no repeated
labelled posets for `n <= 6` (`2*n!-1` nodes), so hash-consing saves nothing
without forgetting. Joining distinct posets costs forgetting bits, and merging
the `n!` leaves of an exact tree into a polynomial number of leaves requires
`Omega(n log n)` bits of forgetting, exceeding the `o(n)` budget. Hence a
bounded "merge width" in the sense of cheap joins does not follow from current
evidence.

**[conjecture]** The most plausible obstruction is that exact optimal play
requires superpolynomially many distinct slot-poset states even after the
framed permutation machinery is used. This is consistent with, but not proved
by, the winning-frontier barrier (`2^{Theta(n log^2 n)}` nonisomorphic
winning states) and with the fast growth of the quotient node counts below.

**[open]** Implication for `SORT-BOUND-U`: membership in NP via framed DAGs
remains unproved and unrefuted. No separation claim is made.

## 2. Fixed model

**[definition]** `S(n)` is the minimum worst-case height of a deterministic
adaptive comparison decision tree sorting `n` distinct labelled keys.

**[definition]** A framed certificate is an acyclic DAG whose nodes carry
comparison instructions and whose arcs carry outcome labels and slot
permutations. At a node the guaranteed strict partial order is extended by
the comparison, transitively closed, and permuted. At a join, guaranteed
orders are intersected. A terminal node must have a total order on slots.
Depth is the longest comparison path. The checker
`scripts/check_framed_strategy.py` verifies soundness in time polynomial in
`n` and graph size.

**[deduction]** If, for every `n`, some framed DAG of size `poly(n)` has
depth `S(n)`, then `SORT-BOUND-U in NP`. No such all-n theorem is known.

## 3. Candidate width notions

Three width parameters are used below. They are not equivalent; each captures
a different possible compression route.

### 3.1 Poset width (Dilworth width)

**[definition]** For a poset `P` on `n` slots, `w(P)` is the size of a
maximum antichain. By Dilworth's theorem this equals the minimum number of
chains in a partition of `P`. It is polynomial-time computable (maximum
matching in the comparability graph).

**[observation]** `w(P) <= n` always. Bounded `w` does not by itself imply a
polynomial number of posets: total orders have `w=1` and there are `n!` of
them. So `w` is not a sufficient certificate-width parameter unless combined
with a restricted family (e.g. chain sizes as parameters).

### 3.2 Merge decomposition width

**[definition]** For a poset that is a disjoint union of `r` chains, the
merge width is `r`, the number of sorted runs. More generally, for a chosen
chain decomposition `P = C_1 ⊔ ... ⊔ C_r`, define `md(P)=r`. For merge-sort
states `md=2`; for a fully sorted state `md=1`.

**[deduction]** If a strategy's reachable states are disjoint unions of at
most `r` chains and the state is described by the `r` chain lengths, then
there are `O(n^r)` states for fixed `r`, hence a polynomial certificate
when `r=O(1)` or `r=O(log n)`. This is the mechanism behind the merge-sort
certificate.

### 3.3 Join in-degree / merge width of a DAG

**[definition]** For a framed DAG `G`, let `mu(G)` be the maximum number of
incoming edges of any node (the maximum number of branches merged by one
join). Call this the *join merge width*.

**[deduction]** `mu(G)=1` means `G` is a tree (up to node relabelling); no
compression by joining distinct histories occurs. `mu(G)` can be exponential
without increasing node count much, so bounded `mu` is neither necessary nor
sufficient for polynomial size. It is recorded here because joins are the
only way to merge distinct histories.

### 3.4 Forgetting loss (entropy width)

**[definition]** (from `research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` §4). For an
edge from annotation `P` to child annotation `Q` arising from feasible outcome
`b` with `m_b=e(TC(P+b))`, set `q_b=m_b/e(P)` and forgetting loss
`lambda_b = log2(e(Q)/m_b) >= 0`. The binary outcome entropy is
`H=-sum q_b log2 q_b`.

**[deduction]** For any sound certificate of depth at most `k`,

`E[sum_edges (1-H+lambda)] = E[D]-log2(n!) <= k-log2(n!)`.

Thus the total per-path imbalance plus forgetting is at most
`k-log2(n!)`. For `k=S(n)` this budget is `S(n)-log2(n!) = o(n)`.

## 4. Constructions and measured widths

### 4.1 Bubble sort certificate

**[literature]** `triangular_bubble_certificate(n)` in
`scripts/check_framed_strategy.py` produces a single path with
`n(n-1)/2` comparison nodes and one terminal node. Verified by
`tests/test_framed_strategy.py` for `n <= 12`.

**[experiment]** Node count: `n(n-1)/2 + 1 = O(n^2)`.

**[deduction]** `mu=1` (no joins). The poset width along a path can be
`Theta(n)`; e.g. after partial bubble passes many elements remain mutually
incomparable. Thus bubble shows that poset width `Theta(n)` is compatible
with polynomial size, but it uses `Theta(n^2)` comparisons, far above `S(n)`
for large `n`.

### 4.2 Merge sort certificate

**[literature]** `research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` §2 gives an all-n
framed DAG for adaptive merge sort. A merge of sorted queues of lengths
`p,q` has `pq` states; summed over a binary merge schedule this is
`n(n-1)/2` comparison nodes. Full-slot permutations make it polynomial-bit.

**[deduction]** Merge decomposition width `md=2` during every merge; join
merge width is small (merges are sequentially composed, not joined). Depth
for powers of two is `n log2 n - n + 1`, which exceeds `log2(n!)` by
`(log2 e - 1)n + O(log n) = Theta(n)`. Therefore this certificate is not
exact for large `n` (Sergeev's `o(n)` surplus), although it is polynomial
and sound.

### 4.3 Ford–Johnson certificate

**[literature]** `research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` §3 gives a
plausible all-n framed certificate for the standard Ford–Johnson bound
`F_FJ(n)=sum_{j=1}^n ceil(log2(3j/4))`, subject to an independent audit
of lifted permutations and stage widths. It maintains one sorted chain plus
pending elements with rank windows; insertion by binary search uses
`O(n)` query nodes per insertion, hence `O(n^2)` total nodes.

**[deduction]** Merge decomposition width is `1 + (number of pending
elements)`, which can be `Theta(n)`. Node count remains `O(n^2)` because
pending count decreases. The Ford–Johnson bound is exact for the known values
`S(n)` through `n=22` (and `20,21` are information-tight), so this
certificate is an exact optimal certificate in all currently resolved small
cases.

Computed Ford–Johnson vs information bound:

| n | F_FJ(n) | ceil(log2 n!) | S(n) known? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 11 | 26 | 26 | 26 |
| 12 | 30 | 29 | 30 |
| 16 | 46 | 45 | 46 |
| 20 | 62 | 62 | 62 |
| 21 | 66 | 66 | 66 |
| 22 | 71 | 70 | 71 (FJ-optimal if exact) |

**[literature]** `S(23)` is open. Thus no small-n case currently distinguishes
an exact optimal certificate from the Ford–Johnson certificate.

### 4.4 Exact single-policy optimal DAG

**[experiment]** `scripts/strategy_dag_probe.py` builds, for `n <= 6`, the
minimax DAG for one optimal policy (first optimal move at each state) and the
union of all optimal strategies.

| n | S(n) | single-policy labelled nodes | all-optimal labelled nodes | single-policy S_n-quotient | all-optimal S_n-quotient |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 1 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 2 |
| 3 | 3 | 11 | 19 | 4 | 5 |
| 4 | 5 | 47 | 181 | 7 | 12 |
| 5 | 7 | 239 | 1551 | 11 | 19 |
| 6 | 10 | 1439 | 65551 | 27 | 134 |

**[experiment]** For `n <= 6`, `single-policy labelled nodes = 2*n! - 1`
exactly, and `nodes = unshared tree nodes`; hash-consing saves zero nodes.
Thus every node in the chosen optimal policy has a distinct labelled poset.

**[experiment]** The S_n-quotient counts grow from `1,2,4,7,11,27`
(single policy) and `1,2,5,12,19,134` (all optimal). The jump at `n=6` is
not polynomial-looking in the tested range, but six points are not a proof.

**[deduction]** These counts are for labelled poset states, not for framed
certificates. A framed certificate may use weaker slot posets and
permutations, so this table is an upper bound on the difficulty of
*lossless* compression, not a lower bound for forgetful compression.

### 4.5 Merge (two-chain) exact costs

**[experiment]** `scripts/obstruction_probe.py` computes exact minimax costs
`M(a,b)` for posets that are disjoint unions of two chains. Selected
violations of the information bound:

| chains (a,b) | e(P) | ceil(log2 e) | M(a,b) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| (2,4) | 15 | 4 | 5 |
| (2,6) | 28 | 5 | 6 |
| (3,5) | 56 | 6 | 7 |
| (4,5) | 126 | 7 | 8 |
| (5,5) | 252 | 8 | 9 |

**[deduction]** Even the simplest merge posets can require one comparison more
than the information bound. Exact optimal sorting must account for such
merge-level obstructions; a certificate that forgets which merge case
occurred incurs at least one bit of forgetting for these cases.

## 5. Obstruction analysis

### 5.1 Lossless joins are unavailable in single optimal plays

**[experiment]** For `n <= 6`, the single-policy optimal DAG has all distinct
labelled posets, so no two distinct histories in that policy can be joined
without changing the annotation to a strictly weaker poset. Any join must
therefore incur positive forgetting loss `lambda > 0`.

**[conjecture]** This no-repeat property extends to all `n` for at least one
optimal policy. If true, a polynomial-size exact certificate cannot be
obtained by merely sharing identical states in a single optimal tree.

### 5.2 Entropy/forgetting budget

**[deduction]** For an exact certificate with depth `k=S(n)`, the total
per-path forgetting plus imbalance is at most `S(n)-log2(n!) = o(n)`. For
`n=12`, `S(12)-ceil(log2 12!) = 1`, so an exact certificate for `n=12`
has at most one bit of total forgetting/imbalance along every path.

**[deduction]** Joining two histories whose states have linear-extension
counts differing by a factor of `2^r` costs at least `r` bits of forgetting
for the merged path. To reduce the `2*n!-1` nodes of a single optimal tree
to `poly(n)` nodes, some node must merge at least
`n! / poly(n)` histories, which naively costs `Omega(n log n)` bits. This
exceeds the `o(n)` budget unless the merged histories have linear-extension
counts within a factor `2^{o(n)}` of each other.

**[deduction]** Therefore a polynomial-size exact certificate would require
an optimal strategy whose reachable histories are, in aggregate,
exponentially close in `e(P)` and can be merged with sublinear forgetting.
No such strategy or compression is currently known. The merge-table
violations show that even two-chain states can be a factor of 2 apart.

### 5.3 State-count barrier

**[literature]** The winning-frontier barrier proves that the number of
nonisomorphic partial orders that are winning states at one depth, including
at budget `S(n)`, is `2^{(1/4-o(1)) n log^2 n}`. Full enumeration of
winning states is therefore impossible even at the exact optimum.

**[deduction]** This does not by itself rule out a polynomial-size strategy
skeleton: an optimal strategy may visit only a polynomial subset of all
winning states. But it does show that any compression must avoid enumerating
the winning frontier, and it places the burden on a constructive
polynomial-state family.

### 5.4 No small-n decisive test for exact optimality

**[deduction]** Ford–Johnson is exact for all `n <= 22` (and `n=20,21`
are information-tight). `S(23)` is open. Consequently there is currently no
small `n` for which an exact optimal strategy provably differs from
Ford–Johnson. Any experimental test of exact-optimal certificates on small
`n` tests Ford–Johnson, which is already polynomially certifiable.

## 6. Implications for `SORT-BOUND-U` NP membership

**[open]** `SORT-BOUND-U in NP` remains open. A positive resolution would
require an all-n theorem that some optimal (or, more generally, height-`S(n)`)
sorter has a polynomial-size framed DAG.

**[deduction]** The known polynomial certificates (bubble, merge sort,
Ford–Johnson) are sound and checkable, but:
- bubble and merge sort are not exact for large `n`;
- Ford–Johnson is exact in all resolved cases but has `Theta(n)` surplus and
  is not exact asymptotically (Sergeev).

**[deduction]** The entropy identity implies that an exact certificate must
have `o(n)` total forgetting. The single-policy optimal DAG has no repeated
states for `n <= 6`, and its quotient counts grow faster than the
polynomial range tested. This is evidence against a simple
join-compression route, but it is not a proof.

**[deduction]** If a bounded-merge-width (in the sense of
polynomial-state, sublinear-forgetting) exact certificate were found, then
`SORT-BOUND-U in NP` would follow. If one can prove that every exact optimal
strategy requires superpolynomially many distinct slot-poset states, then
framed DAGs would not settle NP membership, but that would still not imply
`SORT-BOUND-U notin NP` (another certificate form might exist).

**[open]** No separation claim is made: this report does not claim
`SORT-BOUND-U notin P`, `notin NP`, or `notin PSPACE`. The established upper
bound `SORT-BOUND-U in PSPACE` stands.

## 7. Next decisive obstacles

1. **First non-Ford–Johnson exact case.** Determine the smallest `n` with
   `S(n) < F_FJ(n)`, or prove Ford–Johnson optimal on a larger range.
   Without this, exact certificates cannot be tested against Ford–Johnson.
2. **Polynomial-state exact skeleton.** Construct a family of slot posets
   with `poly(n)` states and an explicit strategy achieving
   `log2(n!) + o(n)` comparisons, then verify it with
   `check_framed_strategy.py` for moderate `n`.
3. **Sublinear-forgetting joins.** Prove or refute that exact optimal play
   can be arranged so all joins have total forgetting `O(n/(log n)^c)`, and
   that the number of distinct slot-poset states is polynomial.
4. **Quotient growth.** Extend the quotient node counts in §4.4 to
   `n=7` or `n=8` (requires better minimax/state compression) to see
   whether growth is polynomial, exponential in `n`, or factorial.
