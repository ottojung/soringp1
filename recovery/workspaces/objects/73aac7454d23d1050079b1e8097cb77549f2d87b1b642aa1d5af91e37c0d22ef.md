## 1. B6: recurrence closure (certifiable fragment)

### 1.1 Exact recurrence target

TR20-096 Theorem 3 is reported to use the excess
\[
s(n) := S(n)-\log_2(n!)
\]
and to obtain an asymptotic recurrence/solution of the form
\[
s(n) \le s(n/2) + O\!\left(\frac{n}{\log^{1/5} n}\right), \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
S(n)=\log_2(n!)+O\!\left(\frac{n}{\log^{1/5} n}\right).
\]
`[uncertain extraction; literature target TR20-096 Thm 3, p.29]`

The nonconstructive part is not the algebra of closing such a recurrence; it is that the one-step `O(...)` term, its domain/threshold matching, and the finite basis are not given as an explicit all-`n>=n0` inequality. `[deduction]`

### 1.2 Explicit computable closure fragment (conditional)

Fix `p>0` and suppose finite data `(n0, A, B)` are supplied with the **one-step effective inequality**
\[
\mathcal H(n0,A,B,p):\qquad \forall n\ge n0,\quad s(n) \le s(\lfloor n/2\rfloor) + A\frac{n}{(\log_2 n)^p} + B,
\]
using `log_2` for concreteness and integer halving; require `n0\ge 4` so denominators are positive and monotone on the relevant tail. (A variant with `ceil(n/2)` and/or pure `A n/(\log_2 n)^p` is handled identically by adjusting `n0,B`.) `[deduction]`

Define the computable schedule/depth
\[
J(n) := \max\{j\in\mathbb N : \lfloor n/2^j\rfloor \ge n0\},\qquad n_J:=\lfloor n/2^J\rfloor\in\{n0,\ldots,2n0-1\}.
\]
Iterating `H(n0,A,B,p)` gives the explicit finite bound
\[
\boxed{\quad
s(n) \le s(n_J) + A\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\frac{\lfloor n/2^j\rfloor}{(\log_2 \lfloor n/2^j\rfloor)^p} + B\,J,
\qquad n\ge n0.
\quad}
\]
This is computable from `(n,A,B,p,n0)` and a finite table/bound for `s(m)` on `m<2n0`. `[deduction]`

A looser closed majorant (certifiable without the sum's denominator schedule detail beyond a cutoff) is obtained by fixing `n1\ge 2n0` and splitting the dyadic terms at the first index where `\log_2(n/2^j)\ge \frac12\log_2 n`: let `K=\lfloor \log_2(n/n1')\rfloor` with `n1'` chosen so that all summation denominators in the main tail are `\ge (\frac12\log_2 n)^p` for `n\ge n1`. Then
\[
\sum_{j=0}^{K-1}\frac{n/2^j}{(\log_2(n/2^j))^p}
\le \frac{2^p}{(\log_2 n)^p}\sum_{j\ge 0}\frac{n}{2^j}
= \frac{2^{p+1}n}{(\log_2 n)^p},
\]
and the finite prefix `j\ge K` plus the basis is absorbed into a computable `C_0(n0,n1,A,B,p)` depending only on the finite table below `n1`. Therefore, **if** `H(n0,A,B,p)` is discharged, one gets an explicit computable
\[
\forall n\ge n1,\qquad S(n)\le \log_2(n!) + C\frac{n}{(\log_2 n)^p}+D,
\]
with `C=2^{p+1}A` after the chosen cutoff and `D` from the finite basis/prefix. This is a **certifiable recurrence-closure fragment**, not a derivation of `H`. `[deduction]`

### 1.3 Exact nonconstructive inequality / finite data to discharge B6

To turn TR20-096 Thm 3's asymptotic closure into an effective bound, the required finite data are:

| Finite datum | What it must certify | Why needed |
|---|---|---|
| `n0` | Explicit threshold for the one-step schedule in Thm 3. | TR20-096 uses asymptotic choices (`n0=n/\log n`, `m\approx\sqrt{n}/\log n` in the extracted analyses); a finite proof needs an integer schedule valid for all `n>=n0`. `[uncertain extraction; literature target TR20-096]` |
| `A,B,p` (or explicit `g(n)`) | `s(n)-s(\lfloor n/2\rfloor)\le g(n)` for all `n>=n0`. | Replaces the single asymptotic `O(n/\log^{1/5}n)` one-step bound. `[deduction]` |
| Integer schedule `m(n), L_t, n0^{chain}(n),` group count | Every Corollary 2 / Thm 2 hypothesis used by the one step holds. | Prevents hidden threshold-matching failures (B5/B2 interface). `[deduction]` |
| Finite basis `S_b` | Sound upper bounds/exact values for `s(m)` or `S(m)` for all `m<n1` (or all `m<2n0`). | Closes the recurrence. For small `n`, known exact/literature values or a verified constructive sort bound can be used if model-faithful. `[deduction]` |

**Exact missing inequality (B6 core):** an explicit, all-`n>=n0` version of
\[
S(n)-\log_2(n!) \le S(\lfloor n/2\rfloor)-\log_2(\lfloor n/2\rfloor !) + A\frac{n}{(\log_2 n)^{1/5}} + B,
\]
obtained from the TR20-096 Thm 3 group-insertion accounting with every hidden constant, schedule approximation, last-group/binary-insertion term, and Stirling/log rounding made explicit. `[deduction; literature target TR20-096 Thm 3]`

## 2. B3: Lemma 2 finite-loss step (specific)

### 2.1 Exact inequality shape requiring finite losses

Local primary-source analyses report TR20-096 Lemma 2 (pp.9–13) as a bridge from a comparison strategy/LP-feasible data to a `P_m` lower bound of the form
\[
P_m\!\left(r + y_i + \frac{(r+2)\phi 2^\phi}{m}\right)
\;\ge\; v_i 2^r
\;-\; \underbrace{\frac{R}{\varepsilon}m 2^{\phi+2}}_{\text{container/capacity loss}}
\;-\; \underbrace{R 2^r \exp\!\left(-\frac{\varepsilon}{2R}\left(\frac{\phi}{w(d+1)(h+1)}-2\right)\right)}_{\text{strategy/interpolation tail loss}},
\]
with a side condition reported as
\[
 m \le \min\{u_{\min},\varepsilon/2\}2^{r-\phi-1},\qquad R=\|v-u\|,
\]
and algorithmic ingredients including a reduced strategy tree, containers, and rounding/induction on ranks. `[uncertain extraction; literature target TR20-096 Lemma 2, (17),(18)]`

TR20-096's abstract-level claim (group insertion gives `log_2(n!)+o(n)`) does not, by itself, make the finite losses in this inequality explicit. `[literature/deduction]`

### 2.2 Specific finite-loss data that must be discharged

The exact nonconstructive/effectivity gaps for this Lemma 2 step are:

1. **Rounding direction/lattice loss.** Real/non-integer lengths/ranks are rounded in some direction in the reported proof. Finite data needed: an explicit integer-rounding lemma giving an additive/multiplicative error term (or a proof that rounding preserves the final inequality after parameter substitution) for all states used by the schedule. `[uncertain extraction; deduction]`
2. **Container capacity/counting loss.** The reported middle term comes from a rough container bound (e.g. `c_{dl+i} <= \phi 2^\phi` in local analyses). Finite data needed: an explicit upper bound on container populations/capacities in the final normalized inequality, with no unnamed `O(1)`/`rough` constant. `[uncertain extraction; deduction]`
3. **Information/positivity side condition.** The proof is reported to require conditions such as `n+2m < 2^(r+2)` / positivity of reduced-tree bookkeeping. Finite data needed: an explicit verified domain where every use of Lemma 2 satisfies these conditions for the chosen integer schedule. `[uncertain extraction; deduction]`
4. **Parameter specialization.** `r,m,\phi,\varepsilon,R,w,d,h,u,v,y_i` are later chosen by `a`-dependent schedules (Lemma 4/Thm 2 in the reported chain). Finite data needed: a closed inequality after substitution, or certified finite/analytic coverage of all `(a,m,s)` cases used. `[deduction]`

A discharged Lemma 2 finite-loss certificate fragment would therefore be:
\[
\forall (r,m,\phi,\ldots)\in\mathcal D_{\mathrm{eff}}:\quad
P_m(A(r,m,\phi,\ldots)) \ge B(r,m,\phi,\ldots)-\Delta_{\mathrm{round}}-\Delta_{\mathrm{cont}}-\Delta_{\mathrm{tail}},
\]
with computable `A,B,\Delta_{\mathrm{round}},\Delta_{\mathrm{cont}},\Delta_{\mathrm{tail}}`, an explicit computable domain `\mathcal D_{\mathrm{eff}}`, and checked side conditions. Producing `\mathcal D_{\mathrm{eff}}` and the three `\Delta` terms for TR20-096's actual schedule is open in this audit. `[open]`

## 3. Consequence for the unary threshold window (no separation claim)

If the finite data in §1.3 and §2.2 were supplied, they would yield a computable
\[
U_{\mathrm{eff}}(n)=\lceil \log_2(n!)\rceil + f(n),\qquad S(n)\le U_{\mathrm{eff}}(n)\ (n\ge n1),
\]
narrowing the `SORT-BOUND-U` middle window: `k < \lceil\log_2(n!)\rceil` is NO and `k >= U_{\mathrm{eff}}(n)` is YES for large enough certified `n`. This would **not** prove `SORT-BOUND-U \in NP/P`, would **not** prove nonmembership, and would **not** imply `P != PSPACE` or `NP != PSPACE`. `[deduction; AGENTS/problem.md constraints]`
