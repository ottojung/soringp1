## Question (all `n`, falsifiable)

Is there a sound, complete, polynomial-size framed permutation certificate /
polynomial-state joinable framed grammar for a height-`S(n)` (or
height `log₂(n!) + o(n)` Sergeev-compatible) optimal/near-optimal sorting strategy for
all `n`?

Focus: Sergeev group insertion stores pending keys in coupled interval containers. Can
this be encoded as polynomial global framed states with sound joins and verified
transitions, or is there a specific incompatibility/barrier for that architecture?

Distinguish existence at larger heights (merge sort, Ford–Johnson). No complexity
separations.

## Fixed model and established baseline

- `[definition][literature]` `S(n)` is the minimum worst-case number of adaptive
  pairwise comparisons to sort `n` distinct labelled keys; `SORT-BOUND-U = {1^n#1^k : S(n) <= k}`
  (`docs/problem.md`).
- `[definition][paper, repo]` A framed permutation certificate is an acyclic DAG on
  `n` slots with comparison-outcome arcs carrying slot permutations; root guarantee
  empty; arc updates take transitive closure and apply permutation; join intersects
  guaranteed relations of feasible incoming arcs; terminal guarantee is total; path
  length `<= k`. This is sound and locally checkable in time polynomial in explicit
  graph size and `n` (`docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` §1,
  `scripts/check_framed_strategy.py`).
- `[literature]` Sergeev (ECCC TR20-096, 2020, Thm. 3) gives a group-insertion sorting
  algorithm with `S(n) = log₂(n!) + O(n/(log n)^(1/5))`; asymptotic, no effective
  finite constants supplied here (`docs/research-sergeev-asymptotic.md`).
- `[paper construction, repo; audit pending]` Adaptive merge sort has an all-`n`
  polynomial framed DAG (`O(n²)` comparison states, `O(n log n)` path length for
  balanced merges) but its merge architecture has comparison deficit
  `n log₂ n - n + 1 - log₂(n!) = (log₂ e - 1)n + O(log n)` for powers of two, so it
  is not Sergeev-compatible/optimal at large heights
  (`docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` §2).
- `[paper construction, repo; audit pending]` Rank-envelope joins give a plausible
  all-`n` `O(n²)`-query framed certificate for the standard Ford–Johnson upper bound
  `F_FJ(n)=∑_{j=1}^n ⌈log₂(3j/4)⌉`, not equal to `S(n)` for all `n`
  (`docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` §3).
- `[open]` No all-`n` polynomial-size framed certificate at exact height `S(n)` is
  known; NP/coNP membership of `SORT-BOUND-U` remains open
  (`docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md`, `docs/research-plan.md`).

## Analysis: Sergeev-compatible framed-state encoding

### Why merge/FJ do not settle the question

`[deduction]` Existence at larger heights is established only for architectures whose
global control state is naturally compressible by the framed invariant:

- Merge sort: a merge state is `(a,b)` (remaining lengths of two ordered queues) plus
  an output-prefix frame; comparisons move deterministically in that product state and
  completed merges join by the exact invariant "ordered prefix + two ordered remaining
  queues" (`docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` §2).
- Rank-envelope insertion (FJ-style, as sketched): pending elements can be given sound
  relaxed per-element rank windows relative to a framed chain; insertions of other
  elements shift windows uniformly enough that leaves can join on a common weak
  invariant (`docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` §3).

These are **insufficient** for Sergeev compatibility because their comparison counts
are asymptotically above `log₂(n!) + o(n)` (merge deficit is linear; FJ bound differs
from `S(n)` in general). Therefore a positive answer requires a different
near-information-optimal architecture, not merely better sharing of merge/FJ DAGs.

### Stated obstruction: coupled interval containers

`[literature/deduction from docs]` The frontier note states: Sergeev's group-insertion
construction gives `S(n) <= log₂(n!) + O(n/(log n)^(1/5))` but stores pending keys in
**coupled interval containers**; short local insertion routines do not automatically
yield polynomial global control states (`docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md`
§5.2). The open task is to give a polynomial-state rank-envelope or nested-subroutine
grammar with sound joins and `o(n)` surplus, or falsify such a proposed grammar.

### Barrier B1 (specific, class-scoped): incompatibility of independent per-key
rank-envelope framed grammars with faithful coupled-container states

**Claim (paper-level barrier, `[deduction]`).** Consider a framed grammar class whose
global state, at join/decision points relevant to pending elements, is represented by:
(i) an explicit slot/permutation frame inducing a reference chain or fixed partition of
slots, and (ii) for each pending key an independent certified rank/value interval (or
other per-key summary) relative to that frame, with joins formed by per-key weakening or
intersection of those summaries without recording inter-key coupling constraints.

Such a grammar is **not generally adequate** as a faithful, sound, complete, and
height-preserving encoding of a coupled-interval-container architecture:

1. **Faithfulness/coupling:** "Coupled interval containers" entail joint feasibility
   constraints: the admissible placement/rank of one pending key may depend on the
   placement/ranks or container occupancy of others (a coupled feasible region in the
   product of possible positions), not a Cartesian product of independent intervals.
2. **Unsound forgetting:** projecting a coupled region to independent per-key intervals
   is a relaxation (Cartesian overapproximation). It can certify comparisons/transitions
   as feasible that are infeasible under the true coupled state, violating soundness
   unless the architecture never relies on the discarded coupling.
3. **Incompleteness / height inflation:** to restore soundness without coupling, the
   grammar must either (a) forbid joins and split histories whenever coupling differs,
   or (b) use only invariants strong enough to be valid after arbitrary feasible joins.
   Option (a) destroys polynomial-state joinability by distinguishing coupled histories;
   option (b) weakens information and, for a near-information-optimal
   (`log₂(n!) + o(n)`) strategy, risks increasing comparison cost unless a stronger
   global invariant is exhibited.
4. **Join condition:** a sound framed join may intersect only guarantees justified on
   every feasible incoming arc (`docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` §1). If
   incoming histories induce different coupling relations not implied by a common
   retained invariant, their safe intersection cannot reintroduce the coupling; thus the
   join either loses necessary structure (unsound/incomplete for the original
   continuation) or must keep the coupling-distinguishing state.

**Consequence.** Any proposed polynomial-state joinable framed grammar for
Sergeev-compatible group insertion must represent or soundly abstract **inter-key
coupling** across containers, not merely independent rank envelopes. This is a specific
incompatibility for the independent-rank-envelope class, not a proof that no polynomial
global encoding exists.

### Barrier B2 (architecture-level obstruction, partially open): global control/state
explosion risk for coupled-container near-optimal strategies

**Observation (`[deduction][open]`).**

- `[deduction]` A global framed state for an adaptive strategy must be sufficient,
  together with verified transitions, to determine/justify all future feasible
  comparisons and terminal orders on every feasible path (soundness/completeness).
- `[literature]` Sergeev achieves only `o(n)` total comparison surplus over
  `log₂(n!)`, hence near-maximal average information per comparison in aggregate
  (`docs/research-sergeev-asymptotic.md`); local/container operations are therefore
  tightly constrained.
- `[open][deduction]` Coupled containers introduce a joint control object (grouping,
  container boundaries/occupancies, and correlations created by prior comparisons). To
  obtain polynomial global states with sound joins, one would need a polynomial-size
  invariant closed under the algorithm's comparisons and all required joins while
  preserving enough coupling to maintain the `o(n)` surplus. No such invariant/grammar
  is specified in the audited repo sources.
- `[deduction]` If the algorithm requires distinguishing superpolynomially many coupled
  configurations that are not equivalent under any sound join-closed framed invariant,
  a faithful polynomial-state joinable framed grammar for that exact architecture is
  impossible (state explosion / dependence on exponentially many configurations). This
  is a **conditional** barrier schema: establishing it rigorously requires either (a) an
  explicit all-`n` polynomial coupled-container grammar, or (b) a proof that every
  sound join-closed invariant for Sergeev's coupled-container transitions induces a
  superpolynomial quotient/state set. Neither (a) nor (b) is currently established here.

### Relation to known general barriers

- `[literature][repo]` The winning-frontier barrier gives
  `2^{(1/4-o(1)) n log² n}` nonisomorphic winning states at one depth even at budget
  `S(n)` (`docs/research-winning-frontier-barrier.md`), so any polynomial framed
  certificate at optimal/near-optimal height must avoid raw enumeration and exploit a
  stronger global join invariant. This constrains but does not by itself refute a
  Sergeev-compatible framed grammar.
- `[deduction]` Existence of polynomial framed certificates at larger heights
  (merge/FJ, subject to audits noted above) shows the obstruction is
  **height/architecture-specific**, not a universal failure of framed DAGs.

## Conclusion (falsifiable)

- **Larger heights:** polynomial-size framed permutation certificates/grammars are
  plausible/constructed in repo notes for merge sort and (sketch-level) Ford–Johnson
  upper bounds, but these are not `S(n)`-optimal and not Sergeev-compatible.
- **Sergeev-compatible / optimal height:** no all-`n` polynomial global framed state,
  sound-join, verified-transition encoding is known.
- **Specific barrier established here:** independent per-key rank-envelope framed
  grammars (Cartesian per-element summaries without inter-key coupling) are incompatible
  with faithful representation of coupled interval-container states (Barrier B1);
  joining/forgetting coupling is unsound unless unused, while preserving distinctions
  without a stronger invariant obstructs polynomial-state joinability or risks height
  inflation.
- **Remaining open obstruction:** prove a concrete polynomial coupled-container framed
  grammar with sound joins and `o(n)` surplus, or prove a rigorous all-`n`
  state-explosion/non-joinability theorem for the actual Sergeev group-insertion
  transition/join structure (Barrier B2 schema). This report does not claim either.

## Falsifiers

1. **Positive falsifier (encoding exists):** an explicit all-`n` polynomial-size framed
   permutation certificate / polynomial-state joinable framed grammar for Sergeev's (or
   any) `log₂(n!) + o(n)`-height strategy, with a proof sketch and locally checkable
   invariants/transitions (and, if claimed, exact `S(n)` height).
2. **Negative falsifier (rigorous barrier):** a theorem showing that for all sufficiently
   large `n` (or an infinite family), every sound, complete, join-closed framed grammar
   realizing Sergeev-compatible coupled-container group insertion requires
   superpolynomially many global states/nodes, or cannot satisfy verified sound joins
   without superpolynomial splitting / `ω(n)` comparison surplus.
