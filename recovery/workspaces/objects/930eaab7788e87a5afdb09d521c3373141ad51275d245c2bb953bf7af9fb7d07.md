# Poly-time root adversary invariant front (`polytime-root-adversary-invariant-n12`)

**Front (from prompt).** Propose and rigorously test a polynomial-time computable (in `n`, on standard poset/transcript representation) predicate/invariant `J(P,d)` that is initializable at antichain root `A_n` and admits an adversary reply rule maintainable under every sorter comparison, such that `J` explains or forces a comparison deficit beyond the information bound in the `n=12` regime (`C(12)=29`, `S(12)=30`) or on an explicit infinite family. Candidates may use comparability-graph/width/degree/chain-antichain summaries but must avoid `#P`-hard extension counting as the check.

**Falsifier criterion.** For each proposed `J`, exhibit a concrete sorter counterstrategy (even schematic/small-`n`) defeating maintenance before any super-information deficit is forced, or show `J` not initializable / not poly-time checkable.

**Scope note.** This is a research report in this worktree only. All claims are labelled **definition**, **literature**, **deduction**, **conjecture**, or **experiment** per `AGENTS.md`. No Lean/kernel claims are made. A comparison lower bound for `S(n)` is not a complexity lower bound for computing `S(n)` (**literature/model fidelity**, `docs/problem.md`).

## 0. Model, representation, and baseline facts

- **Definition (model).** `P=(X,<)` is the current strict partial order on `n` labelled keys after a transcript of comparisons; root `A_n` is the antichain. `F(P)` is the minimax remaining worst-case comparisons to sort all linear extensions of `P`. `S(n)=F(A_n)`. `C(n)=ceil(log2 n!)` is the global information bound and `L0(P)=ceil(log2 e(P))` the local one (**literature/definition**, `docs/problem.md`, `docs/research-one-ply-obstruction.md`).
- **Definition (standard representation).** Transcript = list of compared pairs with outcomes; poset state = transitive closure / successor bitmasks or adjacency of the comparability DAG (size `O(n^2)` bits/words). All predicates below are required to run in time polynomial in this representation and in `n`.
- **Literature (n=12 regime).** `C(12)=29`, `S(12)=30` (Wells; see `docs/literature.md`, `docs/research-plan.md`). No compact published structural certificate localizes the obstruction; exhaustive/pruned search certifies it (`docs/research-n12-nontightness.md` §4, §8).
- **Literature/deduction (relevant barriers).** `e(P)` counting is `#P`-complete in general, so forbidden as the check (`docs/research-one-ply-obstruction.md` §4). Scalar monotone functions of `e(P)`/slack cannot explain gaps: sortability is not monotone in `e` and local non-tightness occurs at `n=5` while global tightness holds (`docs/research-n12-nontightness.md` H1–H3, §8). Efficiency pruning is equivalent to node-level leaf counting, not a new scalar invariant (`docs/research-n12-nontightness.md` §4). Extension monotonicity and merge-factor/rounding bounds are valid but do not yield a compact n=12 explanation (`docs/research-structural-lower-bounds.md`).

**Goal formalized (deduction).** A successful `J(P,d)` for this front would need, for root state with remaining budget `d=C(n)` (or the relevant budget), an adversary strategy `σ` such that: (i) `J(A_n,d)` holds (initializable), (ii) for any incomparable query `q` chosen by sorter, some answer `a∈{<,>}` gives `J(P+a,d-1)` (maintainable), and (iii) `J(P,0) => P` not total / contradiction with sorting completed, or more generally `J` implies a residual comparison demand exceeding the information accounting so that height `C(n)` is impossible. Any such `J` must be checkable in `poly(n)` without `e(P)`.

## 1. Candidate J1 — Width/antichain invariant (Dilworth)

**Definition.** Let `w(P)` be the width (size of a maximum antichain). Define
`J1(P,d) := [ w(P) >= g(n,d) ]` for some threshold function `g` to be chosen (candidates: constant `g=2`, or `g` tied to remaining budget, e.g. `w(P) > 1` while unsorted).

**Poly-time checkability (literature/deduction).** By Dilworth's theorem, `w(P)` equals the minimum number of chains in a chain cover of `P`; for finite posets this equals the size of a maximum matching in the associated bipartite comparability graph, computable in polynomial time (e.g. Hopcroft–Karp / network flow on `2n` vertices). Thus `w(P)` and `J1` are poly-time checkable on the standard `O(n^2)` representation. **Satisfied.**

**Initializability (deduction).** `w(A_n)=n`, so `J1(A_n,d)` holds for any `g(n,d) <= n`. **Satisfied** for natural thresholds.

**Adversary maintenance attempt.** For a comparison `(x,y)`:
- if `x,y` comparable, state unchanged;
- if incomparable, adversary answers to try to preserve a large antichain. Since a single comparability removes at most one element from any particular antichain containing both, and often none from some maximum antichain not containing the pair, adversary can answer to keep `w(P)` nonincreasing slowly. In fact `w(P)` drops by at most 1 per comparison in some cases but can stay large.

**Soundness toward n=12 deficit (analysis).** `J1` with `g>1` only says the order is not total; with larger `g` it is a structural summary, but width alone does not encode extension-count or split-balance information. Total-order completion requires reducing width to 1 eventually, but that is compatible with many total budgets including `C(n)` when tight.

**Falsification (concrete sorter counterstrategy / failure to force deficit).** 
- **Small-n schematic:** at `n=5`, `w(A_5)=5` initializable; an optimal sorter achieves `S(5)=7=C(5)` with no deficit (**literature**, `docs/research-plan.md`). Any `J1` maintained down to near completion cannot force `>C(5)` comparisons, because a completing strategy of height 7 exists. Thus `J1` does not force a super-information deficit in a regime where none exists, and provides no mechanism distinguishing tight from non-tight cases.
- **n=12 regime:** to explain/force `S(12)>29`, `J1` would need a threshold/maintenance argument implying 30 comparisons from width/comparability structure alone. But width is insensitive to the parity/rounding and strategic split obstructions documented for n=12: many width profiles are compatible with both feasible and infeasible 29-comparison searches in principle, and no known theorem derives the Wells gap from `w(P)` (**deduction from literature gap**, `docs/research-n12-nontightness.md`). A sorter can target comparisons that refine chain structure efficiently (consistent with merge-insertion-like progress on chain decompositions) without any width-based reply forcing an extra comparison beyond 29 except by reproducing the full search—i.e. `J1` is not a sufficient maintained invariant for the deficit. 
- **Maintenance not the blocker, implication is:** adversary can often maintain `w(P)>=2` until late, but this only restates "not sorted"; it never yields the +1 deficit at n=12. Therefore J1 **fails the front goal** (no forced/explained super-information deficit). **Claim type: deduction + literature.**

## 2. Candidate J2 — Incomparable-pair / comparability-degree invariant

**Definition.** Let `m(P)` be the number of incomparable unordered pairs, `c(P)=\binom{n}{2}-m(P)` comparabilities, and `d_{inc}(v)` the incomparability degree. Candidate predicates:
- `J2a(P,d): m(P) >= h(n,d)` (enough incomparabilities remain),
- `J2b(P,d): \Delta_{inc}(P) >= h'(n,d)` or `\delta_{inc}(P) >= 1` while unsorted.

**Poly-time checkability (deduction).** From transitive closure/adjacency, `m(P), c(P)`, all degrees, min/max are computable in `O(n^2)` or `O(n^\omega)` trivially poly-time. **Satisfied.** Initializable at root: `m(A_n)=\binom{n}{2}`.

**Maintenance and adversary reply (deduction).** A comparison on an incomparable pair adds at least that comparability and possibly implied ones by transitivity, so `m(P)` decreases monotonically regardless of adversary answer direction except through which transitive edges appear; adversary chooses direction to minimize transitive closure growth (keep `m(P)` large). This is a standard "adversary delays order" heuristic and maintainable in form.

**Falsification.**
- **No super-information forcing:** `m(P)` is a coarse progress measure. For every comparison sort, `m(P)` reaches 0 only at a total order after some number of comparisons, but `J2` supplies no lower bound stronger than "some pairs remain" unless tied to a theorem relating `m(P)` to required height beyond `L0(P)`. No such theorem is known; in particular merge-insertion achieves near-information bounds in many cases despite structured `m(P)` decay (**literature**, Ford–Johnson/Knuth context in `docs/literature.md`).
- **Concrete/schematic counterstrategy:** sorter always compares an incomparable pair (any nontrivial strategy does). Adversary answers to avoid large forced chains when possible, yet for `n=5` the sorter still finishes in 7 comparisons total. For `n=12`, even maintaining high `m(P)` early does not imply impossibility of a 29-height tree—impossibility is a global strategic property not captured by pair count (`docs/research-n12-nontightness.md` §8: obstruction strategic/global, not scalar). 
- **Quantitative gap:** to force deficit at root one would need e.g. `m(P)` high => `e(P)` small in a way contradicting budget, but `m(P)` does not determine `e(P)` (many posets share pair counts with different extension counts). Hence J2 **cannot be a sound sufficient invariant for the n=12 deficit** without reintroducing extension information. **Claim type: deduction.**

## 3. Candidate J3 — Chain/height/Greene-type summary invariant

**Definition.** Let `h(P)` be the height (size of a maximum chain, poly-time by longest path in DAG of closure) and `w(P)` width. Candidate `J3(P,d):= [h(P) <= r_1(n,d) \land w(P) >= r_2(n,d)]` or using chain-antichain decompositions (Mirsky/Dilworth). Greene–Kleitman generalizes to `k`-families but full profiles can be expensive; we restrict to poly-time summaries: `h(P), w(P)`, and maybe sizes of a fixed decomposition.

**Poly-time checkability (literature/deduction).** `h(P)` is longest path in a transitive DAG, computable in `O(n^2)`. `w(P)` poly-time as in J1. **Satisfied** for these summaries.

**Initializability.** `A_n`: `h=1`, `w=n`. **Satisfied** for reasonable `r_1>=1, r_2<=n`.

**Falsification.**
- **Sorter counterstrategy (maintenance defeat for strong forms):** if `J3` requires `h(P)` to stay small (few forced chain relations), sorter can compare elements along emerging chains to increase `h(P)` (build total order on a subset) — adversary cannot avoid creating some comparabilities and transitivity can increase chain length regardless of answer choice when comparing endpoints appropriately? More carefully: on comparing incomparable `x,y`, adversary chooses `x<y` or `y<x`; sorter can pick pairs so that either answer extends some existing chain (e.g. compare chain maximum with an element above it in some linear extension consistent with current knowledge is restricted, but sorter chooses the pair: comparing an element with the top of a current chain yields `h` increase in at least one direction? no, adversary picks direction). Adversary can often answer to keep chains short by placing elements "sideways". So maintenance against sorter may hold for "keep `h` small, `w` large" in some forms.
- **But failure to force deficit:** even if adversary maintains a "flat" poset (large width, small height) for some rounds, this does not imply `F(P)>L0(P)$ or exceed budget at n=12. The known non-monotonic/strategic nature of `F` means shape summaries `(h,w)` are insufficient predictors (cf. H1-H3 failures for other scalars; `docs/research-n12-nontightness.md`). A concrete small-n counterexample to *deficit forcing*: for `n=4`, width/height profiles during optimal sorting still allow completion in `S(4)=5=C(4)`. For n=12, no derivation of +1 from `(h,w)` exists in literature; thus J3 **does not explain or force** the required deficit. **Claim type: deduction/literature.**

## 4. Candidate J4 — "Poly-time one-ply proxy" without `e(P)` (comparability-balance proxy)

**Motivation (deduction from docs).** `docs/research-one-ply-obstruction.md` gives `L1(P)` with `L0 <= L1 <= F` and a strict witness, but computing `L1/L0` uses `e(P)` or extension enumeration counts (`#P`-hard in general / exponential) — explicitly forbidden. A natural poly-time proxy replaces child extension balance by a computable balance score.

**Definition (proxy attempt).** For incomparable pair `q=(i,j)`, let `t_q(P)$ be the number of relations implied by answer `i<j` minus by `j<i` via transitive closure (or use sizes of forced comparabilities / degree changes). Define
`B(P)=\min_{q\in Inc(P)} \max(\phi(P,q,<), \phi(P,q,>))` for some poly-time `\phi` measuring "progress/balance", and `J4(P,d):= [B(P) >= \beta(d)]`.

**Poly-time checkability.** For each candidate pair (at most `\binom{n}{2}`) compute two tentative closures/updates: `O(n^2)` to `O(n^3)` per pair naively, total poly-time. **Satisfied** if `\phi` is local/transitive-closure based.

**Falsification.**
- **Not equivalent to information/adversarial hardness:** transitive-forcing balance is a poor proxy for `e(P+q)$ balance. A comparison can force many edges yet split extensions evenly, or force few yet split very unevenly; H2/H3 show `F` and even local tightness depend on extension structure not reducible to simple graph counts (`docs/research-n12-nontightness.md`).
- **Sorter counterstrategy defeating maintenance before deficit:** sorter can choose a pair where both directions yield similar `\phi` (a "balanced" proxy move) if one exists, breaking `J4` maintenance for any threshold `\beta` set high enough to imply hardness. Because `\phi$ ranges over poly-time graph quantities, there is no guarantee every state admits an adversarially imbalanced proxy answer except in trivial cases; maintaining high imbalance against all sorter choices would itself be a strong structural claim requiring proof.
- **Initializable but not sound for n=12:** at `A_n` symmetry gives balanced proxies; to get a deficit-relevant `J4` one needs it to become and stay high in a way forcing extra comparisons — but any such forcing would be an unproved theorem. Given known falsifications of scalar/local heuristics, J4 **lacks a soundness argument and is vulnerable to balanced-pair sorter moves**. **Claim type: deduction/conjecture (negative).**

## 5. Cross-cutting analysis against the front requirements

| Requirement | J1 width | J2 incomparabilities | J3 (h,w) summaries | J4 TC-balance proxy |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Poly-time checkable, no `e(P)` | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| Initializable at `A_n` | yes | yes | yes | yes (symmetric) |
| Adversary-maintainable vs every sorter move | often/plausible for weak forms | plausible (delay) | mixed/plausible | **not established; sorter may pick balanced proxy pair** |
| Forces/explains deficit beyond `C(12)=29` at n=12 | **no known implication** | **no** | **no known implication** | **no soundness argument** |
| Falsifier outcome | fails goal (no deficit forcing) | fails goal | fails goal | maintenance/soundness doubtful; no deficit proof |

**Deduction (barrier for this template).** The n=12 gap is a global strategic phenomenon not witnessed by published compact local invariants and not predictable by scalar functions of slack/`e(P)`; poly-time graph summaries (width, degrees, chain counts, TC forcing) are even coarser than `e(P)$-derived scalars in the specific information they carry about extension partitions. Therefore, for the candidates above, either maintenance is easy but implication fails, or implication would require an unproved bridge to the minimax search. This does not prove no such `J` exists, but it indicates the decisive missing ingredient is a poly-time observable that provably controls adversarial split quality over a full strategy, not just local poset shape.

## 6. Evidence and claim ledger

- **Literature:** `S(12)=30>C(12)=29`, exact small values, asymptotics context (`docs/literature.md`, `docs/research-plan.md`); efficiency ≡ info bound, non-monotonicity/structural obstruction discussion (`docs/research-n12-nontightness.md` citing Wells, Peczarski, Stober–Weiß).
- **Literature/definition:** `#P`-hardness caveat for exact extension counting and polynomial-space budgeted composition context (`docs/research-one-ply-obstruction.md`).
- **Deduction:** poly-time computability of width/chain height/degrees/closure updates; initializability at `A_n`; that weak invariants (non-total/order-progress summaries) do not force super-information deficit; that proxy balance is not sound for extension balance.
- **Experiment (reported from repo docs, not rerun here):** smallest local/monotonicity witnesses and merge witnesses informing non-scalar obstruction (`docs/research-n12-nontightness.md`, `docs/research-structural-lower-bounds.md`).
- **Conjecture (negative/heuristic):** simple poly-time comparability-graph summaries are insufficient to certify the n=12 deficit without encoding the global minimax search.

## 7. Obstacles

1. **Global strategic gap:** `S(12)>29` is certified by exhaustive search; no compact human-checkable invariant is known (`docs/research-n12-nontightness.md` §9).
2. **Forbidden strong oracle:** `e(P)$ gives the relevant split information but is `#P`-hard; admissible `J` must avoid it, creating a large expressiveness gap.
3. **Soundness vs maintainability tension:** invariants easy for an adversary to maintain tend to be too weak to imply a comparison deficit; invariants strong enough to imply deficit are hard to maintain/check in poly-time against all sorter moves.
4. **Non-monotonicity:** local "progress" measures misorder true difficulty (Peczarski example, n=6 witnesses), undermining greedy/proxy designs.

## 8. Next decisive question

Can one define a **poly-time computable potential** `\Phi(P,d)\in \mathbb{Q}` (or small vector) derived only from comparability-graph/chain-decomposition data such that (a) `\Phi(A_{12},29)$ is below a completion threshold, (b) for every sorter comparison there is an adversary reply with `\Phi(P',d-1) \le \Phi(P,d)+\delta` in a controlled way, and (c) `\Phi$ at depth 29 still certifies at least two unresolved outcomes? A decisive falsifier would be either (i) a formal proof that any such `\Phi` satisfying (a),(b) collapses to a known-scalar case and cannot certify (c) on n=12, or (ii) an explicit constructed `\Phi` validated against the canonical Wells/Stober–Weiß obstruction states (or a congruence-quotiented replay) showing maintenance on that search frontier. This targets the core soundness-maintainability gap with a checkable artifact rather than another informal scalar.
