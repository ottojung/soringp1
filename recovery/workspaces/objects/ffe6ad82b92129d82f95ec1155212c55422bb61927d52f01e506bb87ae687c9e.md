# Canonical labeling in the symmetry-quotient evaluator (scratch)

**Question.** Can replacing the quotient DP's exact poset canonicalizer with a known algorithm improve its all-state time bound? This note is scratch research, not an integrated result.

## Input and distinctions

Input is a labelled strict partial order on vertex set `[n]={1,...,n}`, encoded by its `n×n` Boolean relation matrix (or equivalently its transitive-reduction adjacency matrix plus a convention for recovering the order). Input length is Θ(n²) bits. A canonical labeling returns a permutation/relabeled encoding invariant under isomorphism; it is stronger than graph-isomorphism decision, which only answers whether two given encodings are isomorphic. Isomorphism here means arbitrary bijections preserving the order relation. A poset may be represented as a directed graph, or as its undirected comparability graph only with care: comparability graphs do not in general uniquely encode the orientation/order. The safe reduction is the directed relation graph, with colors/directions preserved.

## Literature bounds and obstacle

Babai, *Graph Isomorphism in Quasipolynomial Time*, arXiv:1512.03547 (2015, revised 2016), proves GI decision in `exp((log n)^{O(1)})` time on n-vertex graphs. This is a decision bound, not by itself a canonical-labeling bound. The established general-purpose canonical-labeling bound commonly cited is `exp(O(sqrt(n log n)))` (Luks-style), while practical individualization/refinement methods have no polynomial worst-case guarantee. A direct transfer to posets requires preserving directed/colored structure; standard graph gadgets have polynomial size, so the general quasi-polynomial GI result gives at best quasi-polynomial-in-n isomorphism testing, not automatically a canonical form with the same bound. No source-backed `2^{O(n log n)}` or polynomial worst-case canonical-labeling theorem for these particular reachable posets was identified. This is an obstacle, not a lower bound: the structured family may admit a specialized algorithm, but would need a proof exploiting its structure.

## Effect on quotient-DP complexity

There are `A000112(n)` unlabeled n-element posets, and this count is `2^{Θ(n²)}` (OEIS A000112; see also the asymptotic enumeration of Kleitman–Rothschild, *The number of finite topologies*, 1970, for the dominant layered orders). Thus even ideal constant/poly(n)-time canonicalization cannot make a DP that visits every isomorphism class sub-`2^{Θ(n²)}` merely by speeding up canonicalization. With per-state cost `C(n)`, the direct all-state bound is `A000112(n)·poly(n)·C(n)`: replacing a generic canonicalizer by a `2^{O(n log n)}` or polynomial one only changes the multiplicative per-state factor, not the `2^{Θ(n²)}` state-count exponent. The existing labelled-state recurrence is already `2^{O(n²)}`; quotienting may reduce constants/state counts but no asymptotic exponent improvement follows from canonical labeling alone.

This concerns the all-state recurrence for exact `S(n)`. It does **not** improve or imply an overall exponent improvement for `S(n)` absent a smaller quotient-state count or a different recurrence. The unary threshold budgeted DFS is a separate algorithm.

**Conclusion:** general GI literature supplies quasi-polynomial decision and general canonical-labeling bounds, not the requested specialized poset canonicalizer bound as an automatic consequence. Even granting `poly(n)` canonicalization, the all-poset state count remains `2^{Θ(n²)}`. Further progress must compress/avoid states, not merely canonize each one faster.
