# Exec report: polynomial merge-decomposition certificate frontier (SoringP1 #301)

**Front:** `polynomial merge-decomposition certificate`  
**Question (all n, falsifiable):** Can exact height-`S(n)` optimal sorting strategies be represented by a polynomial-size certificate based on merge/series-parallel or bounded-width decomposition of the comparison DAG/state graph (beyond known `O(n^2)` fixed constructions like merge sort / Ford–Johnson upper bounds), or is there a concrete structural obstruction showing optimal strategies require non-decomposable superpolynomial structure? State implications for `SORT-BOUND-U` in NP.

**Scope / model:** Adaptive comparison sorting of `n` distinct labelled keys, exact minimax/worst-case height `S(n)`, unary threshold language `SORT-BOUND-U = {1^n # 1^k : S(n) <= k}`. Evidence is labeled; no Lean/repo changes beyond this report.

## 1. Evidence reviewed

- **[definition/repo]** `docs/problem.md`: `S(n)` is minimax height; NP membership is not established by exhibiting an exponentially large sorting tree. `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ PSPACE` is the active baseline.
- **[repo paper theorem, pending audit]** `docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md`: framed permutation-DAG certificates are locally sound/checkable in time polynomial in explicit graph size and `n`; if some polynomial-size framed graph attained height `S(n)` for every `n`, then `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ NP`. No all-`n` polynomial-size completeness at exact budget `S(n)` is known.
- **[repo paper construction, pending audit]** `docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` §2: adaptive merge sort admits an all-`n` framed DAG with `O(n^2)` comparison states and `O(n log n)` path length (balanced merge schedule), via state `(a,b)` = remaining lengths in each sorted queue and identity `∑ pq = n(n-1)/2`.
- **[literature/repo deduction]** `docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` §2: equal-halves merge architecture uses `n log2(n)-n+1` comparisons for powers of two, exceeding `log2(n!)` by `(log2(e)-1)n + O(log n)`; with Sergeev's `S(n)=log2(n!)+o(n)` this excludes that architecture from exact optimality for all sufficiently large powers of two. Improving DAG sharing cannot repair the comparison deficit.
- **[repo paper construction, pending audit]** `docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` §3: rank-envelope joins give a plausible polynomial framed certificate for the standard Ford–Johnson upper bound `F_FJ(n)=∑_{j=1}^n ceil(log2(3j/4))`, but this is not `S(n)` for every `n`.
- **[repo paper theorem]** `docs/research-winning-frontier-barrier.md` (as summarized in `docs/research-plan.md`): at budget `S(n)` there are `2^{(1/4-o(1)) n log^2 n}` nonisomorphic winning states at one depth; full winning-frontier/transcript enumeration is ruled out even at the exact optimum.
- **[repo deduction/experiment]** `docs/research-series-decomposition.md`: ordinal-sum identity `F(A ⊕ B)=F(A)+F(B)` is exact but the antichain root is series-prime; the rule does not shrink stored state count in `n<=5` tests and decomposable-state fraction decreases in that range. No asymptotic/state-space reduction or complexity consequence follows.
- **[literature]** Stober–Weiß (ALENEX 2023, arXiv:2206.05597) give exact `S(16..18)` and `S(28)>=99`; OEIS A036604 / prior literature give exact values through `n<=22` with `n=23` open (`docs/literature.md`, `docs/research-plan.md`).

## 2. Computation / small-n and family checks

**[computation]** Local script (`python3`, reproducible inline in this session) compared information bound `C(n)=ceil(log2 n!)`, known `S(n)` for `n<=22`, Ford–Johnson bound `F_FJ(n)`, and balanced merge sort on powers of two.

- `F_FJ(n)==S(n)` for `n=1..21` in known values, but `F_FJ(22)=71 > S(22)=70` (also `C(22)=70`). Thus Ford–Johnson is an all-`n` upper-bound family, not an exact all-`n` optimal family.
- Balanced merge sort comparisons: `n=2:1,4:5,8:17,16:49,32:129,64:321` vs `C(n)=1,5,16,45,118,296` (surplus `0,0,1,4,11,25`). Consistent with the documented asymptotic non-optimality of fixed merge decomposition for large powers of two.

Interpretation: for `n<=21` (except the `n=22` exception relative to FJ) there exist known fixed/algebraic upper-bound procedures matching `S(n)`; the framed-certificate frontier notes plausible polynomial DAG encodings for merge sort and FJ *as constructions*, but an independently audited, all-`n`, exact-height-`S(n)` framed/merge-decomposed certificate is not established by these facts. For large powers of two, fixed merge decomposition is refuted as an exact optimal architecture by comparison count alone.

## 3. Obstruction analysis (merge/series-parallel/bounded-width decomposition)

### 3.1 Fixed merge/series-parallel algorithm architectures

- **[deduction from literature/repo]** A decomposition that commits to a fixed merge architecture (e.g. balanced merge of equal halves composed sequentially) cannot be exact-optimal for all `n`: its path length is provably above `log2(n!)` and hence above `S(n)=log2(n!)+o(n)` on infinitely many large powers of two. This is a concrete architectural obstruction, independent of DAG sharing.
- **[deduction]** Ford–Johnson/rank-envelope insertion schedules are a stronger structured family and match `S(n)` on many small/medium known cases, but `F_FJ(22)>S(22)` shows the standard FJ schedule is not exact-optimal for all `n`. Therefore “known `O(n^2)` fixed constructions” do not supply an all-`n` exact certificate.

### 3.2 State/poset series decomposition

- **[deduction from repo]** Ordinal-sum decomposition applies only when current knowledge factors as a full product `L(A)×L(B)` with all cross-block comparisons fixed. The root antichain is indecomposable, and generic reachable states need not decompose; correlations break naive subset/product generalizations. Hence series-parallel state decomposition gives exact local identities but no global polynomial-size representation of an optimal root strategy.

### 3.3 Bounded-width / polynomial decomposition of the comparison DAG or state graph

- **[repo theorem + deduction]** Any representation that must explicitly enumerate, address, or separately certify the `2^{(1/4-o(1)) n log^2 n}` nonisomorphic winning states on optimal paths (or otherwise scale with winning-frontier/transcript cardinality) is superpolynomial at exact budget `S(n)` (winning-frontier barrier). This is a concrete obstruction to *state-enumeration* and *transcript/frontier-enumeration* certificate schemes, and to any decomposition whose width/size is lower-bounded by distinct optimal state classes.
- **[deduction, partial]** A merge/series-parallel or bounded-width DAG could in principle compress many histories by joins/forgetting, but to remain a valid exact-height-`S(n)` certificate it must preserve enough information to justify every future comparison and every terminal total order. The repo identifies no all-`n` width/compression invariant proving polynomial bounded width for optimal strategies; conversely, known exact/near-exact structured families (merge, FJ) either lose optimality on some `n` or are only upper-bound constructions. Therefore no polynomial-size merge/series-parallel/bounded-width decomposition certificate for exact `S(n)` is currently constructed, and the winning-frontier theorem blocks the most direct “enumerate/compress the optimal state graph” routes.

**Net assessment:** there is a **concrete structural obstruction** to (i) fixed merge architectures as exact-optimal all-`n` certificates, and (ii) certificate schemes based on explicit optimal-state/frontier enumeration. There is **no repo-established theorem** ruling out every conceivable succinct decomposed/joining certificate of an optimal strategy, but also no construction achieving exact height `S(n)` for all `n`.

## 4. Implications for `SORT-BOUND-U` in NP

- **[deduction from frontier doc]** `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ NP` would follow if, for every `n`, some polynomial-size, polynomial-time-checkable framed/decomposed certificate attests a strategy of height `<=k` whenever `S(n)<=k` (in particular at `k=S(n)`).
- **[deduction]** Known polynomial merge/FJ framed constructions certify upper bounds `k >= M(n)` (merge sort bound, `F_FJ(n)`), so they witness membership of corresponding yes-instances with those larger budgets; they do **not** witness `SORT-BOUND-U` at the exact threshold for all `n`.
- **[deduction]** The identified obstructions (fixed-architecture comparison deficit; superpolynomial winning frontier for explicit enumeration) eliminate major natural routes to a polynomial merge-decomposition certificate at height `S(n)`, but they do not prove `SORT-BOUND-U ∉ NP`. Given `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ PSPACE`, `SORT-BOUND-U ∉ NP` would imply `NP != PSPACE` (`docs/problem.md`), so it is out of scope as an unconditional target here.

## 5. Conclusion (falsifiable status)

- **Constructions:** polynomial-size merge/series-parallel (framed DAG / rank-envelope) certificates exist for important **upper-bound families** (merge sort, plausibly Ford–Johnson) [repo constructions, audit pending], but none is known to realize exact `S(n)` for all `n`.
- **Obstruction:** fixed merge decomposition is not exact-optimal for all sufficiently large powers of two; standard FJ is not exact at `n=22`; explicit optimal winning-frontier/state enumeration is superpolynomial [literature/computation/repo theorems]. These obstruct the named decomposition/certification routes.
- **Open:** exact-optimal all-`n` polynomial-size merge/series-parallel or bounded-width decomposed certificate remains open; resolving it either way would materially affect the NP-certificate frontier for `SORT-BOUND-U` (positive: places relevant instances/witness form in NP if fully general and checkable; negative via the specific routes above: narrows viable certificate designs, without separating NP from PSPACE).
