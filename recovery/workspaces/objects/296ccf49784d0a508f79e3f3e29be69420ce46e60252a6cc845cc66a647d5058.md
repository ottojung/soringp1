## Family covered

**All 16,999 isomorphism classes of posets on exactly 8 elements** (the full
`n=8` case, not a subset). No family restriction was needed: after removing the
enumerator bottleneck the entire level took ~85 s, far inside the 25-minute
budget.

Enumeration method (`scripts/oracle_n8.py`): posets are represented as 8 bitmask
rows; for each canonical `(n-1)`-poset we add a new **maximal** element with
every possible down-set and deduplicate by the 1-WL canonical form from
`scripts/oracle3.py`. Adding a maximal element reaches every isomorphism class
(every finite poset has a maximal element). This avoids oracle3's enumeration
of ~431 million *labelled* 8-posets, which was the reason `n=7` took 11.5 min
there. The minimax memo remains keyed by `(n, canonical form)` (the earlier
memo-bug fix), with a separate cache of canonical forms by labelled rows.

## Result

| property | definition | counterexamples in n=8 |
|---|---|---|
| strong | some **optimal** first comparison has both children `>= m-2` | **0** |
| weak (WL) | some first comparison (not necessarily optimal) has both children `>= m-2` | **0** |

**No counterexample found, in the strong or the weak form, among all 16,999
isomorphism classes on 8 elements.** Combined with the completed `n<=7` check
(2450 classes), that is **19,449 classes checked, zero counterexamples**.

## Honest limits

* This is a **finite computation** over `n<=8`, not an all-`n` theorem. A
  counterexample at `n>=9` remains logically possible.
* Correctness rests on the validated canonical form and minimax recurrence,
  not on an externally checked library. The `n=8` count matching the published
  value is strong evidence the 1-WL form did not merge classes at this size,
  but it is still a home-grown invariant.
* No `S(n)` formula, certificate, or complexity-class claim is made here. The
  antichain values were used only as a sanity check on the oracle.
* The weaker lemma remains unproved in general; see `notes/weaker-lemma.md` for
  the sharp obstruction.
