## 1. Question (falsifiable, empirical guidance for all-`n`)

For small `n` where `S(n)` is known/computable here, for optimal height-`S(n)` comparison strategies (adaptive decision trees/policies correct on all `n!` permutations, worst-case depth `S(n)`):

- **(d1)** number of **distinct labelled poset states** appearing as node labels (states on the fixed label set `[n]`, equality as labelled partial orders / identical successor relations).
- **(d2)** number of **distinct unlabelled isomorphism-class states** appearing as node labels (quotient by poset isomorphism / relabelling).
- **(d3)** **minimum**, over all optimal height-`S(n)` strategies, of these counts (reported separately for labelled `d3_l` and unlabelled `d3_u` as feasible).

Distinguish **node occurrences** in a strategy from **distinct states** (hash-consed/DAG view). Also consider the **union over all optimal strategies** (all poset states that can appear on some optimal play).

Falsifiers (guidance):
- *"Large distinct quotient required"*: evidence that optimal strategies force very large `d2/d3_u` (e.g. close to `n!` or superpolynomial growth in a strong form) as `n` grows.
- *"Small quotient possible"*: find optimal strategies with very small `d2/d3_u` (strong compression beyond trivial leaf collapse), suggesting quotient/certificate compression might be feasible.

## 2. Model and definitions used

- Adaptive comparison sort of `n` distinct labelled keys; state = transitive closure of comparisons made so far (strict partial order), represented as successor bitmasks `succ[i]` has bit `j` iff `i < j` (same model as `scripts/evaluate.py`, `scripts/strategy_dag_probe.py`, `scripts/state_compression.py`).
- Minimax value: `F(P)=0` if total, else `F(P)=1+min_{(i,j) incomparable} max(F(P+i<j), F(P+j<i))`; `S(n)=F(antichain_n)`. Known values used: `S(1..6)=0,1,3,5,7,10` (`docs/problem.md`, literature).
- A **correct reduced comparison tree** for `n` has exactly `n!` leaves (one per permutation/total order) and `2*n!-1` node occurrences.
- **Labelled state identity**: identical successor-bitmask tuple on `[n]`.
- **Unlabelled/isomorphism class**: canonical form under `S_n` relabelling via 2D colour refinement + lexicographic minimum over colour-preserving permutations (same canonicalization approach as `scripts/strategy_dag_probe.py` / `scripts/state_compression.py`).
- **Optimal state-policy**: for each poset state reachable under the policy, choose one comparison among moves achieving minimax value `F(P)` (same poset -> same move). This is the natural object for minimizing distinct poset states in a hash-consed/DAG representation. (A non-state-uniform history-dependent optimal tree could in principle differ, but cannot have fewer distinct poset labels than a DAG-consed view and is not the usual compressed form; noted as a modelling caveat.)

## 5. Interpretation and patterns (empirical, small n)

1. **Node occurrences vs distinct states:** every correct reduced strategy has `2*n!-1` node occurrences and `n!` labelled leaf states, but distinct-state counts depend on reuse/isomorphism. (definition/deduction)
2. **No labelled-state reuse observed in optimal state-policies:** for `n<=5`, every sampled optimal state-policy (and every optimal state-policy for `n=3` exhaustively) had `d1 = 2*n!-1`; i.e. no labelled poset state appeared twice. Consequently `d3_l = 2*n!-1` for `n<=3` exactly and equals `2*n!-1` in all sampled cases for `n<=5`. This is strong small-`n` evidence that optimal strategies do not revisit labelled posets, but it is **not proved for all `n`** here.
3. **Unlabelled quotient is much smaller but nontrivial:** `d2_union` grows as `1,2,5,12,19` for `n=1..5` in this data (all reachable optimal-play isomorphism classes), while leaves always collapse to 1 class. Minimum over strategies `d3_u` can be smaller than union/naive choices (`n=3`: 4 vs 5; `n=4`: <=7 vs 12 union). Still, `d3_u >= 1` is trivial and observed values grow with `n` (4, <=7, <=14), giving no sign of bounded/small-constant quotient among optimal strategies.
4. **Union vs single optimal strategy:** the union of all optimal-strategy labelled states grows quickly (`19,181,1551` for `n=3,4,5`) and far exceeds any single strategy's `2*n!-1`, reflecting many alternative optimal comparisons; but this is not d3.
5. **Relevance to all-`n` falsifiers:** 
   - Against "large distinct quotient *required* on some/all optimal strategies" in the strong sense `d3_u` comparable to `2^{\Omega(n\log^2 n)}`: trivial counting gives `d3_u <= d3_l <= 2*n!-1 = 2^{\Theta(n\log n)}` for any optimal strategy (deduction), so such a super-`2^{\Theta(n\log n)}` lower bound on `d3` is impossible; this is consistent with (and complementary to) the winning-frontier barrier, which shows many nonisomorphic winning states exist globally but cannot all fit on one optimal strategy. Small-`n` data show actual `d3_u` far below `2*n!-1`.
   - Against "small quotient possible": no evidence here of polynomial/bounded `d3_u` over `n<=5`; observed minima increase with `n`. This does **not** rule out sub-`2^{\Theta(n\log n)}` or other compressed representations, but provides no positive small-`n` sign of very small optimal quotient families beyond leaf collapse.

## 6. Feasibility limits

- `n<=5` (120 leaves, depth 7): full minimax value computation and union/optimal-move enumeration were tractable with in-memory Python scripts (using existing bitmask-poset model).
- Exact `d3_u`/`d3_l` over all optimal **state-policies** requires optimizing over move choices across all reachable states with interaction/sharing; exhaustive enumeration was feasible for `n=3` (192 policies) but infeasible by brute force for `n=4,5` (73 and 591 multi-choice states respectively, with up to 6/10 choices). I reported achieved upper bounds from heuristic/random sampling.
- `n=6` (`S(6)=10`, 720 leaves, 130023 labelled posets total) was **not attempted**: computing the full optimal-play union/minimax over all relevant states and then exploring optimal strategy choices would be substantially more expensive in this environment and time budget; feasibility for tight `d3` bounds at `n=6` remains uncertain here (union enumeration may be possible with optimized code, but not established by this run).
- No exhaustive enumeration over non-state-uniform history-dependent optimal trees was attempted.
