## A1. Positive result: WL holds for $m$-saturated posets with $m\le 3$

**Proposition A1.** If $Q$ is $m$-saturated and $m(Q)\le 3$, then $f(Q)\ge m(Q)-2$.

*Proof.* $m=2$: threshold $0$, trivial (all depths $\ge 0$).

$m=3$: suppose for contradiction $f(Q)\le 0$. Then **every** incomparable pair
$(u,v)$ has one child of depth $0$, i.e. $Q^{u<v}$ or $Q^{v<u}$ is a total order.
Pick an incomparable pair $(a,b)$ with $Q^{a<b}=L$ a chain; let
$A=\{x : x\le_Q a\}$, $B=\{y : b\le_Q y\}$. Write $a=a_p=\max A$,
$b=b_1=\min B$, and index the chains $A:a_1<\dots<a_p$, $B:b_1<\dots<b_q$.
For $v<_L a$, $v\parallel_Q a$ would force $b\le_Q a$ (by the cross-pair property
below), impossible; hence $A$ is exactly the initial segment of $L$ up to $a$,
$B$ the final segment from $b$, $A\cup B=V$, $A\cap B=\varnothing$; $a,b$ are
consecutive in $L$. All cross relations of $Q$ are of the form $a_i<b_j$: set
$M=\{(i,j):a_i<_Q b_j\}$ and $C=M^c$ (missing cross pairs). Transitivity in the
two chains gives: $(i,j)\in M,\ i'\le i,\ j'\ge j\Rightarrow(i',j')\in M$; hence
$C$ is closed under $i\uparrow,\ j\downarrow$. We have $(p,1)\in C$ ($a\parallel b$).

For a missing pair $(i,j)\in C$, $Q+(a_i<b_j)$ is total iff $C\subseteq\{\le(i,j)\}
=\{i'\le i,j'\le j\}$, since adding $a_i<b_j$ adds exactly the relations
$a_{i'}<b_{j'}$ with $i'\le i,\ j'\le j$. Because $(p,1)\in C$, this forces
$i=p,\ j=1$: **only $(a_p,b_1)$ has a total $a<b$ child.** For any other
$(i,j)\in C$ the orientation $b_j<a_i$ must therefore be total. Adding
$b_j<a_i$ is consistent iff $M\cap\{i'\ge i,j'\le j\}=\varnothing$ (automatic,
since $C$ is $i\uparrow,j\downarrow$-closed and contains $(i,j)$), and it is
total iff $M\cup\{i'\ge i,j'\le j\}$ covers all pairs, i.e. $C=\{i'\ge i,j'\le j\}$.
Take any $(i,j)\in C$ with $(i,j)\ne(p,1)$. If $i<p$, then for $(i,1)\in C$ the
$a<b$ orientation needs $C\subseteq\{i'\le i,j'\le1\}$, impossible because
$(p,1)\in C$; hence $b_1<a_i$ is total, forcing $C=\{i'\ge i,j'\le1\}$ (column
$j=1$). But then $(i',1)\in C$ for every $i'\ge i$, including $i'>i$; for such
$(i',1)$ neither orientation is total ($C\not\subseteq\{\le(i',1)\}$ since
$(p,1)\in C$; and $C\ne\{i''\ge i',j''\le1\}$ since $(i,1)\in C$). Contradiction.
The only remaining possibility is that every $(i,j)\in C$ has $i=p$, i.e.
$C=\{(p,j):1\le j\le q'\}$ for some $q'\ge1$. For $(p,q')$, the $a<b$ orientation
is total (its rectangle covers $C$) and the $b<a$ orientation is also total
($C=\{(i',j'):i'\ge p,j'\le q'\}$), again forcing $m(Q)\le1$. Every case
contradicts $m(Q)=3$; hence no such $Q$ exists. ∎ (cross-pair property: if
$u\parallel v$ in $Q$ and $u<_L v$, then adding $a<b$ makes $u,v$ comparable, so
$u\le_Q a$ and $b\le_Q v$.)

**Consequence.** A minimal counterexample to (WL) has $m\ge 4$ (and is
$m$-saturated by Theorem 3).

## A2. Sharper barrier in a minimal counterexample: the push dichotomy

**Proposition A2.** Let $Q$ be a minimal counterexample, $m=m(Q)\ge4$,
$m$-saturated, so $f(Q)=m-3$. Let $(a,b)$ be any comparison with deep child
$D=Q^{a<b}$ ($m(D)=m-1$) and shallow child $S=Q^{b<a}$ ($m(S)=s\le m-3$).
Then:

1. $f(D)=m-3$ exactly ($D$ is tight). *Proof:* $D$ has depth $m-1<m$, so WL holds
   for $D$: $f(D)\ge m(D)-2=m-3$. If $f(D)\ge m-2$, pushing that witness gives
   $f(Q)\ge m-2$, contradiction.
2. $D$ has an **optimal** witness attaining $f(D)$: some $(c,d)$ optimal in $D$
   with children $D_1=D^{c<d}$ of depth $m-2$ and $D_2=D^{d<c}$ of depth $m-3$.
   *Proof:* an optimal pair of $D$ has max child $=m-2$; if all had min $\ge m-2$
   then $f(D)\ge m-2$, contradicting (1).
3. **Push dichotomy.** For every such $(c,d)$:
   $$m(Q^{c<d})=m-1\quad\text{(gains a full level over }m(D_1)=m-2),$$
   $$m(Q^{d<c})=m-3\quad\text{(no gain at all over }m(D_2)=m-3).$$
   *Proof.* Lower bounds: $Q^{c<d}\supseteq D_1$, $Q^{d<c}\supseteq D_2$, so
   $m(Q^{c<d})\ge m-2$, $m(Q^{d<c})\ge m-3$. Upper bounds: from $Q^{c<d}$ compare
   $(a,b)$: the $a<b$ branch is $D_1$ (depth $m-2$), the $b<a$ branch $S^{c<d}$
   (depth $\le s\le m-3$), so $m(Q^{c<d})\le 1+(m-2)=m-1$; symmetrically
   $m(Q^{d<c})\le 1+\max(m-3,s)=m-2$. Because $Q$ is $m$-saturated, $(c,d)$ is
   optimal for $Q$, so $\max=m-1$, forcing $m(Q^{c<d})=m-1$. Pushing $D_1,D_2$
   also gives $f(Q)\ge m-3$; with $f(Q)\le m-3$ we get $f(Q)=m-3$, forcing
   $m(Q^{d<c})=m-3$. ∎

**Reading of A2 (sharper than `wl-alln-front.md` §"Why the recorded induction…").**
The existing barrier says: saturate the deep child, apply WL there (children
$\ge m-3$), push back — one level short, because the needed strengthening
(children $\ge m(D^*)-1$) is false on `a<b, c isolated`. A2 shows much more: in
any minimal counterexample the lifting is **rigid** — the deep side of *every*
optimal witness in $D$ always gains a level to $m-1$, and the shallow side is
*always* stuck at exactly $m-3$, for every choice of witness and of same-depth
extension. Hence **no** application of the lifting can supply the missing level;
the witness must be a comparison whose shallowness in the child is repaired by
information outside $D$ (the shallow sibling $S$). This makes the "missing
ingredient is non-local" statement precise and unconditional.

## A3. Infinite explicit family: the lifting's bound $m-3$ is sharp and witness-choice-dependent

For $n\ge2$ let $P_n$ be the chain $c_1<\dots<c_{n-1}$ plus an isolated element
$x$. Sorting $P_n$ is exactly inserting $x$ into one of $n$ slots of the chain.

**Fact.** $m(P_n)=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$, and for the pair $(x,c_i)$,
$m(P_n^{x<c_i})=\lceil\log_2 i\rceil$, $m(P_n^{c_i<x})=\lceil\log_2(n-i)\rceil$.
*Proof:* binary search on the slot (upper bound) and $2^m\ge n$ (lower bound);
after $x<c_i$ only the first $i$ slots remain, after $c_i<x$ only the last
$n-i$. ∎

Fix $m\ge3$ and set $n=2^{m-1}+1$, $Q_m=P_n$. Then
$m(Q_m)=\lceil\log_2(2^{m-1}+1)\rceil=m$, and for every pair $(x,c_i)$,
$\max(\lceil\log_2 i\rceil,\lceil\log_2(n-i)\rceil)=m-1$ (since
$i,n-i\le 2^{m-1}$ and $i+(n-i)=2^{m-1}+1>2^{m-1}$), so **$Q_m$ is $m$-saturated**.
Also $f(Q_m)=m-2$ (take $i=2^{m-2}$: depths $m-2,m-1$; both $\ge m-1$ is
impossible since $i+(n-i)>2^{m-1}$). So $Q_m$ is tight and WL holds for $Q_m$.

Now set $t=2^{m-2}+1$ and $D=Q_m^{x<c_t}$ (the deep child of $(x,c_t)$, depths
$m-1$ and $m-2$); $m(D)=m-1$ and $t$ is the number of available slots for $x$
inside $D$. In $D$ the pair $(x,c_j)$ has depths $\lceil\log_2 j\rceil$ and
$\lceil\log_2(t-j)\rceil$. For $m\ge4$ put $j_0=2^{m-3}$, $j_1=j_0+1$:
* both $(x,c_{j_0})$ and $(x,c_{j_1})$ are f-witnesses of $D$ (each has depths
  $\{m-2,m-3\}$, so $f(D)=m-3=m(D)-2$: $D$ is tight);
* pushing $(x,c_{j_0})$ to $Q_m$ gives depths $\{m-3,\,m-1\}$ — **fails** WL;
* pushing $(x,c_{j_1})$ to $Q_m$ gives depths $\{m-2,\,m-1\}$ — **succeeds**.

(Arithmetic: $j_0=2^{m-3}$, $j_1=j_0+1$, $t-j_0=j_1$, $t-j_1=j_0$,
$n-j_0=3\cdot2^{m-3}+1$, $n-j_1=3\cdot2^{m-3}$; both witnesses have $D$-child
ceilings $\{m-3,m-2\}$, while the pushed ceilings are $\{m-3,m-1\}$ for $j_0$
and $\{m-2,m-1\}$ for $j_1$.)
For $m=4$ this is the concrete
$Q_4=$ chain $c_1<\dots<c_8$ plus isolated $x$, $D=Q_4^{x<c_5}$, witnesses
$(x,c_2)$ (pushes to $\{1,3\}$, fails) and $(x,c_3)$ (pushes to $\{2,3\}$,
succeeds).

**Interpretation.** WL for the child $D$ supplies *some* witness but
no criterion to choose a lifting one: two optimal $f$-witnesses of the same $D$
behave oppositely under pushing. The choice depends on how $D$ is embedded in
$Q$ (equivalently on the shallow sibling), which is exactly the non-local data
the recorded method does not use. This also proves the bound $m-3$ of the
lifting is **tight as a guarantee**: an adversarial (legal) choice of witness
attains exactly $m-3$ even though $Q_m$ itself satisfies WL.

**No counterexample is claimed.** A3 is a barrier/sharpness result, not a
refutation of (WL); $Q_m$ satisfies WL. By A1 no counterexample exists with
$m\le3$; none is known at any $m\ge4$.

$m\ge2$ (equivalently, every minimal counterexample) has a comparison with both
children $\ge m-2$; by A1 it suffices to treat $m\ge4$. A2 shows no

needs non-local information about the shallow sibling. Nothing here proves (WL)
for all $n$. Finite evidence (here only $n\le8$; the $n=9$ run contributed none)
is **not** an all-$n$ theorem; a counterexample at $n\ge10$ remains logically
possible. I make
no $S(n)$ formula, certificate or complexity claim. All minimax values used in
A3 are closed-form for the family "chain + isolated" and were derived, not
estimated.
