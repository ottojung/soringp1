# Comparison-split / Kraft obstruction (research note)

**Status:** in-progress investigation.  
**Scope:** unary `SORT-BOUND-U`, model per `docs/problem.md`.  
**Question:** all-n necessary condition on comparison trees stronger than Kraft; force uniform super-log gap for infinite family or show splits are dense enough to approach information bound.

## Setup

Kraft equality holds for any full binary comparison tree: $\sum_{leaves\ \ell} 2^{-d(\ell)} = 1$, giving $n! \le 2^h$ i.e. $h \ge \lceil \log_2 n! \rceil = C(n)$.  
The constraint is: for a state (transitively closed poset) $P$ with $e(P)$ linear extensions and we perform a comparison of incomparable $u,v$, we get split $(e(P+uv), e(P+vu))$ with $e(P+uv)+e(P+vu)=e(P)$ and each side being the number of extensions consistent with adding that relation. Also on the antichain any comparison gives $(n!/2,n!/2)$.

Kahn–Saks (1984) show every poset has some comparison with balance in $(3/11,8/11)$. This means the algorithm *can* choose balanced splits when they exist. Sergeev (2020) gives $S(n)=\log_2(n!)+O(n/(\log n)^{1/5}) = C(n)+o(n)$.

## Known obstructions

- **Exact-reset / branch divisibility** (`docs/research-exact-reset-realizability.md`): going from $A_n$ to $V_n$ requires ratio $n$ integer classes, but first split gives equal halves; for odd $n$, $n/2$ not integer $\implies$ impossible to do exact reset. This is a *necessary condition from the split masses* at the root level (stronger than just total count) — modular/divisibility constraint from realizable comparison splits.
- **Local $L1 > L0$** exists (witness $n\ge5$) but gives only +1 in some cases, not obviously forcing super-log gaps all-n.
- **Tightness is irregular**: equality $S(n)=C(n)$ holds for $n\le11,20,21$ and fails elsewhere; no known infinite family proof either way.

## Stronger-than-Kraft condition (candidate)

For any internal node at depth $c$ with budget $h\ge c$ and state $P_c$ reachable by $c$ comparisons on a height-$h$ correct tree, let $e_c = e(P_c) \le 2^{h-c}$ (Kraft/information). Performing comparison $(u,v)$ gives $e_{c+1}^{(1)}=e(P_c+uv), e_{c+1}^{(2)}=e(P_c+vu)$, sum $e_c$.  
A necessary condition is: for the chosen comparison, $\max(e_{c+1}^{(1)},e_{c+1}^{(2)}) \le 2^{h-c-1}$ in a perfectly balanced info-tight tree, but more directly the *partition* $(e_{c+1}^{(1)}, e_{c+1}^{(2)})$ must be realizable as the linear-extension counts from some incomparable pair in *some* poset reachable by valid comparison history.  
The exact-reset obstruction shows this is not automatic: even if sums match, the root split of $A_n$ produces equal halves but you need to form groups of size $(n-1)!$ and $n/2$ must be integer. So **divisibility/modular constraints on the branch masses** are necessary and stronger than the global Kraft inequality $\sum 2^{-d}=1$.

## Horn A vs Horn B

- **Horn A (super-log gap):** Can we derive an all-n necessary condition forcing $S(n) - C(n) = \Omega(\log n)$ (or some uniform super-log gap) for infinitely many $n$?
- **Horn B (approach bound):** Are comparison-realizable splits dense/flexible enough (Kahn–Saks balanced splits always exist, and structure is rich) that $S(n)-C(n)=o(n)$ (and in fact we can approach the info bound asymptotically)?

Known: Sergeev gives $S(n)-C(n)=o(n)$ which is consistent with B; exact-reset gives strong obstruction for *exact resets* but not obviously for *near-exact* trees; tight cases are sporadic. The board's falsifier for the front prefers trying to establish B ("splits are dense enough...") or disprove via A.

## Analysis

**Branch-divisibility is a stronger necessary condition than Kraft.** The exact-reset obstruction shows: even when total counts satisfy the height requirement, the *recursive partitioning* imposed by pairwise comparisons creates modular constraints. This is similar to some "graph bandwidth" or more directly to the fact that comparison splits are "convex cuts" / order ideals in the permutohedron and have additional structure. So there is indeed a condition stronger than Kraft from comparison realizability.

**On the horns:**
- For *exact* achievement of $C(n)$ (information bound), branch-divisibility/modular obstructions are severe (like exact-reset). This matches why tight cases $S(n)=C(n)$ are rare and sporadic.
- For *asymptotic approach* to the bound, Sergeev's construction shows $S(n)-C(n)=o(n)$ is achievable. The Kahn–Saks 3/11-8/11 theorem guarantees that balanced comparisons exist at every state - this is a strong property suggesting the comparison model is "dense" enough that asymptotically we aren't blocked from approaching the information bound by comparison constraints alone. The exact-reset obstruction is about *exact* transitions to specific posets; it's not obviously a barrier to constructing some tree that stays close to the information bound.

**Conclusion (tentative):** The comparison constraint gives branch-divisibility/modular necessary conditions stronger than Kraft (e.g. exact-reset obstruction). However, these are obstacles to *exact* tightness, not to *asymptotic approach*. The existence of balanced comparisons (Kahn–Saks) and explicit constructions achieving $S(n)=\log_2(n!)+o(n)$ support **Horn B**: splits are dense/flexible enough to approach the information bound asymptotically; there is no known uniform all-n necessary condition forcing a fixed super-log gap for all large $n$ - instead obstructions are local/modular and can be avoided by different strategies.
