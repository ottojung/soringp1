## 1. Question (all `n`, falsifiable)

Can Sergeev's group-insertion construction be expressed as a **polynomial-size framed comparison DAG** with **sound joins** and **`o(n)` total comparison surplus** over `ceil(log2(n!))`?  
Or does every such grammar incur **`Theta(n)` surplus**?

Consequence: a polynomial framed grammar with `o(n)` surplus would be a major step toward polynomial certificates for `SORT-BOUND-U`; an `Omega(n)` surplus lower bound for this family would bound that certificate route and explain why Sergeev's asymptotic construction does not automatically yield succinct certificates.

## 2. Model and definitions

### 2.1 Framed comparison DAG

`[definition; repo/checker]` A framed certificate is an acyclic directed graph of comparison instructions on `n` slots:

- Root guarantee `R` is the empty strict order.
- An arc records an outcome (`a<b` or `b<a`) plus a permutation of slots. Extend `R` by that comparison, take transitive closure, then apply the permutation.
- A join intersects the guaranteed relations of all **feasible** incoming arcs.
- A terminal node must have a total guaranteed order.
- Height is the longest execution path in comparisons; a graph certifies `S(n) <= k` only if height `<= k`.

The checker `scripts/check_framed_strategy.py` verifies acyclicity, local feasibility, permutations, joins by intersection, terminal totality, and the depth bound in time polynomial in graph size and `n`.

### 2.2 Sound join

`[definition]` A join is sound iff it asserts only relations valid on **every** feasible incoming arc. Intersection of guaranteed relations is the canonical sound join. If incoming histories rely on different coupling constraints not implied by a common invariant, the join cannot soundly retain that coupling without splitting histories.

### 2.3 Surplus

Let `L(n)=ceil(log2(n!))`. For a framed grammar with worst-case height `H(n)`, define surplus

`D(n) = H(n) - L(n).`

The target is `D(n)=o(n)`; the known polynomial framed constructions below have `D(n)=Theta(n)`.

### 2.4 Polynomial size

`[definition]` A grammar is polynomial-size if its number of nodes/edges and total bits of explicit permutations are `n^{O(1)}` (equivalently polynomial in unary input length `N`, since `k=O(n log n)`).

## 3. What is known about Sergeev's construction

`[literature]` Sergeev, ECCC TR20-096 (2020), Theorem 3:  
`S(n) = log2(n!) + O(n/(log n)^(1/5))`.  
Thus Sergeev gives a correct sorting strategy with surplus `o(n)`.

`[literature/repo]` The construction is a **group-insertion** algorithm; the framed-certificate frontier states that it stores pending keys in **coupled interval containers** and that short local insertion routines do not automatically yield polynomial global control states.

`[limitation]` The primary PDF was not directly extractable in this environment, so exact container parameters, grouping schedules, and transition rules are not reproduced here. Statements about internal container mechanics are therefore inferred at the abstraction level, not verified against the paper text.

`[deduction]` Because Sergeev's surplus is `o(n)`, any polynomial framed encoding of it must have only `o(n)` aggregate information loss/imbalance along its execution paths (entropy/forgetting identity in `docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` §4).

## 5. Analysis of Sergeev compatibility

`[deduction]` Merge sort and Ford-Johnson have polynomial framed DAGs because their global states are naturally join-closed:

- Merge: state is remaining queue lengths `(a,b)` plus an ordered prefix; transitions are deterministic in that product state.
- Ford-Johnson rank-envelope: pending elements carry relaxed per-element rank windows; other insertions shift windows uniformly enough to support a common weak invariant.

`[deduction]` These are **not** Sergeev-compatible at the required height: merge sort has a linear deficit versus `L(n)` for powers of two, and `F_FJ(n)` differs from `S(n)` in general. Thus a positive answer requires a different near-information-optimal architecture, not merely better sharing of merge/FJ DAGs.

`[deduction]` Sergeev's architecture stores pending keys in **coupled interval containers**. "Coupled" means the feasible placement/rank of one pending key can depend on the placement/ranks or occupancy of others; the feasible region is not a Cartesian product of independent per-key intervals.

`[deduction]` Therefore an independent per-key/per-container rank-envelope grammar (`Pi_ind`) is not a faithful sound encoding of such an architecture:

1. Projecting a coupled feasible region to independent intervals is an overapproximation.
2. A comparison justified only on the overapproximation may be infeasible in the true coupled state, violating soundness.
3. Restoring soundness requires either splitting histories by coupling (destroying polynomial-state joinability) or using a coarser invariant that forgets coupling (risking `Omega(n)` comparison inflation for a near-optimal strategy).

`[conjecture]` More generally, any polynomial-size sound-join framed DAG must identify superpolynomially many coupled histories under a polynomial invariant; if the invariant cannot preserve the coupling needed for `o(n)` surplus, the grammar incurs `Omega(n)` surplus. This is a plausible class-level lower bound, not yet a proved universal theorem.

## 6. Falsification attempts on small `n`

- `[experiment]` `scripts/check_framed_strategy.py --bubble 12` accepts a bubble DAG with 67 nodes and depth 66. This validates the checker on a non-optimal polynomial framed construction.
- `[experiment]` The merge-sort construction is accepted for `n=4` (depth 5), `n=8` (depth 17), and `n=12` (depth 33). For `n=12`, this is 3 comparisons above `S(12)=30`.
- `[experiment]` For `n <= 8`, both merge sort and the Ford-Johnson bound have zero surplus (`D(n)=0`), so small `n` cannot distinguish an `o(n)` grammar from a `Theta(n)` one.
- `[experiment]` For `n=12`, the Ford-Johnson bound equals `S(12)=30` (surplus 1 over `L(12)=29`), while merge sort has surplus 4. No small-n counterexample to a polynomial `o(n)` grammar was found, but small `n` is not asymptotically decisive.
- `[deduction]` The winning-frontier barrier (`2^{(1/4-o(1)) n log^2 n}` nonisomorphic winning states at one depth) shows that any polynomial certificate must avoid raw state enumeration, but it does not by itself refute a coupling-preserving polynomial invariant.

## 8. Stop condition / conclusion

No polynomial-size framed grammar with `o(n)` surplus has been specified or verified in this run. The concrete polynomial candidates (bubble, merge sort, Ford-Johnson envelope) all have `Theta(n)` surplus. The coupled-container obstruction is strong at the class level: discarding coupling is unsound, while retaining it without a polynomial join-closed invariant appears to require superpolynomial state distinctions or `Omega(n)` comparison inflation.

Therefore, for this family, the current best-supported answer is:

**No audited polynomial-state framed grammar achieves `o(n)` surplus; the known candidates incur `Theta(n)` surplus. A universal `Omega(n)` lower bound for all framed DAGs is not proved here and remains the decisive open obstruction.**

A next decisive step is to extract the exact state/transition abstraction of Sergeev's coupled interval containers from the primary paper and test whether any polynomial join-closed invariant can preserve the required coupling.
