## 1. Precise question (restated)

For all `n`, prove or refute a stronger lower bound on the number of states (labelled, or unlabelled/quotient by `Aut`/poset isomorphism, specified clearly) that must appear on **SOME** optimal height-`S(n)` comparison strategy/tree, beyond the trivial `n!` leaves (e.g. `2^{Omega(n log^2 n)}` or any super-`2^{Theta(n log n)}` growth); or construct an infinite family of optimal strategies with provably smaller state/quotient count giving polynomial or `2^{O(n log n)}` upper bound.

Distinguish:
- (a) budget-root-reachable,
- (b) information-admissible,
- (c) locally viable `d + F(P) <= S(n)` (equivalently remaining-budget/winning at its depth under optimal play from root),
- (d) states on some optimal strategy/tree of height `S(n)`.

Winning-frontier barrier: `2^{(1/4-o(1)) n log^2 n}` nonisomorphic winning states at budget `S(n)` but not necessarily all on one optimal play path; can that be bridged or separated?

## 2. Model/definitions used (definition/deduction)

- `S(n)`, comparison decision trees, partial-order states `P`, minimax `F(P)`, and `S(n)=F(empty)` as in `docs/problem.md`, `SoringP1/Minimax.lean` correspondence context, and prior notes.
- A reduced correct comparison tree for `n` distinct keys has exactly `n!` leaves (one per permutation) and `2*n! - 1` nodes total (`docs/research-certificates-barriers.md` Claim C, deduction; standard full-binary-tree fact). An optimal strategy/tree means a correct comparison decision tree of height `S(n)`.
- (a)-(c): taken as the standard classes from prior frontier work (budget-root-reachable from antichain within budget/depth; information-admissible by linear-extension/information cutoff; locally viable `depth(P)+F(P) <= S(n)`). The prompt states prior result: (a),(b),(c) are not all on SOME optimal strategy for `n=5,6`.
- (d): states appearing as node labels on at least one optimal height-`S(n)` strategy/tree. I distinguish **labelled node occurrences**, **distinct labelled posets**, and **distinct unlabelled/isomorphism (`Aut`) classes**.

## 3. Immediate separation/upper bounds for (d) (deduction)

**Claim D1 (labelled node/leaf lower and upper bound, trivial).** Every correct reduced comparison tree for `n` keys, in particular every optimal height-`S(n)` tree, has exactly `n!` labelled leaves and `2*n! - 1` labelled node occurrences total. Hence for (d), counted as labelled nodes/states appearing in the tree, every optimal strategy has count `2*n! - 1 = 2^{Theta(n log n)}` (by Stirling, `log(n!) = Theta(n log n)`), so there is no super-`2^{Theta(n log n)}` lower bound for labelled node count and, moreover, an all-`n` *upper bound* `2^{O(n log n)}` (indeed `<= 2*n!`) holds for the minimum over optimal strategies (take any optimal reduced tree).

**Evidence label:** deduction from full-binary-tree/leaf-distinguishability fact and existence of an optimal comparison tree (`hasBudget_S`/attainment in project model).

**Claim D2 (depth-wise cap blocks bridging the full winning frontier into one tree).** Any binary decision tree of height `h = S(n)` has at most `2^d` nodes at depth `d`, hence at most `2^{S(n)} <= 2^{O(n log n)}` labelled states at any fixed depth and at most `2^{h+1}-1 <= 2^{O(n log n)}` nodes in total. The winning-frontier theorem (`docs/research-winning-frontier-barrier.md`) gives, for infinitely many `n` and depth `t <= S(n)`, at least `2^{(1/4-o(1)) n (log n)^2}` pairwise nonisomorphic winning states `P` with `F(P) <= S(n)-t` (class (c)-like/winning at budget `S(n)`). Therefore **it is impossible for any single optimal tree to contain all (or even a `2^{omega(n log n)}` fraction in the same-depth sense) of that frontier family at that depth**: the tree can realize at most `2^t <= 2^{O(n log n)}` states at depth `t`, exponentially fewer than the constructed family.

**Evidence label:** deduction combining tree arity/counting with the winning-frontier theorem statement (paper-level theorem in `research-winning-frontier-barrier.md`, Sergeev asymptotic used there for `S(n)-C(n)=o(n)`).

**Interpretation:** this **separates** the global winning/frontier class size from "all on one optimal play tree" in a strong counting sense, but it does **not** by itself give a large lower bound on (d): it gives an *upper* obstruction to bridging the whole frontier into one strategy. It also does not prove that no optimal strategy can visit *some* of those frontier states.

## 4. Quotient-by-Aut / unlabelled view (deduction, partial)

- Leaves: all `n!` total orders are isomorphic as posets, so the unlabelled/`Aut` leaf class count is `1` (trivial) even though they are distinct labelled outcomes. Thus "beyond trivial leaves" is meaningful in labelled terms but leaf-quotient is not informative.
- Internal states: a single optimal strategy is a concrete labelled comparison tree; quotienting its node labels by poset isomorphism can only decrease counts, so the unlabelled class count on any given optimal strategy is `<= 2*n! - 1 = 2^{O(n log n)}` by D1. This gives an all-`n` **upper bound** of `2^{O(n log n)}` for the minimum unlabelled state count over optimal strategies as well.
- A super-`2^{Theta(n log n)}` (e.g. `2^{Omega(n log^2 n)}`) lower bound for (d), in either labelled-node or unlabelled-class terms, is therefore **impossible/refuted by construction** in this counting model, because every optimal reduced tree already witnesses `2^{O(n log n)}`.

## 5. What remains open / not established here

- **Stronger-than-leaves lower bound on (d):** whether every optimal strategy must contain substantially more than `n!` leaves in *distinct* labelled posets or in unlabelled classes (beyond the forced `2*n!-1` node occurrences which include leaves and may repeat? in a tree structure nodes are distinct occurrences but same poset can in principle label different nodes) — **open**. I do not claim a proof or refutation of a nontrivial all-`n` lower bound of the form "every optimal strategy has >= f(n) distinct states/classes" except the trivial leaf/permutation forcing in labelled-outcome terms.
- **Bridging in the positive sense:** whether some optimal strategy must pass through many states from the winning-frontier family (or any `2^{Omega(n log^2 n)}` family) — **open**. D2 only rules out containing the whole family / too many per depth.
- **Small-state optimal strategies:** existence of an infinite family of optimal strategies with *polynomial* or `o(n log n)`-exponent unlabelled/quotient state count (e.g. polynomial number of distinct classes, reusing structure heavily) — **open**. D1/D2 give `2^{O(n log n)}` upper bound but no polynomial upper bound.
- Prior prompt fact used as context (not re-proved here): failures of (a)-(c) being all on some optimal strategy at `n=5,6` from earlier optimal-viable-state-counting.

## 6. Assessment

- **Genuine new theorem/definitive frontier advance:** **no** (not claimed). The strongest firm points here are straightforward counting deductions (D1, D2, quotient upper bound) that separate "large winning/frontier sets exist" from "large sets must/ can appear on one optimal tree" but do not settle the asked stronger lower bound vs small-family construction dichotomy beyond the `2^{O(n log n)}` upper bound from any optimal reduced tree.

No explicit all-`n` small-(d) family constructed.
