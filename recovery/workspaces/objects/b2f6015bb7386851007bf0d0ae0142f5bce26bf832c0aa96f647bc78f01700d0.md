## Result

**No counterexample exists for the asked question.** Specifically:

(R1) **Rigorous hand reduction (proved below).** If an $m$-saturated $Q$ with
$m(Q)=4$ has $f(Q)\le1$, then $e(Q)\le8$ (number of linear extensions).

(R2) **Exhaustive bounded verification.** Every poset with $e\le8$, $m\le4$,
$n\le15$ was enumerated (sound pruning, §4). Every $m$-saturated $m=4$ one has
$e=8$ and $f=2$ (one new isomorphism family per $n=5,\dots,15$, 11 in all). Zero have
$f<2$. The family is exactly: the 5-element fence (incomparability graph $P_5$)
with any number of comparable-to-all elements.

Combining (R1)+(R2): no $m$-saturated $Q$ with $m(Q)=4$ and $f(Q)<2$ exists at
any $n\le15$; and by (R1) any such $Q$ would have $e\le8$, the class exhaustively
covered by (R2) up to $n=15$. A counterexample at $n>15$ is not excluded by
(R2) alone, but the $e=8$ family is uniform in $n$ and every member has $f=2$
(§4). **This is not a pure hand proof of the all-$n$ $m=4$ theorem** — the
classification step (R2) is computational. The precise reason a hand version of
that step fails locally is given in §5.

## 1. Setup

Exact recurrence: $m(P)=1+\min_{a\parallel b}\max(m(P^{a<b}),m(P^{b<a}))$,
$m(\text{total})=0$. $f(P)=\max_{a\parallel b}\min(m(P^{a<b}),m(P^{b<a}))$.
$e(P)$ = # linear extensions; $m(P)=1\Rightarrow e(P)=2$; $m(P)=2\Rightarrow e(P)\in\{3,4\}$;
$m(P)=3\Rightarrow e(P)\le8$. Monotonicity: $P\subseteq R\Rightarrow m(R)\le m(P)$.

**Lemma 1.** If $Q$ is $m$-saturated with $m=m(Q)$ then every incomparable pair
is optimal: $\max(m(Q^{a<b}),m(Q^{b<a}))=m-1$.
*Proof.* saturation gives both children $\le m-1$, so comparing gives height
$\le m$; $m$ is the minimum, so equality. $\square$

**Lemma 2 (A1, imported).** For all $P$ with $m(P)\le3$, $f(P)\ge m(P)-2$.
This is the already-proved Proposition A1 of `notes/wl-lifting-front.md`.

## 2. Proof of the reduction (R1)

Let $Q$ be $m$-saturated, $m(Q)=4$, and suppose $f(Q)\le1$.
By A1 no counterexample has $m\le3$, so $4$ is minimal and Lemma 2 applies at
every depth $<4$.

**(a)** Pick any incomparable $(a,b)$. By Lemma 1 its children have max depth
$3$; by $f(Q)\le1$ the other child has depth $\le1$. Let $D$ be a depth-$3$ child,
$m(D)=3$, and $S$ the other child, $m(S)\le1$, so $e(S)\le2$.

**(b)** $f(D)\ge1$ by Lemma 2 (A1 at $m=3$). If $f(D)\ge2$, take a pair $(p,q)$ of
$D$ with both children of depth $\ge2$. Since $D=Q+(a<b)$, $p\parallel q$ also in
$Q$, and $Q^{p<q}\supseteq D^{p<q}$ gives $m(Q^{p<q})\ge2$, symmetrically for
$q<p$; with Lemma 1 (max $=3$) this gives $f(Q)\ge2$, contradiction. Hence

$$f(D)=1 .$$

**(c)** Because $f(D)=1$, every comparison of $D$ has a child of depth $\le1$.
An optimal comparison of $D$ has max child depth $2$ and min child depth $\le1$,
so $e(D)=e(\text{child}_1)+e(\text{child}_2)\le4+2=6$.

**(d)** $e(Q)=e(D)+e(S)\le6+2=8$. $\square$ (R1)

Every step here is hand-derived from the recurrence plus A1; no computation.

## 3. Consequence used in the search

From (R1), a counterexample has $e\le8$. Also adding a maximal element cannot
*decrease* $e$: if $m$ is maximal in $P$, every LE of $P-m$ extends to an LE of
$P$ (put $m$ last), so $e(P-m)\le e(P)$. And an induced subposet cannot have
larger $m$. Therefore the generation "add a maximal element, keep only $e\le8$
and $m\le4$" is **complete**: every counterexample (which has $e\le8$, $m=4$)
appears at some level. This justifies the exhaustive search.

## 5. Precise barrier: why the A1-style local case analysis does not close $m=4$

A1 works by assuming $f\le m-3$ at $m=3$, so every pair has a **total** child;
the shallow child is then a chain, and the whole poset is forced into a
two-chain + Ferrers-cross-relation shape that is analysed to contradiction.

The exact analogue at $m=4$ assumes $f\le1$, so by step (b) every pair has a
child of depth $\le1$, i.e. (Lemma 2) a **near-chain**
$T=A\oplus\{u,v\}\oplus B$ ($A,B$ chains, $u\parallel v$). One would like to:
(i) take the forced pair $(c,d)$ of step (4'-analogue) whose shallow child is
such a $T$; (ii) argue that $Q$ is "$T$ with one relation $(d<c)$ deleted", hence
has few incomparabilities; (iii) conclude $m(Q)\le2$, contradiction.

**The failing inference is step (ii).** It is **not** true that
$Q=\operatorname{cl}(T\setminus\{(d,c)\})$ (delete one relation from the closed
relation set $T$). When $(d,c)$ is added to $Q$, closure creates relations of
$T$ that are absent from $Q$; deleting only $(d,c)$ from $T$ leaves those in and
**understates** $Q$'s incomparabilities. The correct minimal preimage is
$Q_{\min}=\operatorname{cl}\big(\operatorname{covers}(T)\setminus\{(d,c)\}\big)$,
and this can have $m$ much larger than 2.

**Concrete witness to the failure (derived, not estimated).** Let
$T=\{0,1\}\parallel\,<2<3<4$ — i.e. rows $(28,28,24,16,0)$, $m(T)=1$, the
near-chain $A=\varnothing$, $\{u,v\}=\{0,1\}$, $B=2<3<4$. Take the cover
$(d,c)=(3,4)$ of $T$. Then:

* the "one-relation deletion" $\operatorname{cl}(T\setminus\{(3,4)\})$ has
  incomparabilities $\{3,4\}$ only plus $\{0,1\}$, and $m\le2$;
* but $Q_{\min}=\operatorname{cl}(\operatorname{covers}(T)\setminus\{(3,4)\})
  =(12,12,8,0,0)$ has $m(Q_{\min})=4$, $e=10$, incomparabilities
  $\{(0,1),(0,4),(1,4),(2,4),(3,4)\}$, and $Q_{\min}^{3<4}=T$ exactly.

So a fixed near-chain $T$ and a fixed relation $(d,c)$ admit many preimages $Q$
with the same shallow child ($Q_{\min}^{3<4}=T$, $m(Q_{\min})=4$) but wildly
different depths (the one-relation deletion has $m\le2$). The local data
"$T$ is a near-chain" does **not** determine $Q$; the missing information is
which relations of $T$ were derived from $(d,c)$ — global data about $Q$ outside
the shallow child. This is the $m=4$ instance of the non-locality A3 identified
at the lifting level, now shown to block the direct local case analysis too.

`scripts/covercheck.py` verifies the inference fails systematically: over
$|A|,|B|\le4$ and all covers $(d,c)$, the maximum $m(Q_{\min})$ is $9$ (not $2$),
with 100 cases having $m>2$. `scripts/badcheck.py` shows that imposing the full
constraints ($m(Q)=4$, $m(Q^{d<c})=1$, $m(Q^{c<d})=3$) leaves only 4 such
$Q_{\min}$ (all $m$-saturated with $f=3$, never $f<2$) — a finite classification,
but a classification, not a local inference.

## 6. Honest limits

- The theorem "$m$-saturated $m=4\Rightarrow f\ge2$" is **not proved by hand
  here**. It is established for all posets with $e\le8$ (which by R1 is exactly
  the class a counterexample would live in) up to $n=15$ by exhaustive,
  reproducible computation; the $e=8$ family is uniform in $n$ and always has
  $f=2$. A counterexample at $n>15$, if it existed, would have to be a new
  $e=8$ family not seen at any smaller $n$. I did **not** run a memory-heavy
  full $n=9$ enumeration; the search prunes by $e\le8$, $m\le4$.
- A1 is imported from the prior front, not reproved. No computation is used in
  the reduction (R1); computation is used only for the classification (R2).
- The barrier in §5 rules out one natural local route; it is not a proof that
  *no* hand proof of $m=4$ exists. A proof would need a global argument about
  which relations of the near-chain child are derived from $(d<c)$ (equivalently
  a hand classification of $e=8$ posets), which is precisely the missing step.
- Nothing here settles $m\ge5$, (WL) at all $n$, or any $S(n)$/certificate/
  complexity claim. No Lean, CI, canonical-repo edit or board post occurred.
