# Independent review: finite T(4) threshold package

**Reviewed object:** SoringP1 commit
`41f1b50102d9d2949d9c0934bbf59907d607dc90` on
`research/t4-threshold-computational-20261106`.

**Disposition:** The finite mathematical claim `T(4)=12` is independently
supported by the proof reductions and reproducible exhaustive computation
described below. This is not a Lean-checked theorem, an all-n result about
`S(n)`, or a complexity classification for `SORT-BOUND-U`. The source branch
remains local and unmerged. `git verify-commit` reports a valid signature, but
the signing key is not trust-certified.

## Independent evidence

I inspected the theorem reductions and wrote a separate checker at
[`scripts/t4_threshold/reverse_independent_review.py`](../scripts/t4_threshold/reverse_independent_review.py).
It does not import the package generator or validators. It enumerates by
adding the *smallest* label first, the reverse of the package's largest-label
column recursion, and computes extension sets and minimax values directly.

The independent checker reports:

- The `T(3)=7` connected base: all 62,991 candidate edge subsets with
  `n=2..7` and `n-1 <= q <= 6` were examined. Exactly 49 encode transitive
  connected posets with `e<=7`; all have `F<=3`. Histogram by `(e,F)`:
  `(2,1):1`, `(3,2):3`, `(4,2):4`, `(5,3):10`, `(6,3):10`, `(7,3):21`.
- The sharpness witness `(0,0,1,3,7)` has `(e,F)=(8,4)`.
- For connected residuals with `e in {11,12}`, width at most three, and
  `q<=11`, reverse-label enumeration found exactly the 132 saved encodings
  for `n<=11`, with no omissions or extras. It independently obtained
  `F=4` for every candidate.
- At `n=12`, with `q=11`, the independent enumeration found exactly the 12
  saved encodings, again with `F=4` for every candidate. The connectivity
  condition and `q=n-1` imply the incomparability graph is a tree.
- The six-element sharpness witness `(20,16,0,23,0,22)` has `(e,F)=(13,5)`.

The finite enumeration is exhaustive because every poset can be relabelled by
a natural linear extension; its incomparability edges then uniquely determine
the poset. For the connected `e=11,12` residual, the proved bound
`q <= e-1` and connectedness `q >= n-1` give `n<=11` for `e=11`, and at
`n=12,e=12` force `q=11`. The review checker enforces the natural-extension
transitivity condition when adding each new minimum; it prunes only when the
exact truncated extension count already exceeds 12, which cannot decrease
when an element is added. Candidate-set equality is checked, not just counts.

The bound `q<=e-1` follows from the connected graph of linear extensions
under adjacent swaps: for each incomparable pair there are extensions in
both orientations, and the unique spanning-tree path between them must contain
an adjacent swap of that pair. Distinct pairs label distinct tree edges.
Width at most three follows from `e>=w!` by ordering a maximum antichain.

For disconnected posets, the incomparability components are ordinal-sum
factors, with extension counts multiplying and optimal depths adding. For
`e<=7`, the only products of two nontrivial factor counts are `2*2` and
`2*3`, covered by the connected base. For `e=11`, primality leaves one
nontrivial factor. For `e=12`, the nontrivial products are `12`, `6*2`,
`4*3`, and `3*2*2`. The `4*3` case requires the often-unstated fact that
every poset with at most four extensions is sortable in at most two
comparisons. This follows from the connected `e<=4` cases in the exhaustive
base (`q<=3` implies at most four elements), plus ordinal-sum factorization;
the independent histogram confirms the connected cases.

For `8<=e<=10`, Kahn--Saks (1984) supplies a comparison whose larger child
has at most `floor(8e/11) <= 7` extensions, so the `T(3)=7` base completes
those cases in at most four comparisons. I checked the journal's published
abstract, which states the `3/11` to `8/11` orientation-proportion theorem;
I did not inspect the full article proof. The source is Kahn and Saks,
*Balancing poset extensions*, *Order* 1 (1984), 113--126,
https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00565647.

## Scope and remaining delivery

These checks establish the finite threshold theorem only, conditional on the
cited Kahn--Saks theorem. They do not establish an improved evaluator for
`S(n)`, an all-n sorting theorem, or membership in a smaller complexity class.
The source commit has no external independent branch CI or merge. No source
files in the reviewed commit were changed; only this separate review worktree
contains the review checker and this report.
