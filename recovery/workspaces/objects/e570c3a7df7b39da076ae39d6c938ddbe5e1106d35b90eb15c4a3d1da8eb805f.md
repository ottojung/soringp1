## 2. Precise hypothesis (falsifiable, all `n`)

Let $F(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lceil \log_2(3i/4)\rceil$ (with the $i=1$ term 0) be the worst-case comparison count of Ford–Johnson merge-insertion (**literature/definition**). Merge-insertion is the canonical **Fibonacci/Jacobsthal-split** adaptive strategy: it forms pairs, recursively sorts winners, and inserts pending elements in Jacobsthal-number-driven groups/orders yielding the $\lceil\log_2(3i/4)\rceil$ insertion widths (**literature**, Knuth; see also in-repo `docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` §3).

**H (global optimal characterization/achievement, all $n$).** For every $n \ge 1$, the optimal comparison-sorting number satisfies
$$
S(n)=F(n),
$$
and this optimum is achieved (hence characterizable) by the Ford–Johnson Jacobsthal-split/merge-insertion strategy (a Fibonacci/Jacobsthal-like split rule).

A weaker “broad exact class extending to all sufficiently large $n$” reading is: there exists $n_0$ such that for all $n \ge n_0$, some optimal (height-$S(n)$) strategy is characterized by a Ford–Johnson/Jacobsthal-like split rule, in particular $S(n)=F(n)$ for all $n\ge n_0$.

**Consequence if true:** evaluating $S(n)$ on the unary input $1^n$ would be trivial (output the closed form/value $F(n)$ computable in time polynomial in $n$, hence in the unary input length $N$), placing $S_{\mathrm{unary}}\in \mathrm{FP}$ / `SORT-BOUND-U` in `P` via the standard threshold equivalence noted in `docs/problem.md`. Structurally, an explicit uniform optimal strategy family would be identified for all $n$.

**Literature (as in `docs/research-plan.md`, `docs/literature.md`).** Exact values: $S(n)=I(n)$ for $1\le n\le 11$; $S(12)=30$ (Wells/Peczarski); $S(16)=46,S(17)=50,S(18)=54$ (Stober–Weiß 2023); exact values known for all $n\le 22$ as tabulated there.

**Deduction (finite consistency, not a proof of H).** For every $n\le 22$, $F(n)=S(n)$ (the Ford–Johnson Jacobsthal-split strategy achieves the known optimum, including the first information-bound gap at $n=12$ where both equal $30$). Thus H is **not refuted on any currently known exact value** and the Jacobsthal-split rule is optimal on this broad finite class $1\le n\le 22$.

## 4. Proof, refutation, or obstruction

### 4.1 No finite counterexample from known exact $S(n)$
**Deduction from literature/experiment:** since $S(n)\le F(n)$ always (FJ is a correct uniform sorting strategy) and $S(n)=F(n)$ for all $n\le 22$, no $n\le 22$ is a counterexample to $S(n)=F(n)$.

### 4.2 Asymptotic obstruction to the all-$n$ / large-$n$ form of H
**Literature.** Sergeev (2020), as recorded in-repo, proves $S(n) \le \log_2 n! + o(n)$, hence in particular $S(n) = \log_2 n! + o(n)$ given the information lower bound $S(n)\ge \log_2 n!$.

**Literature/deduction (standard asymptotic of merge-insertion).** Ford–Johnson satisfies $F(n)=\log_2 n! + \Theta(n)$; equivalently there exists a constant $c>0$ and $n_1$ with $F(n) \ge \log_2 n! + c n$ for all $n\ge n_1$ (consistent with the computed gaps, e.g. $\approx 0.111n$ at $n=1000$).

**Deduction (rigorous obstruction).** Combining these: for sufficiently large $n$, $o(n) < c n$, so
$$
S(n) \le \log_2 n! + o(n) < \log_2 n! + c n \le F(n).
$$
Therefore there exists $n_2$ such that for all $n\ge n_2$, $S(n) < F(n)$. 

**Conclusion on H:** 
- The strong all-$n$ equality $S(n)=F(n)$ is **false in general** (refuted asymptotically for all sufficiently large $n$), even though it holds on the entire currently-known exact range $n\le 22$.
- The weaker “Jacobsthal-split/Merge-insertion characterizes optimal strategies for all sufficiently large $n$” is **obstructed** in the specific Ford–Johnson instantiation (it cannot be optimal for large $n$ because of a linear comparison overhead above $\log_2 n!$). A different, as-yet-unspecified Fibonacci/Jacobsthal-like global split rule achieving Sergeev’s $o(n)$ surplus is **not established** here; existing in-repo notes (`docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` §2, §5) record that the equal-chain merge architecture has an unavoidable linear deficit and that Sergeev-compatible polynomial-state control is open.

### 4.3 Structural note (non-characterization)
**Deduction.** Even where $F(n)=S(n)$ (e.g. $n\le 22$), equality of worst-case counts does not by itself characterize *all* optimal strategies as Jacobsthal-split strategies; it only witnesses that one such strategy attains the optimum. No uniqueness/characterization theorem is claimed.

## 5. Consequence

- **For the central question (uniform evaluation of $S(n)$, unary encoding):** The Jacobsthal-split merge-insertion rule yields a valid, explicit polynomial-time-computable **upper bound** $S(n)\le F(n)$ for all $n$ (**literature**), but it does **not** provide an all-$n$ exact evaluator of $S(n)$ and thus does not imply $S_{\mathrm{unary}}\in \mathrm{FP}$ or `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ P`. Any exact characterization via this split is limited to a finite/bounded regime unless a fundamentally different $o(n)$-overhead realization is found.
- **For research direction:** A “Fibonacci/Jacobsthal split achieves $S(n)$ for all $n$” is a **refuted/obstructed hypothesis** asymptotically (by Sergeev vs. linear overhead of FJ). The productive residual question—consistent with `docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md`—is whether some Jacobsthal/Sergeev-compatible splitting and join/control structure can be made into a polynomial-state (certificate/verifier-friendly) all-$n$ strategy with $o(n)$ surplus; this remains **open** and is distinct from proving $S(n)=F(n)$.
- **Falsification status summary:** H (equality/optimality of FJ for all $n$) — **falsified asymptotically** (all sufficiently large $n$); **consistent** with all known exact $n\le 22$. No new bound on $S(n)$ beyond literature is proved here.
