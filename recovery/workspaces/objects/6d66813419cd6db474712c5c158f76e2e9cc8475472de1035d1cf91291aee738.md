## Question (all n, falsifiable)

Can one prove an improved all-n upper bound on `S(n)` via an explicitly analyzed adaptive merge/insertion strategy, beyond Ford-Johnson where known or via a new uniform construction, yielding

`S(n) <= C(n) + f(n)`, `C(n)=ceil(log2 n!)`, with explicit `f(n)=o(n)`

or a concrete constant-factor improvement in the gap? Or is there a precise obstruction showing why known adaptive merge families cannot beat current bounds?

## Fixed model and baseline

`S(n)` is the minimum worst-case number of adaptive pairwise comparisons needed to sort `n` distinct labelled keys. `C(n)=ceil(log2 n!)` is the information lower bound. Known exact values through `n=22` are tabulated in `docs/research-plan.md` and include `S(12)=30` against `C(12)=29`.

## Construction/analysis 1: balanced binary merge

For a power of two `n=2^m`, an optimal equal-chain merge schedule uses

`n*m - n + 1 = n(log2 n - 1) + 1`

comparisons. The information bound is

`log2(n!) = n log2 n - (log2 e)n + O(log n)`.

Thus the surplus is

`(log2 e - 1)n + O(log n) ≈ 0.4427 n`.

This is a linear, constant-factor deficit. Improving DAG sharing cannot repair the comparison count: the merge architecture itself performs too many comparisons. So binary merge is **not** an `o(n)`-surplus construction.

## Construction/analysis 2: Ford-Johnson merge-insertion

Ford-Johnson gives the explicit all-n bound

`M(n) = sum_{i=1}^n ceil(log2(3i/4))`.

Arithmetic check (`scripts/upper_witness.py`, `fj_upper_bound`):

| n | S(n) | C(n) | M(n) | M(n)-S(n) | M(n)-C(n) |
|---|------|------|------|-----------|-----------|
| 10 | 22 | 22 | 22 | 0 | 0 |
| 12 | 30 | 29 | 30 | 0 | 1 |
| 16 | 46 | 45 | 46 | 0 | 1 |
| 20 | 62 | 62 | 62 | 0 | 0 |
| 22 | 71 | 70 | 71 | 0 | 1 |

For larger n:

| n | M(n) | C(n) | (M-C)/n |
|---|------|------|---------|

| 1000 | 8641 | 8530 | 0.111 |

Thus `M(n)-C(n)=Theta(n)`. Ford-Johnson is exact on all known values `n<=22`, but its surplus over the information bound does not tend to `o(n)`. It is the best explicit finite adaptive merge/insertion construction in the repository's audited material, but it does not satisfy `f(n)=o(n)`.

## Construction/analysis 3: Sergeev group insertion

Sergeev's theorem (ECCC TR20-096, Theorem 3) states

`S(n) = log2(n!) + O(n/(log n)^(1/5))`.

This does yield an all-n upper bound

`S(n) <= C(n) + f(n)`, with `f(n)=O(n/(log n)^(1/5))=o(n)`,

at the asymptotic level. So the answer is **yes in the asymptotic/literature sense**.

However, the audited effective-extraction report shows that the published proof does not supply an explicit finite threshold or constant: Lemma 4 requires `s>=20`, which forces an astronomically large `n` (at least double-exponential in the natural reading) before the construction is in force, and hidden constants are not tracked. Therefore this is not, as currently published, an explicitly analyzed finite uniform construction with a computable `f(n)` for every `n`. It is an asymptotic theorem, not an exact-height merge/insertion strategy with explicit finite constants.

## Obstruction for known adaptive merge families

The precise obstruction is comparison-count and state-coupling, not merely implementation detail:

1. **Fixed merge architectures have a linear deficit.** Equal-halves merge costs `n log2 n - n + 1`; the information bound is `n log2 n - (log2 e)n + O(log n)`. The gap is `(log2 e - 1)n`, so no amount of DAG sharing can make this architecture `o(n)`-optimal.

2. **Ford-Johnson has linear slack.** Its Jacobsthal insertion windows produce `M(n)-C(n) ≈ 0.085 n` in the computed large-n regime. The windows are cheap on average but not sufficiently tight in aggregate to reach `o(n)`.

3. **Reaching `o(n)` requires coupled interval containers.** Sergeev's construction stores pending keys in coupled interval containers. A simple framed adaptive merge/insertion grammar based on independent per-key rank envelopes is a Cartesian overapproximation of that coupled feasible region: forgetting inter-key coupling is unsound unless unused, while retaining all coupled distinctions destroys polynomial-state joinability. Thus known simple adaptive merge/insertion families cannot be patched by better sharing alone; they lack the coupled-container structure needed for sublinear aggregate surplus.

This is an architecture-specific obstruction, not a universal impossibility theorem for all succinct certificates. The winning-frontier barrier (`2^{(1/4-o(1)) n log^2 n}` nonisomorphic winning states at one depth) additionally rules out raw enumeration of optimal states, but it does not by itself rule out a future coupled-container grammar.

## Conclusion

- **Asymptotic improved upper bound:** yes. Sergeev proves `S(n) <= log2(n!) + O(n/(log n)^(1/5))`, so `f(n)=o(n)` exists at the asymptotic level.
- **Explicit finite adaptive merge/insertion construction beating Ford-Johnson with `f(n)=o(n)` or a concrete better constant:** no currently established construction. Ford-Johnson remains the best explicit finite merge/insertion bound; it is exact on all known values through `n=22` but has `Theta(n)` surplus.
- **Precise obstruction:** fixed merge architectures have a linear comparison deficit, Ford-Johnson has linear aggregate slack, and achieving sublinear surplus requires coupled interval-container structure not captured by simple adaptive merge/insertion or independent rank-envelope families.

## Falsifiers

1. An explicit finite uniform construction (or fully constant-tracked version of Sergeev's construction) with `S(n) <= C(n)+f(n)` and `f(n)=o(n)` for all sufficiently large n, with a locally checkable height analysis.
2. A rigorous all-n theorem showing that every sound join-closed framed grammar for Sergeev-style coupled-container group insertion requires superpolynomially many global states, or forces `omega(n)` surplus.
3. A new adaptive merge/insertion family with a provably smaller constant than Ford-Johnson's `≈0.085 n` surplus on an infinite family of n, without relying on coupled containers.
