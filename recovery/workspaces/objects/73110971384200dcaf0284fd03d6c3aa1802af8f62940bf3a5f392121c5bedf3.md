# Report: polynomial-size symmetry-quotient strategy DAG for S(n)

## Summary
**Answer:** No obvious polynomial construction is apparent; the evidence points to a likely obstruction (or at least no known construction), but no formal proof of impossibility is established from available data.

## What the data shows
From `soring301/docs/research-strategy-certificates.md` and experiments (`scripts/strategy_dag_probe.py`):

- Single optimal policy DAG = tree (no hash-consing benefit), `DAG(1)=2n!-1`. Symmetry quotient `quotient(1)` (isomorphism classes of posets visited by some optimal strategy): 
  - lex tie-breaking: n=1:1,2:2,3:4,4:7,5:11,6:27
  - best found by random search/tie-breaking: n=6 gives down to ~14, still grows quickly
- All-optimal DAG union `quotient(all)`: n=1:1,2:2,3:5,4:12,5:19,6:134 (jumps 7× from n=5→6)
- Growth of `log2(DAG(all))/n` is increasing over n≤6 (consistent with super-polynomial), but n≤6 too small for asymptotic claim
- A quotient certificate (with explicit relabeling permutations) is poly-time checkable; the question is whether number of quotient nodes is polynomial in n

## Analysis

### Why a polynomial construction seems hard
1. **Optimal (exact S(n)) vs asymptotically optimal:** Merge sort gives O(n²/log n) state classes roughly but is not optimal for all n (e.g. n=5 gives 8 vs optimal 7). Ford-Johnson better but also not achieving exact S(n) for all n. To achieve *exact* S(n) for all n, the strategy must adapt to the exact information-theoretic/poset constraints at each n; known optimal strategies don't form a uniform polynomial family.
2. **Quotient growth under optimality:** Even with optimal tie-breaking we can't get quotient(1) small at n=6 (minimum found ~14). The jump suggests combinatorial explosion as the optimal strategy must make finer distinctions.
3. **State types multiply:** Optimal strategies visit posets of various "shapes" with different numbers of incomparable pairs and different minimax values; the number of isomorphism classes of partial orders grows quickly (A000112) and optimality is a strong constraint but not obviously polynomially restrictive.
4. **No uniform structure obvious:** Unlike comparison sorts with fixed patterns (merge sort has O(n log n) comparisons but wrong count; insertion sort similar), achieving the exact minimum requires different comparison patterns that don't collapse under symmetry for larger n.

### Why obstruction/proof is hard
- n≤6 is tiny; exponential vs polynomial vs n^(log n) all plausible from current data
- The "framed certificate" / quotient model is checkable if given, but existence of polynomial all-n quotient for some optimal strategy is open
- Sparsity/Mahaney gives barriers for NP-hardness of SORT-BOUND-U, but doesn't directly rule out polynomial-size certificates or polynomial quotient DAGs
- The all-optimal union grows fast, but we only need *some single optimal strategy* (quotient(1)), which is much smaller

### Candidate obstructions to consider
1. **Information-theoretic/poset class explosion:** The number of "information states" (up to symmetry) that remain necessary to achieve exactly the information-theoretic optimum at certain n is super-polynomial. Since S(n) equals log2(n!) at n∈{1..11,20,21} but not others, the structure might be inherently more complex.
2. **Tie-breaking freedom isn't enough:** Random search over optimal moves didn't find polynomial collapse for n=6; greedy heuristics also failed to reduce below ~14.
3. **Root-reachable equivalence stronger than relabeling fails:** The frontier question (root-reachable-certified-quotient) suggests there may be no polynomial bound on distinct classes for any equivalence stronger than relabeling that still preserves optimality - i.e. you cannot compress below some super-polynomial number.

## Conclusion
**No polynomial-size symmetry-quotient strategy DAG construction is known, and available experiments are consistent with super-polynomial growth for optimal strategies. However, there is no formal obstruction proof in the examined literature.** The question remains open. Any claim of impossibility would require asymptotic analysis beyond n≤6; any claim of construction would require a uniform family achieving exact S(n) with polynomial quotient classes - none is obvious from current data.
