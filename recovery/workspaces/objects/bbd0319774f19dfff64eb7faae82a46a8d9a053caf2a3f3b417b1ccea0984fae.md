# Analysis of the proposed all-n obstruction claim

## Claim under test
For all sufficiently large n where n! is not a power of two, there is an all-n combinatorial obstruction (beyond simple integer rounding of log2(n!)) forcing S(n) >= ceil(log2(n!)) + 1 due to comparison (pairwise test) basis constraints on the state graph, independent of specific poset structure counts.

Falsifier: Exhibit an infinite family or a general construction showing a comparison-based decision tree can achieve height exactly ceil(log2(n!)) for all n in that class despite n! not power of two, or prove the comparison basis imposes no additional all-n lower bound beyond the Kraft/information bound.

## Known facts (primary/literature as in repo)

- Information bound: C(n)=⌈log2 n!⌉ <= S(n). Tightness holds for small n (n<=11) and returns at larger n (n=20,21). Non-tight at others where known (n=12 gives S(12)=30 > 29). (docs/research-certificates-barriers.md, literature.md)
- n! is not a power of two for most n>=3 (factorial grows quickly and has odd prime factors). 
- For n=20, log2(20!)~61.077 so C(20)=62; docs state S(20)=C(20)=62 despite 20! not a power of two. Similarly S(21)=C(21)=66. (docs/research-certificates-barriers.md:125)
- Peczarski/Wells/Stober–Weiss show non-tightness is not explained by a simple scalar function of e(P) or by monotonicity alone (same e can give different m(P), fewer extensions can be harder). The obstruction is a property of the comparison-split structure/state graph constraints, not just Kraft leaf counting.
- Hanamura–Iwata gives a local poset example where e=7<8 but is sortable at ceil(log2 7)=3 (tight) while other posets with same/local counts behave differently - reinforcing that structure matters.
- The Kraft inequality is necessary for any prefix code/binary tree regardless of test types; comparison basis restricts allowed splits (not every partition into two subsets is realizable by a single pairwise comparison from a given poset/state consistent with order constraints). However, the question asks: does this basis create an *all-n obstruction* (for ALL sufficiently large n in that class) forcing +1 beyond rounding?

## Result

**The claim is false as stated.**

Counterexamples exist: n=20 and n=21 satisfy "n! is not a power of two" but S(20)=⌈log2 20!⌉ and S(21)=⌈log2 21!⌉ (tight), so no +1 obstruction holds. The pattern of tightness is irregular (returns at 20,21 after failures elsewhere), showing there is no universal all-n obstruction for all sufficiently large n in that class.

The comparison basis does impose constraints beyond Kraft in general (e.g. explaining non-tightness cases like n=12 via split-structure constraints as shown by Peczarski/Stober–Weiss), but those constraints do not force a +1 for *every* n where n! is not a power of two. The boundary is not "rounding plus comparison basis forces +1", but depends on structure/symmetry for specific n.

## Distinction (as requested)
- General-poset results: e(P) counting is #P-hard and monotonicity fails; sortability depends on poset structure/symmetry, not just size of e(P). 
- Certificate soundness vs completeness: information bound is necessary (sound). Tightness (achieving ceil(log2 n!)) means there exists some comparison tree achieving the Kraft bound for that n - so comparison basis is *not* blocking completeness to the information bound for those n; the obstruction in non-tight cases is that *all* comparison trees fall short, which is a property of the constrained split graph for those specific state families, not a general theorem forcing failure for all large n with n! not a power of two.

## Evidence cited
- docs/research-certificates-barriers.md:125 states S(n)=⌈log2 n!⌉ for n<=11 and n=20,21
- docs/research-n12-nontightness.md details non-monotonicity in e(P) and structure-dependence
- Computed: n=20 gives ceil(log2 20!)=62, 20! not power of two; literature says tight.

## Conclusion
Disprove the all-n claim. No infinite/all-large-n obstruction from comparison basis beyond rounding is known; counterexamples at 20,21 refute it. The real phenomenon is sporadic non-tightness due to comparison-split constraints for particular n/structures.
