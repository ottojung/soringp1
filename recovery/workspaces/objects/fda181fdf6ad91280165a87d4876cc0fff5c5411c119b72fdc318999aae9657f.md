**Falsifiable target.** Propose a *structural* (grounded in reachable posets / comparison strategy, not a scalar fit in `n!` or `2^{C(n)}/n!`) necessary and sufficient condition, or a computable characterization, that predicts for which `n` the bound is tight (`S(n)=C(n)`), consistent with all known `n ≤ 22`, and attempt to generalize to all `n` or give a concrete obstruction.

**Why it would matter (consequence).** A genuine structural characterization separating tight from non-tight `n` would isolate which features of the minimax game over partial orders force the `+1` loss, potentially yielding a reusable lower-bound witness schema or, conversely, a constructive strategy family certifying tightness—directly informative for evaluating/understanding `S(n)` beyond exhaustive search. A rigorous obstruction (showing no condition of a given structural form can characterize tightness) would delimit the kind of invariant any future method must use.

## 2. Model and structural objects (definitions)

- **State (poset).** After `c` comparisons, the information state is a transitively closed partial order `P` on `n` labelled elements; `e(P)` is its number of linear extensions. Root: antichain `P0`, `e(P0)=n!`. A comparison `(u,v)` on incomparable elements yields children `P+uv`, `P+vu` with `e(P+uv)+e(P+vu)=e(P)`. **[definition]**
- **Minimax value.** `m(P)` = minimum further comparisons to sort every extension of `P` (`m(P)=0` iff total order). `S(n)=m(P0)`. **[definition]**, aligned with `adv`/`S_eq_adv_univ` context in repo docs.
- **Node-level information feasibility at budget `h`.** A state at depth `c` is *h-feasible* if `e(P) ≤ 2^(h-c)` (equivalently `⌈log₂ e(P)⌉ ≤ h-c`). A comparison is *h-admissible* at depth `c` if both children are `h-feasible`, i.e. `max(e(P+uv),e(P+vu)) ≤ 2^(h-c-1)`. This is definitionally equivalent to the classical “efficiency” pruning (`E(P)=n!/(2^c e(P)) ≥ n!/2^h`) (Stober–Weiss; see `docs/research-n12-nontightness.md` §4). **[definition/deduction]**
- **Information-feasible strategy game.** Define `W_h(P,c)` to hold iff `P` is `h`-feasible at `c` and either `P` is total or some `h`-admissible comparison from `P` has both children satisfying `W_h(·,c+1)`. **[definition]**

## 3. Hypothesis (structural/computable characterization)

**H (Strategy-structural feasibility).** For all `n ≥ 0`,
> `S(n)=C(n)`  iff  `W_{C(n)}(P0,0)` holds.

Equivalently, tightness of the global information bound is characterized by the existence of a comparison strategy whose entire reachable poset trace stays inside the *information-feasible region* `F_{n} = {(P,c) : e(P) ≤ 2^{C(n)-c}}` (a structural constraint on states/transitions of the minimax game), with no node ever forced outside it.

**Computable form.** `W_{C(n)}(P0,0)` is decidable by finite AND/OR search over the finite DAG of reachable labelled posets modulo the game (the Wells/Peczarski/Stober–Weiss forward/backward pruning searches are instances/optimizations of this decision). Hence H is, in principle, a computable characterization of the predicate `Tight(n) := [S(n)=C(n)]` grounded in strategy/poset structure rather than in a closed scalar expression over `n`.

## 4. Derivation / status of H

**Claim 4.1 (necessity, deduction).** If `S(n) ≤ C(n)`, there is a height-`C(n)` decision tree. Along any path to a node at depth `c` representing `P`, the subtree below has height `≤ C(n)-c` and `≥ e(P)` leaves, so `e(P) ≤ 2^{C(n)-c}` and every internal comparison is `h`-admissible. Therefore `W_{C(n)}(P0,0)`.

**Claim 4.2 (sufficiency, deduction).** If `W_{C(n)}(P0,0)`, the witnessing choices form a correct comparison tree of height `≤ C(n)`: feasibility is preserved downward and leaves occur exactly at total orders. Hence `S(n) ≤ C(n)`. Combined with `S(n) ≥ C(n)`, we get equality.

**Conclusion on H.** **[deduction]** H is a **correct necessary-and-sufficient characterization** of tightness, and it is structural in the sense that it quantifies over adaptive strategies and the poset-state transition system under the nodewise extension-count constraint. It is also **computable** (finite game). However, it is essentially a *reformulation* of `S(n) ≤ C(n)` as a reachability/strategy property in the admissible poset game, not a new invariant that predicts tightness from local/static structure of `n` or of individual posets without solving (or pruning) the global game.

## 5. Check against known values `n ≤ 22`

**5.1 Agreement with the table (literature-consistent).** By 4.1–4.2, H predicts `Tight(n)` iff the known `S(n)` equals `C(n)`. Using the established values:

- `n = 1–11, 20, 21`: `S(n)=C(n)` ⇒ `W_{C(n)}(P0,0)` is **true**. **[literature + deduction]**
- `n = 12`: Wells showed the information-feasible/admissible forward exploration at height `29` cannot sort `12!` (equivalently no height-29 tree exists), hence `W_{29}(P0,0)` is **false** and `S(12)=30`. **[literature]** (Wells 1966/1971; reported/confirmed in modern form by Peczarski 2012, Stober–Weiss 2023 ALENEX; see `docs/research-n12-nontightness.md` §4).
- `n = 13–19, 22`: exhaustive/pruned searches establish `S(n)=C(n)+1`, hence `W_{C(n)}(P0,0)` **false**. **[literature]** (Peczarski 2002/2012, Stober–Weiss 2023, OEIS A036604 as index; values as collated in repo docs).

Thus H is **consistent with all known `n ≤ 22`**.

**5.2 Structural content visible in known cases (consistency checks).**
- The forced-prefix phenomenon at `n=12, h=29` (only `1/2` splits admissible early, constraining the second comparison; `docs/research-n12-nontightness.md` H4/Experiment C2 logic) is a *local consequence* of `F_{12}` but, as noted there, also occurs at tight `n` (e.g. 5,10), so it cannot replace H. **[literature/deduction]**
- For `n=13`, Peczarski’s bottleneck analysis shows the failure is strategic/non-scalar inside `F_{13}`’s complement/edge behavior (poset with fewer extensions can be harder; symmetry matters locally), matching H’s “global strategy over posets” nature rather than any nodewise scalar test. **[literature]**

## 6. Attempted strengthening toward a predictive all-`n` structural criterion (and falsifications)

To go beyond the game reformulation, a predictive criterion would need a structural property `Φ(n,P,c)` (or on reachable poset classes) deciding admissibility/winnability without global search. The repo’s prior experiments and literature already falsify the main scalar/local candidates that might be hoped to yield such a `Φ`:

| Candidate strengthening | Structural form intended | Outcome vs known/checked cases | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Φ` = slack/ratio `2^{C(n)}/n!` threshold | global numeric from root | **falsified** (e.g. `n=5` tight with less slack than `n=12`; `n=20` tight, `n=22` not) | `[experiment]` `docs/research-n12-nontightness.md` H1; table above |
| `Φ` monotone in `e(P)` / function only of `e(P)` | local poset invariant | **falsified**: `m(P)` not monotone in `e(P)` (`n=6`, `e=13` harder than `e=14`) and not even a function of `e(P)` (`n=5`, two posets with `e=8` have `m=3,4`) | `[experiment]` H2; `[literature]` Peczarski `n=13` `P16/Q'16` |
| `Φ` = local information tightness `m(P)=⌈log₂ e(P)⌉` on reachable states | nodewise feasibility | **falsified** as necessary local condition for global tightness intuition: local failures at `n=5` (`e=8,m=4`) while `S(5)` tight; also not sufficient alone without global routing | `[experiment]` H3; `[literature]` Hanamura–Iwata boundary at `e=7` |
| `Φ` = forced balanced prefix / split shape alone | early strategy structure | **not predictive**: same forced early pattern at tight `n=5,10` and non-tight `n=12` | `[deduction/experiment]` H4 |
| `Φ` = symmetry/`|Aut(P)|` protects | poset symmetry | **falsified** at `n=6` (many locally loose posets with nontrivial automorphisms) | `[experiment]` H6 |
| `Φ` = decomposable/chain-merge family tight | poset class (width/series-parallel flavor) | **falsified**: e.g. `Merge(2,4)`, `M(2,4)=5>4` | `[experiment/literature]` H7 |

**Deduction.** Any structural characterization that factors through a function of `e(P)` alone, or through a fixed local split/symmetry predicate ignoring strategic routing, cannot be necessary and sufficient for `Tight(n)` over all `n` consistent with these cases. Therefore a *predictive* all-`n` characterization must be sensitive to the **global AND/OR structure of strategies over the poset transition graph** (as in H), or to a yet-unknown invariant that provably tracks that global game. **[deduction from cited results]**

## 7. Generalization attempt and concrete obstruction

**Attempted generalization (to all `n`).** H generalizes verbatim: `Tight(n) ⇔ W_{C(n)}(P0,0)` for every `n`. This is universal in form but computationally intractable at scale (state explosion; congruence-quotiented search is required in practice—cf. failed in-repo reproduction at `n=12` noted in `docs/research-n12-nontightness.md` §7, item 6).

**Concrete obstruction (to a “simple” structural predictor).**
1. **Non-scalar / non-local obstruction:** Because `m` is not a function of `e` and local bound failures do not align with global failure (`n=5` local fail but global tight; `n=12` global fail), there is no characterization of the form `Tight(n) ⇔ Ψ(n, \{e(P): P reachable\})` nor one decidable from isolated poset features without the adversarial/strategic quantification over comparisons. **[experiment/deduction]**
2. **Certified only by global search in known hard case:** For `n=12`, non-tightness is established by exhaustive admissible search with no published compact human-readable bottleneck certificate localizing a small structural witness (`docs/research-n12-nontightness.md` §§4,8,9). This is a concrete barrier to extracting from current knowledge a closed-form structural `n`-predicate. **[literature]**
3. **Irregular pattern:** `Tight(n)` holds at `1–11,20,21` and fails at `12–19,22` within `n≤22`; no monotone or simple periodic structural law in `n` is supported, and scalar slack is non-predictive. **[literature/experiment]**

**Net result.** Generalization of the *characterization principle* succeeds (H is universal) but generalization to a *tractable/predictive structural test* is obstructed by the strategic, non-monotone nature of the poset minimax game evidenced above.

## 8. Falsification of the proposed H (attempt)

- **Could H be too strong?** No: necessity follows from any height-`C(n)` tree (4.1).  
- **Could H be too weak?** No: sufficiency constructs such a tree (4.2).  
- **Empirical tension point:** if some `n` had `S(n)=C(n)` but every optimal tree necessarily visited a node with `e(P) > 2^{C(n)-c}` (impossible, since it would need `>2^{C(n)-c}` leaves under it) — ruled out by counting. No counterexample within known `n≤22`. **[deduction/literature]**

H survives falsification as an equivalence; what fails is the hope that H collapses to a simpler structural invariant.

## 9. Consequence and next decisive obstacle

- **What we can defend:** A precise structural/computable characterization of information-bound tightness is the existence of a winning strategy in the `C(n)`-budget information-feasible poset game, `Tight(n) ⇔ W_{C(n)}(P0,0)`, consistent with all known `n≤22`. This cleanly separates the *global strategic* obstruction from refuted scalar/local proxies. **[deduction + literature]**
- **What is ruled out:** Any predictor that is (i) a function of `e(P)` alone, (ii) determined by fixed local split/slack/symmetry without global strategy, or (iii) a simple scalar function of `n`/`n!`, cannot characterize tightness. **[experiment/literature]**
- **Next decisive obstacle:** Produce either (a) a compact, checkable structural certificate/witness explaining `¬W_{29}(P0,0)` for `n=12` (or any non-tight `n`) in terms of an explicit unavoidable poset-subgame property, or (b) a provable structural invariant on strategies/poset classes that implies or refutes `W_{C(n)}(P0,0)` without full enumeration. Until then, tightness for new `n` is known only via exhaustive/pruned game search. This aligns with `docs/research-plan.md` (“bound gap” / `n=12` obstruction) and the realistic frontier (structural theorems/certificates/barriers, not separations).
