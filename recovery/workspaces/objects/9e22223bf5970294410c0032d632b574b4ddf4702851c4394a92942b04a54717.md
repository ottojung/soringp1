## 1. Model fidelity

`[definition]` (per `docs/problem.md`): `S(n)` is the minimum worst-case number of adaptive pairwise comparisons to sort `n` distinct labelled keys (no ties). States are partial orders/extension sets of the antichain root; height is worst-case comparisons on any path.

`[experiment]` The script matches this model:
- `N=13`, `C=33`, `13!=6,227,020,800`; target is the Wells admissibility test for height `C(13)=33`.
- States are transitively closed strict partial orders on `{0..12}` as successor bitmasks (`one_ply_obstruction.add_relation`).
- `e(P)` is computed by exact order-ideal DP (`one_ply_obstruction.linear_extensions`), integer arithmetic.
- At depth `c` from `S_c`, children are at depth `c+1`; admissibility requires **both** outcomes `e(child) <= 2^(C-c-1) = 2^(32-c)`.
- The chosen continuation is the **heavier child** `max(e1,e2)` (tie broken by `>=` in code, irrelevant to the `e` value recorded).

This is consistent with the Wells/Peczarski/Stober–Weiss one-sided heavier-child forward level discipline as summarized in `docs/research-n12-nontightness.md` §4 `[literature context]`.

## 2. Congruence quotient

`[experiment]` `quotient_heavy_states` groups heavier children by `networkx.algorithms.isomorphism.DiGraphMatcher.is_isomorphic` on the transitive-closed successor DAG (`succ_to_dag`). Classes store representative `succ`, `e`, `count`, incomparable-pair count, and `L0=ceil_log2(e)`.

`[deduction]` Poset isomorphism preserves incomparability, extension count of outcomes under corresponding moves, both-child admissibility, and the heavier-child extension count. Therefore representatives suffice for this one-sided heavier-child level definition, and reported orbit counts/classes are up to this quotient (as stated by the script).

Assumption/caveat `[experiment]`: correctness of the quotient depends on NetworkX DiGraph isomorphism on these small 13-node transitively closed DAGs; I did not independently prove the implementation, but the method is standard and the run was deterministic (no randomization, no network).

## 5. What this does *not* establish

`[deduction]`
- **Not a proof of `S(13)>33`.** No level became empty through depth 5.
- **Not evidence for `S(13)<=33`.** A non-empty one-sided heavier-child frontier does not construct or imply a height-33 sorting strategy.
- **Not a complete refutation discipline by itself for `n>=13`.** `[literature context]` The artifact correctly notes that for `n=12` one-sided Wells exploration can empty a level, while for `n>=13` complete refutation may require second children and/or bidirectional/complete handling (cf. the literature summary in `docs/research-n12-nontightness.md`). Therefore even a future empty one-sided level would need audit and possible second-child/bidirectional treatment before claiming `S(13)>33`.
- **No complexity/separation claim.** This is a finite adaptive-comparison search experiment; it does not affect P/NP/PSPACE claims.

## 6. Audit conclusion

The model, Wells heavier-child rule, congruence quotient, and resource-cap handling are stated clearly and are consistent with the intended computation.

**Audit verdict:** computation and counts reproduced; claim boundaries are correct. The result is **inconclusive** and must not be cited as `S(13)>33` or `S(13)<=33`.
