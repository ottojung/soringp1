## 1. Question (restated, all `n`)

Fix `n` distinct labelled keys and `S(n)` the minimum worst-case adaptive comparison count (`docs/problem.md`). Consider comparison strategies/decision trees of optimal height `S(n)` (correct on every permutation). For states on **some** optimal height-`S(n)` strategy, define:

- **(d1)** number of **distinct labelled poset states** appearing as node labels (posets on the fixed label set `[n]`, equality as labelled partial orders / identical relation on `[n]`).
- **(d2)** number of **distinct unlabelled poset isomorphism classes** appearing as node labels (quotient by poset isomorphism / relabelling in `S_n`; `docs/research-compression-next.md`).
- **(d3)** the **minimum**, over all optimal height-`S(n)` strategies, of the chosen count (d1 or d2, specified).

Total node occurrences in a reduced correct comparison tree are `2*n! - 1`, but different node occurrences may share the same poset label; quotienting by isomorphism may collapse leaves.

Ask: prove any nontrivial all-`n` lower bound `> Omega(n)` or `> n!` in a meaningful sense for (d1)/(d2)/(d3), or construct an infinite family of optimal strategies with provably small (d1)/(d2)/(d3) (polynomial, `2^{O(n log n)}` with tight form, etc.)? Falsifier: a superpolynomial/larger lower bound for **every** optimal strategy's distinct quotient states, or an explicit optimal family with bounded/slow-growing distinct quotient states.

## 2. Model facts used (definition / deduction / literature context)

- **Reduced comparison tree:** internal nodes compare two keys and have two children (outcomes), leaves are total orders/permutations; correct iff every of the `n!` permutations reaches a distinct compatible leaf. A reduced correct tree has exactly `n!` leaves and, being a full binary tree, exactly `2*n! - 1` node occurrences total (`n! - 1` internal). **(definition/deduction)**.
- Existence of an optimal height-`S(n)` comparison tree is the attainment/minimax fact in the project model (`hasBudget_S`, `S_le_iff`, `S_eq_adv_univ`; `docs/problem.md`, `kernel-results.md`). **(literature/project context)**.
- Height/depth counting: a binary tree of height `h` has at most `2^d` nodes at depth `d` and at most `2^{h+1}-1` nodes total. **(deduction)**.
- Known bounds: `ceil(log2(n!)) <= S(n) <= O(n log n)` (standard/literature), with exact small values known in the literature; this report does not re-derive them (`docs/literature.md`). **(literature)**.
- Winning-frontier barrier (`docs/research-winning-frontier-barrier.md`, paper-level): for infinitely many `n`, at a common depth within budget `S(n)` there are at least `2^{(1/4-o(1)) n (log n)^2}` pairwise nonisomorphic reachable winning partial orders (surviving information/exact-optimal-budget pruning), but this is an enumeration barrier, not a time lower bound and not a claim that all appear on one optimal strategy. **(literature/paper-level context)**.

## 3. Basic deductions for (d1), (d2), (d3)

### D1. Distinct labelled states satisfy `n! <= d1(T) <= 2*n! - 1` for every correct reduced strategy `T` (deduction)

- **Lower:** each of the `n!` leaves is a labelled total order (a linear extension on `[n]`); different permutations give different labelled relations, so all `n!` leaf states are distinct labelled posets. Hence `d1(T) >= n!` for every correct strategy, in particular every optimal one.
- **Upper:** distinct labelled poset states are a subset of node occurrences/labels present in the tree, so `d1(T) <= 2*n! - 1`.
- **Meaning of `> n!`:** since `d1 >= n!` is forced by leaves, a *nontrivial* lower bound in this metric would need to exceed `n!` (e.g. `n! + g(n)` or multiplicative/superlinear-in-`n!` internal contribution). This report does **not** establish such an all-`n` excess for every optimal strategy (d3-d1 could, in principle, be close to `n!` if internal labels repeat heavily, though reachability/branching constraints may forbid that; unresolved).

### D2. Unlabelled/isomorphism quotient collapses leaves: `1 <= d2(T) <= 2*n! - 1` (deduction)

- All total orders on `n` elements are isomorphic as posets (a chain of length `n`), so all `n!` leaves lie in one unlabelled isomorphism class. Thus the trivial labelled-leaf count `n!` does **not** transfer to d2.
- The root is the antichain class and internal states contribute further classes depending on the strategy, but without a structural theorem this gives only `d2(T) >= 1` (or `>= c` from easy internal distinctions in non-degenerate cases; not formalized here as an all-`n` sharp bound). No all-`n` lower bound `> Omega(n)` or `> n!` for d2/d3-d2 is proved here.

### D3. Universal upper bound on the minimum over optimal strategies: `d3 <= 2*n! - 1 = 2^{Theta(n log n)}` (deduction)

Let `T*` be any optimal reduced comparison tree of height `S(n)` (exists by attainment). Then

`d3_{labelled} <= d1(T*) <= 2*n! - 1`, and `d3_{unlabelled} <= d2(T*) <= d1(T*) <= 2*n! - 1`.

By Stirling, `log2(n!) = n log2 n - n log2 e + O(log n)`, so `2*n! - 1 = 2^{n log2 n + O(n)} = 2^{Theta(n log n)}`. Therefore every optimal-strategy-distinct-state count (labelled or unlabelled quotient) can be made at most `2^{O(n log n)}` in node-count/exponent form by choosing any optimal reduced tree.

**Consequence / falsifier note:** any proposed all-`n` lower bound on `d3` that is asymptotically larger than this, in particular `2^{Omega(n (log n)^2)}` or any `2^{omega(n log n)}` form, is **impossible** for the "minimum over optimal strategies" quantity, because it would contradict the `2* n! - 1` upper bound realized by an optimal tree. (A `2^{Omega(n log^2 n)}` *existence* of states globally, as in the winning-frontier barrier, is consistent only for families not all realized as distinct states on one optimal tree.)

## 4. Relation to the winning-frontier barrier (deduction)

The winning-frontier theorem exhibits `2^{(1/4-o(1)) n (log n)^2}` nonisomorphic winning states at a common depth `t <= S(n)`. However, any single strategy/tree of height `S(n)` has at most `2^t <= 2^{S(n)} <= 2^{O(n log n)}` nodes at depth `t` total. Since `n (log n)^2` dominates `n log n`, for sufficiently large `n` in that family the constructed frontier cannot fit into one depth level of any optimal tree, let alone be required as distinct states on one optimal strategy.

This **separates** "many winning/quotient states exist globally" from "many must appear on some one optimal strategy" but does **not** bridge to a large lower bound for (d1)/(d2)/(d3): it gives a width/counting obstruction against embedding the whole frontier, not a forcing argument. Whether any optimal strategy must include *some* large subfamily of such states remains open here.

## 5. Small-`n` guidance only (not evidence for all-`n`)

- Small fixed `n` where `S(n)` is known can, in principle, be explored to observe reuse of labelled/unlabelled states in particular optimal trees (node occurrences vs distinct posets). I did not run exhaustive small-`n` optimal-tree enumeration or quotient counting here; any such experiment would be guidance only and would not establish an all-`n` bound.
- Literature knows exact `S(n)` for `n <= 22` with substantial computational effort near the top of that range (`docs/literature.md`), so complete distinct-quotient accounting over all optimal strategies is nontrivial even for moderate `n`.

## 6. Assessment

- **Proved here (deduction):** `d1(T) >= n!`; `d1(T), d2(T) <= 2*n! - 1`; hence `d3 <= 2*n! - 1 = 2^{Theta(n log n)}` for both labelled and unlabelled distinct-state counts. This refutes any super-`2^{Theta(n log n)}` (e.g. `2^{Omega(n log^2 n)}`) all-`n` lower bound on the minimum-over-optimal-strategies distinct count, while being consistent with the known large winning-frontier *existence* result.
- **Not proved/refuted here:** a nontrivial all-`n` lower bound strictly above the forced leaf level in a quotient-sensitive sense (e.g. `d3_{unlabelled} >= n^{1+epsilon}` or any superpolynomial-in-`n` but `<= 2^{O(n log n)}` bound), or a nontrivial all-`n` excess `d3_{labelled} - n!` lower bound; and construction of an infinite family of optimal strategies with provably small distinct quotient states (polynomial, or `2^{c n log n}` with explicit optimality). Optimality of explicit infinite families is the main obstacle.
- **Genuine new finding:** **no theorem claimed**. The observations are immediate counting consequences of the decision-tree model plus the existing frontier/optplay context; they clarify the question/falsifier but do not settle the substantive open direction (tight lower bound for d3 vs small optimal quotient family).
