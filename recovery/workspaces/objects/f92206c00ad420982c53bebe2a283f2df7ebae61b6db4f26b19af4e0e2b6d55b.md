## 2. Question

Must every uniform deterministic algorithm that obtains exact height `S(n)` by local minimax choices consult a linear-extension counting oracle (compute `e(P)` exactly, or to a precision that determines the minimax value), or can a polynomial-time surrogate suffice?

Equivalently: is exact linear-extension counting (`#P`-hard in general, Brightwell–Winkler 1991) an unavoidable subproblem of exact optimal sorting?

This front asks necessity, not sufficiency. The earlier "computable-entropy-balance-surrogates" front already showed that even exact `e(P)` does **not** determine `F(P)` (P1/P2 both `e=8` but `F=3` vs `4`; `Merge(2,4)` has `e=15`, info bound `4`, `F=5`).

   - Poset model: transitive-closure successor bitmasks, exactly as in the repo scripts.
   - Cheap invariants tested (all polynomial-time computable, no `e(P)`):
     width, poset height (longest chain), `#min`, `#max`, number of comparable pairs, number of connected components of the comparability graph, series (ordinal-sum) component count, and automorphism-group size.
   - A "collision" is a pair of posets with identical cheap-invariant tuple but different `e(P)` **and** different `F(P)` (minimax remaining comparisons). 3. Tested on the families named in the front:
   - Ordinal sums (series): `F` is additive, `e` is multiplicative; rounding of `ceil(log2)` before multiplying can make the structural bound strictly stronger than the global information bound (witness `A=Merge(2,1)` ⊕ `B` with `e=15`, info `4`, block-local bound `5`, `F=5`).

   - W-gadget family: no "W-gadget" family is defined in the repository or its notes; not separately tested. The general exhaustive poset test subsumes any fixed-shape family on `≤6` elements.
4. Ran `n=3,4,5,6` exhaustive (6: 130023 posets).

## 5. Necessity argument (finite, not a theorem)

The collision pairs are states reachable from the antichain root (any finite poset can be built by adding comparabilities), so they can occur as subproblems during exact sorting of `n` keys.

If a uniform algorithm evaluated states using only the cheap invariants, it would assign the same value to two posets with identical cheap invariants but different `F`, and therefore could not be exact. Hence exact evaluation must refine beyond width, height, ordinal/series decomposition, component counts, and automorphism orbits. The canonical such refinement is `e(P)`, and in every collision above `e(P)` differs between the two posets, so computing `e(P)` (at least to the precision that distinguishes them) is unavoidable for those states.

This is necessity of an `e`-computation step, not sufficiency: `e(P)` alone still does not determine `F(P)` (P1/P2, `e=8`, `F=3` vs `4`). So an exact algorithm needs `e(P)` *plus* further structural information; but it cannot avoid `e(P)` entirely.

## 6. Surrogate algorithm sketch (if one insisted on a polynomial-time route)

No such sketch is supported by the evidence. The natural polynomial-time surrogates (width, height, #min/#max, components, series decomposition, automorphism size) all collide. The only known polynomial-time algorithms in the literature (Cardinal et al. 2013) compute constant-factor approximations of `log e(P)` and do not decide exact `S(P)≤k`. Therefore an exact polynomial-time evaluator would require a new invariant not in the tested set; the collision test gives no candidate.

## 7. Honest limits

- Finite evidence only (`n≤6`). This is an obstruction, not a proof that no polynomial-time surrogate exists for all `n`; an untested invariant could in principle distinguish the collision pairs without computing `e`.
- The test does not prove that `e(P)` itself must be computed exactly (as opposed to a cheaper refinement that happens to separate these particular posets); it shows only that the natural cheap invariants are insufficient.
- `#P`-hardness of `e(P)` (Brightwell–Winkler) does not by itself make `e` unavoidable for the antichain family `S(n)`: the antichain has `e=n!` trivially, yet `S(n)` is not known to be polynomial-time computable. The obstruction here is the collision of cheap invariants, not counting hardness.
