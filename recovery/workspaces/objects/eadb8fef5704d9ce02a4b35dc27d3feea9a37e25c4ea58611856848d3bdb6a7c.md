## A rigorous reduction: WL for all posets ⇔ the strong property on "m-saturated" posets

Call a finite poset `Q` **m-saturated** if adding *any* new comparability lowers
its minimax value: for every incomparable `(x,y)`,
`m(Q^{x<y}) <= m(Q)-1` and `m(Q^{y<x}) <= m(Q)-1`.

**Lemma 1.** In an m-saturated `Q`, every incomparable pair is an *optimal*
comparison, i.e. `1 + max(m(Q^{x<y}), m(Q^{y<x})) = m(Q)`.

*Proof.* The two child depths are `<= m-1`, so comparing `(x,y)` first gives a
strategy of height `<= m`. Since `m` is the minimum over all first comparisons,
`m <= 1 + max(...)`; equality follows. ∎

**Lemma 2 (maximal extension).** For every finite `P` there is a finite `Q`
on the same ground set with `P ⊆ Q` (as relation sets), `m(Q) = m(P)`, and `Q`
m-saturated. Take `Q` maximal among `{R : P ⊆ R, m(R) = m(P)}` (finite set).
Any proper one-relation extension of `Q` has smaller `m`, which is Lemma 1's
hypothesis.

**Theorem 3 (reduction).** `(WL)` holds for every finite poset iff every
m-saturated finite poset `Q` with `m(Q) >= 2` has an incomparable pair `(x,y)`
with both children of depth `>= m(Q)-2`. For m-saturated `Q`, by Lemma 1 this
is exactly the **strong** property (an *optimal* comparison with child depths
`{m-1, >= m-2}`).

*Proof.* The forward direction is trivial. Conversely let `P` be arbitrary,
`m = m(P) >= 2`, and `Q ⊇ P` the maximal extension from Lemma 2 (`m(Q)=m`).
Take the assumed witness `(x,y)` in `Q`. It is incomparable in `Q`, hence also
in `P`; and `P^{x<y} ⊆ Q^{x<y}`, so monotonicity of `m` gives
`m(P^{x<y}) >= m(Q^{x<y}) >= m-2`, and symmetrically. Thus `(x,y)` witnesses
(WL) for `P`. ∎

**Consequence.** A *minimal* counterexample to (WL) (least `m`) may be taken
m-saturated; on it weak and strong coincide. Hence the finite search's two
columns ("strong" and "weak") are not redundant in principle: the strong
column is the one that would have to fail first, and it is the right target for
an all-`n` proof. This is new relative to `notes/weaker-lemma.md`.

## Why the recorded induction still stops one level short (sharpened)

Let `Q` be an m-saturated counterexample of least `m`. By Lemma 1 every
comparison is optimal, so by counterexample assumption **every** comparison has
child depths `{m-1, s}` with `s <= m-3`. Pick one with deep child
`D = Q^{a<b}` (`m(D)=m-1`) and saturate `D` to `D*` with `m(D*)=m-1`. By
minimality `D*` satisfies (WL), so it has a comparison `(c,d)` with both
`D*`-children of depth `>= m(D*)-2 = m-3`. Since `D* ⊇ Q`, monotonicity gives
`m(Q^{c<d}) >= m(D*^{c<d}) >= m-3`, and symmetrically. This is exactly the
level `m-3` — one short of the required `m-2`. To improve the constant one
would need both `D*`-children `>= m(D*)-1`, which is false already for the
3-element poset `a<b, c isolated` (record in `notes/weaker-lemma.md`). So:
**every argument of the shape "saturate → induct at depth m-1 → push back by
monotonicity" provably stops at `m-3`; the missing ingredient is non-local.**
This is a precise barrier for the recorded method, not a proof of (WL).

**Corollary 4 (exact gap; chain of tight posets).** Let `Q` be a counterexample
of least `m`, taken m-saturated by Theorem 3, so `f(Q) <= m-3`. Pick any
comparison `(a,b)`; by Lemma 1 its deep child `D = Q^{a<b}` has depth `m-1`.
Let `D*` be a maximal same-depth extension of `D`, so `D*` is m-saturated with
`m(D*) = m-1`. By minimality of `m`, `D*` satisfies (WL), so it has a comparison
whose two `D*`-children have depth `>= m-3`; since `D* ⊇ Q`, pushing that pair
back to `Q` gives a comparison of `Q` with both children `>= m-3`. Hence

    f(Q) = m-3   exactly.

Moreover `D*` must itself be **tight**: `f(D*) = m-3 = m(D*)-2`, for if
`f(D*) >= m-2 = m(D*)-1` then `D*` would have a balanced optimal comparison
whose push witnesses (WL) in `Q`. Thus a minimal counterexample would force a
chain of tight posets at depths `m, m-1, ..., 2` — precisely the family that is
abundant in the finite scans (tight counts above). This is why the conclusion
"finite evidence through n=8" cannot settle the question: the supporting tight
chain exists at every level; only the *last* lifting step is missing.

## A small unconditional case (proved here)

(WL) holds for every `P` with `m(P) <= 3`. For `m=2` the threshold is `0`,
trivial. For `m=3`, if every comparison had a depth-`0` (total-order) child,
then for some incomparable pair `(a,b)` adding just `a<b` would make `P` total,
so `P` would have exactly one incomparable pair and `m(P)=1`, contradiction.
Hence some comparison has both children of depth `>= 1 = m-2`. (This is not new
relative to the finite scans, but it is proved, not enumerated.)
