# ESO/NP-style certificates and obstructions for `SORT-BOUND-U` yes-instances

**Status (2026-11-05, billy-dev / SoringP1 #301):** research note; not Lean-checked. This note analyzes the *form* of possible short existential certificates for yes-instances of `SORT-BOUND-U` and the corresponding verification/obstruction picture, especially at exact height near the information bound. It makes **no complexity-class separation claims** (in particular it does not claim `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ NP`, `∉ NP`, `NP = PSPACE`, or `NP ≠ PSPACE`). Items are labelled `[definition]`, `[literature]`, `[deduction]`, `[experiment]` (only where citing prior repo experiments), `[paper-level]`, `[conjecture]`, or `[open]`.

**Requested context read:** `AGENTS.md`, `docs/problem.md`, `docs/research-plan.md`, `docs/research-certificates-barriers.md`, `docs/research-n12-nontightness.md`, `docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md`, `docs/research-strategy-certificates.md`, `docs/literature.md`. `docs/exact-s12-fo-tc-nonframed.md` is **not present** in this checkout.

## 1. Model and the exact yes-instance question

`[definition]` For `n ≥ 0`, `S(n)` is the minimum worst-case height of a deterministic adaptive comparison decision tree that sorts every permutation of `n` distinct labelled keys (`docs/problem.md`).

`[definition]` The active unary threshold language is

> `SORT-BOUND-U = { 1^n # 1^k : S(n) ≤ k }`.

Its actual input length is `N = n + k + 1`. A *yes-instance* means the sorter has a strategy of height at most `k` against an **adaptive adversary** choosing comparison outcomes consistently with some input permutation.

`[deduction]` Membership `1^n#1^k ∈ SORT-BOUND-U` is equivalent to a win in the finite comparison game `G(n,k)`: from the antichain, the sorter picks incomparable pairs, the adversary answers `<` or `>`, transitive closure is taken, and the sorter wins iff a total order is reached within `k` comparisons (`docs/research-certificates-barriers.md` Claim A). Thus a yes-certificate must justify an existential choice (a sorter strategy / policy) whose correctness is universal over all adversary answers / all `n!` inputs.

## 2. Falsifiable question

**Question.** For `SORT-BOUND-U` yes-instances, especially the hard root case `n=12, k=30` where `C(12)=⌈log₂ 12!⌉=29 < S(12)=30`, is there a *sound* existential certificate format with an *explicit finite verifier* that is polynomial-size in the unary input length `N` (or, more sharply, polynomial in `n` for informative `k = Θ(S(n)) = Θ(n log n)`)? If not, what is the precise obstruction: the universal adversary quantifier, the need to certify disjunctive/merged knowledge, invariant inadequacy at exact height, or superpolynomially many reachable/winning states?

**Consequence if positive (all-n, exact height).** A uniform family of such certificates verifiable in deterministic polynomial time would give a standard NP-style/ESO-style upper-bound witness for `SORT-BOUND-U` yes-instances. This note does **not** assert that consequence is achieved.

**Consequence if negative for a proposed format.** A refutation of a *specific* certificate format (by a counterexample, a completeness gap, or a superpolynomial-size lower bound for that format) rules out that route to succinct upper-bound certificates, but it is **not** a proof that `SORT-BOUND-U ∉ NP`.

## 3. What an ESO/NP-style certificate must certify

`[deduction]` A sound existential certificate for `S(n) ≤ k` has the shape

> ∃W : V(1^n#1^k, W) accepts,

where `V` is an explicit finite deterministic verifier. For NP membership one additionally needs `|W| ≤ poly(N)` and `V` runs in `poly(N)` time. The *semantic* content of `W` must establish

> for every adversary strategy / every input permutation π, the policy encoded by `W` reaches a total order in ≤ k comparisons.

So the quantifier pattern is `∃ strategy · ∀ adversary/inputs`. An NP/ESO-style upper bound requires compressing the universal side into a checkable object; it does **not** allow the verifier to replay an explicit `n!`-leaf tree except at exponential cost.

`[deduction]` Equivalently, over the `2^{O(n²)}` finite states of the poset game, `W` can be read as a finite second-order witness (a strategy relation, a ranked DAG, or annotated states) whose local conditions imply the global minimax win. This is the sense in which the certificate is "ESO/NP-style, but with an explicit finite verifier": the existential object is finite and explicit, and the verifier checks local constraints rather than performing unbounded search.

## 5. Lower-bound / verification obstructions

### 5.1 The universal adversary quantifier

`[deduction]` A yes-certificate for `S(n) ≤ k` cannot merely exhibit one good comparison path; it must cover **both** outcomes of every chosen comparison, recursively, up to depth `k`. In explicit form this is the full decision tree. In compressed form it is a strategy DAG/branching program/policy relation. Therefore every proposed short certificate must contain enough structure to verify that **no adversary branch** exceeds the budget.

`[literature, deduction]` The same universal-vs-existential asymmetry appears in the opposite direction for fixed sorting networks: verifying that an arbitrary comparator network sorts all inputs is coNP-complete (Parberry), while adaptive sorting strategies choose comparisons existentially. These are different models and neither gives a hardness theorem for `SORT-BOUND-U`; the point is only that "all inputs/adversary answers" is the expensive quantifier that any succinct certificate must tame (`research-certificates-barriers.md` Claim D).

### 5.2 Disjunctive knowledge and join soundness/completeness

`[paper-level]` Framed certificates may merge control states by intersecting guaranteed relations. Intersection is **sound** (it never asserts unjustified knowledge) but can be **incomplete** for a particular proof: it may discard disjunctive correlations needed later. `research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` reports that an optimal five-comparison network for four elements is a counterexample to naive immediate joining of an arbitrary correct comparator network. Therefore a proposed short existential format must either (a) avoid lossy joins, (b) carry enough auxiliary/disjunctive information through joins, or (c) prove that lossy joins are never needed for *some* optimal strategy at height `S(n)`. Option (c) is exactly the open completeness question.

### 5.3 Invariant inadequacy at exact height

`[literature]` The information-theoretic invariant `e(P) ≤ 2^(k-c)` (linear-extension/leaf-count bound) is necessary nodewise but not sufficient globally: `S(12)=30 > 29 = C(12)` (Wells), and the known lower-bound proofs are exhaustive searches (`docs/literature.md`, `docs/research-n12-nontightness.md`, Stober–Weiß).

`[deduction, experiment]` `research-n12-nontightness.md` shows the obstruction is not captured by simple scalar functions of a poset: the efficiency pruning is equivalent to node-level leaf counting; sortability is not monotone in `e(P)` (smallest witness at `n=6`, `e=13,m=5` harder than `e=14,m=4`; also the same `e` can give different `m` at `n=5`); a forced balanced prefix occurs at both tight and non-tight `n`. Thus any short lower-bound witness or exact-height yes/no separator stronger than replaying strategic search would need a genuinely new invariant/certificate object.

`[open]` No polynomial-size, checkable invariant is known that certifies `S(n) > k` in the hard regime `k ≥ C(n)`, e.g. `n=12,k=29`; and no polynomial-size invariant is known that certifies `S(n) ≤ k` at exact or near-exact height except by encoding an explicit winning strategy.

### 5.4 Superpolynomial disjunctive / state knowledge

`[experiment, open]` The all-optimal labelled-poset DAG has `65,551` nodes already at `n=6` (state count within optimal play, not the full `130,023` labelled posets), and `S_n` quotienting reduces but does not settle the asymptotic count (`134` quotient nodes at `n=6`, up from `19` at `n=5`). These data are *consistent with* superpolynomial growth but too small for an asymptotic lower bound (`research-strategy-certificates.md`).

`[literature, deduction]` The full labelled-poset space has size `2^{Θ(n²)}` (A001035 / Kleitman–Rothschild), and memoized DP over all posets gives `2^{O(n²)}` numeric time. The winning-frontier barrier in `research-winning-frontier-barrier.md` rules out full winning-frontier enumeration at exact budgets; a short certificate, if it exists, must avoid enumerating transcripts, winning states, or all reachable posets.

`[paper-level]` Conversely, exponentially many nonisomorphic histories do not by themselves force a large forgetful DAG: `research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` gives a generic insertion/history example where many isomorphism types compress to a small information loss. So "many states exist" is necessary context but not a proof of superpolynomial certificate size for the right compression.

## 6. The hard root case `S(12)=30 > C(12)=29`

`[literature]` Wells proved `S(12)=30` (information lower bound `29`, upper bound from Ford–Johnson/merge insertion is `30`). Later exhaustive-search methods (Peczarski; Stober–Weiß) confirm and extend exact values; for `n=12`, Stober–Weiß report a `no` answer for sortability in `29` comparisons by pruned bidirectional search. These proofs certify the universal adversary side by exhaustive/structured search, not by publishing a short independent invariant (`docs/literature.md`, `docs/research-n12-nontightness.md`).

`[deduction]` This case is a stress test for succinct yes-certificates at `k=30` and succinct no-certificates/witnesses at `k=29`:
- At `k=29` (no-instance for the root), a short witness would need to defeat every height-29 sorter strategy or supply a stronger-than-information invariant; none is known.
- At `k=30` (yes-instance), a short witness would need to encode a complete height-30 winning sorter strategy against all adversary outcomes. The gap of exactly one comparison above `C(12)` means the certificate cannot rely only on leaf counting; it must capture strategic routing where the information bound is locally tight but globally insufficient at height 29.

`[open]` A human-readable, short, independently checkable certificate for `S(12)>29` is not known in the sources surveyed here. Likewise, an explicit polynomial-size framed/ESO-style certificate for the yes-instance `S(12) ≤ 30` is not established here.

## 7. Assessment

`[deduction]` **Sound short existential format (general, all-`n`, exact height): open.** Known sound and explicitly checkable formats are either exponential in the relevant size measure (explicit trees, naive single-policy labelled DAGs), not proved complete at exact `S(n)` (framed/joined DAGs), or proved only for non-optimal upper bounds (merge/FJ-style constructions pending full optimality/audit claims where relevant).

`[deduction]` **Verifier requirement is clear; witness existence is not.** The verifier side is not mysterious: local checks of poset transitions, permutations, joins/intersections, totals, acyclicity, and depth are polynomial in the explicit certificate. The unresolved part is whether the universal adversary quantifier can always be compressed into polynomially many such local checks at exact height.

`[deduction]` **Precise obstruction, not a separation.** The main obstruction to an NP/ESO-style certificate is the combination of
1. universal adversary coverage over all comparison outcomes/inputs;
2. possible need to preserve disjunctive knowledge across merges/joins, while lossy joins may be unsound as proofs for arbitrary strategies;
3. inadequacy of known scalar invariants (`e(P)`, efficiency, slack, symmetry heuristics) at exact height, exemplified by `S(12)=30>C(12)=29`;
4. potentially superpolynomial optimal-play state/DAG complexity, with no proof either way for the best compressed format.

This is an obstruction to currently known and naively proposed succinct-certificate routes. It must **not** be read as a proof that `SORT-BOUND-U` is outside NP, because `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ PSPACE` is already known and an NP lower bound for it would imply `NP ≠ PSPACE`.

## 8. Falsifiable next checks

1. **Completeness counterexample for framed certificates:** find an `n` and an optimal height-`S(n)` strategy such that every sound framed/join certificate of that strategy (or of any optimal strategy) requires superpolynomial size, or show a concrete strategy that no framed verifier accepts at height `S(n)`. Either outcome must include an explicit size/proof argument.
2. **Constructive exact-height witness:** produce an explicit, machine-checkable framed/quotient certificate for a nontrivial exact case beyond tiny `n` (ideally including or illuminating `n=12,k=30`) with verified soundness and recorded size.
3. **Join/disjunction normal form:** prove that every optimal strategy admits a polynomially bounded sound join discipline (or exhibit the first forced superpolynomial disjunction). This targets obstruction 5.2 directly.
4. **Exact-height invariant test:** propose a candidate invariant stronger than `e(P)` and test it against the known non-monotone/same-`e` witnesses and the `n=12` boundary; reject it if it fails local soundness or fails to separate `k=29` from `k=30`.

## 9. Bottom line

For `SORT-BOUND-U` yes-instances, there is **no known** sound, explicit, polynomial-size existential certificate format that is complete at exact height for all `n`; explicit trees are exponential, and framed/quotient DAGs are sound/checkable in principle but have unresolved exact-height completeness and size. The hard `S(12)=30>C(12)=29` case illustrates why: the universal adversary must be strategically defeated at a height where the information invariant is insufficient, and known proofs of that fact are exhaustive searches rather than short certificates. This is a precise certificate/verification obstruction, **not** a complexity separation.
