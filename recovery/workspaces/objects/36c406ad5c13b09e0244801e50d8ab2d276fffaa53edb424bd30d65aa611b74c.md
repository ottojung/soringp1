# Weaker lemma needed by the threshold induction — attempt and precise obstruction

**Status:** no all-`n` proof obtained. Finite check `n<=7` (and the bounded `n=8`
run, see `n8-result.md`) finds no counterexample. Below is a rigorous account of
what the natural methods do prove, and exactly where they stop.

**Statement used.** `research-core-threshold.md` is not present in this bound
cwd; I use the form given in the task dispatch:

> (WL) Every finite poset `P` with `m(P) >= 2` has **some** incomparable pair
> `(a,b)` (not necessarily optimal) with
> `m(P^{a<b}) >= m(P)-2` and `m(P^{b<a}) >= m(P)-2`.

Write `m = m(P)`, `f(P) = max over incomparable pairs of min(child depths)`.
Then (WL) is `f(P) >= m-2`. The strong property tested by `oracle*.py` implies
(WL) but is not implied by it.

## 1. What is easy and what the push-to-root method proves

**Proposition 1 (push-to-root, limit `m-3`).** Let `P` have `m >= 2`, let `(a,b)`
be an **optimal** first comparison, `D = P^{a<b}` (so `m(D) = m-1`) and
`S = P^{b<a}` (depth `s <= m-1`). Let `(c,d)` be an **optimal** first
comparison of `D` (the root of an optimal strategy tree for `D`; hence legal in
`P`, and `max(m(D1),m(D2)) = m-2`). Put `D1 = D^{c<d}`, `D2 = D^{d<c}`. In the
unbalanced case `s <= m-3`, `(c,d)` is **optimal** for `P`, and

    m(P^{c<d}) >= m(D1),   m(P^{d<c}) >= m(D2),
    max(m(P^{c<d}), m(P^{d<c})) = m-1.

*Proof.* Legality: `c,d` incomparable in `D` implies incomparable in `P`.
Lower bounds: `P^{c<d}` extends `D1` by all relations of `P` not in `D`, so
monotonicity (`P ⊆ Q ⇒ m(Q) <= m(P)`) gives `m(P^{c<d}) >= m(D1)`; symmetric.
Upper bounds: from `P^{c<d}`, compare `(a,b)`. The `a<b` branch lands in `D1`
(depth `<= m-2`, since `(c,d)` is optimal in `D`), the `b<a` branch lands in
`S^{c<d}` (depth `<= s`). Hence `m(P^{c<d}) <= 1 + max(m-2, s)`; symmetrically
`m(P^{d<c}) <= 1 + max(m-2, s)` (using `m(D2) <= m-2`). In the unbalanced case
`s <= m-3` both are `<= m-1`; combined with the universal
`max(child depths) >= m-1`, equality follows. If the split is balanced
(`s >= m-2`) (WL) already holds. ∎

**Corollary 2.** Combining Proposition 1 with an optimal comparison of `D`:
there is always an optimal comparison of `P` with better child `>= m-3`.
This is the "one step short" result already recorded in `proof-attempt.md`.

## 2. Why the obvious induction cannot close the gap

The hope was: apply (WL) inductively to the deep child `D` (`m(D) = m-1`),
obtain a comparison of `D` with both children `>= m(D)-2 = m-3`, push it to
`P`, and conclude `f(P) >= m-2`.

This fails by exactly one level:

* (WL) at level `m-1` yields children of `D` of depth `>= m-3`.
* After pushing, the children of `P` still only have lower bound `m-3`
  (Proposition 1 gives lower bounds, not equality), i.e. `f(P) >= m-3`,
  *not* `m-2`.

So the map `P ↦ D ↦ P` sends threshold `2` at level `m-1` to threshold `3` at
level `m`; induction does not improve the constant. This is not an artefact of
a weak estimate: it is forced, because the needed stronger auxiliary statement
is **false**:

**Counterexample to the threshold-1 strengthening.** Let `Q` be the poset on
`{a,b,c}` with the single relation `a < b` and `c` isolated. Then `m(Q) = 2`,
yet for both incomparable pairs the two child depths are `{0,1}`:

    (c,a):  c<a -> total (depth 0);  a<c -> a<b, a<c  (depth 1)
    (c,b):  c<b -> a<b, c<b  (depth 1);  b<c -> total (depth 0)

Hence no comparison of `Q` has both children `>= m(Q)-1 = 1`. So one cannot
strengthen the induction hypothesis to "both children `>= m-1`" and hope to
push down a stronger bound. Any proof of (WL) must use a genuinely different
move (or an additional argument about the shallow child `S`, below).

## 3. The exact missing ingredient

Assume `P` is an extremal case: `f(P) = m-3`, i.e. (WL) just fails. Let `(a,b)`
be optimal with children `D` (depth `m-1`) and `S` (depth `s <= m-3`). Let
`(c,d)` be a comparison of `D` with `D2 = D^{d<c}` of depth `<= m-3` (such a
comparison must exist whenever (WL) fails, by §2). Push it to `P`.

Write `p1 = m(P^{c<d})`, `p2 = m(P^{d<c})`. Then

    p2 >= m(D2),                       (Proposition 1)
    p2 <= 1 + max(m(D2), m(S^{d<c})) <= 1 + (m-3) = m-2,

because after `d<c` the `(a,b)` split sends `a<b` to `D2` and `b<a` to
`S^{d<c}` (adding a relation to `S` cannot increase its depth). So
`p2 ∈ {m-3, m-2}`. If `p2 = m-2` then, since `max(p1,p2) = m-1`, we get
`p1 = m-1`, i.e. `(c,d)` is a **balanced optimal** comparison of `P` and (WL)
holds. Therefore an extremal counterexample must satisfy the very specific
condition:

> for every optimal root `(a,b)` with deep child `D` and shallow child `S`,
> and every comparison `(c,d)` of `D` whose `d<c` branch has depth `<= m-3`,
> the push satisfies `m(P^{d<c}) <= m-3` — the interaction with `S` never
> supplies the missing comparison.

Equivalently ( contrapositive ), (WL) would follow if one could show that for
some such `(c,d)` the combined poset `P^{d<c}` needs `m-2` comparisons even
though each of its `(a,b)`-branches needs only `<= m-3`. Nothing in the
minimax recurrence forces this; it is the precise gap. Note also that the
extremal case is *consistent* at every level of the recursion (there is no
infinite descent): applying the argument again to the new deep child just
reproduces an optimal comparison with depths `{m-3, m-1}`.

## 4. Finite evidence

The exact oracle (`scripts/oracle_n8.py`, memo key `(n, canonical form)`,
validated against `scripts/oracle3.py` and the known counts `1,2,5,16,63,318,
2045,16999` and `S(n)`) checked both the strong property and (WL) for every
isomorphism class:

* `n <= 7` (2450 classes): **no counterexample to (WL)**.
* `n = 8` (16999 classes): see `notes/n8-result.md`.

A concrete illustration that unbalanced optimal comparisons exist but do not
violate (WL): the poset `2+2` (two disjoint 2-chains `x<a`, `b<y`) has `m = 3`.
Comparing the top of one chain with the bottom of the other (`a` vs `b`) is
optimal with depths `{0,2}`, but comparing the two minima (`x` vs `b`) is
optimal with depths `{2,2}` — the push-to-root of §1 recovers exactly this
balanced comparison.

## 5. Honest conclusion

No all-`n` proof of (WL) was found. The obstruction is sharp: the only
general-purpose tool (induct on the deep child, push a comparison back to the
root) loses one level, the natural strengthening that would repair it is
false for an explicit 3-element poset, and closing the gap requires a
non-recursive argument about how the shallow child interacts with the deep
child (§3). A finite check, even `n=8`, is not an all-`n` theorem.
