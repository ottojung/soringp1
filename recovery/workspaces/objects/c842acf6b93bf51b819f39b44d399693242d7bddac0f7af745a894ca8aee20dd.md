# exec-report: optimal-viable state counting

**Worktree:** `soring301-exec-optimal-viable-state-counting-20261117`, HEAD `6a96398`, clean tree.
**Question (all n):** Separate, on the n-antichain root, the four state sets

- (a) budget-root-reachable posets,
- (b) information-admissible posets (`e(P) <= 2^{k-d}` etc.),
- (c) locally viable posets (`d(P)+F(P) <= k`),
- (d) posets lying on **some** height-`S(n)` optimal strategy.

Can an all-n superpolynomial lower bound or polynomial upper bound for (d) be proved with a controlled family? In particular, are there states satisfying (a)-(c) but not (d), or is every (c)-viable state (d)-optimal-reachable?

**Result (short):** Criteria (a)-(c) do **not** imply (d). Explicit failures already occur at `n=5` (`700` labelled / `10` unlabelled states) and grow to `7500` / `23` at `n=6`. Thus "locally viable" is not "optimal-play reachable." No polynomial upper bound for (d) is proved (and the `n!` leaves already make the labelled count superpolynomial). No stronger all-n superpolynomial lower bound for (d) than the trivial leaf count `n!` is obtained here; the exact obstruction is the missing optimal-move/ancestor condition (see §4). No Lean, repository, or PR changes.

## 1. Definitions used

State = transitively closed labelled poset `P` on `[n]`; `F(P)` exact minimax value (`F(empty)=S(n)`); `e(P)` = number of linear extensions; `d(P)` = minimum number of comparisons (transcript length) needed to reach `P` from the antichain (BFS distance in the poset lattice; this is the most permissive depth, so counts below are upper bounds for sets (a)-(c)). Budget `k=S(n)`.

- (a) budget-root-reachable: `d(P) <= S(n)`.
- (b) information-admissible: (a) and `e(P) <= 2^{S(n)-d(P)}`.
- (c) locally viable: (a) and `d(P)+F(P) <= S(n)`.
- (d) optimal-play: `P` occurs in the all-optimal DAG (union of all optimal height-`S(n)` strategies), i.e. reachable from the root by a chain of comparisons each satisfying `F(Q)=1+max(F(Q+xy),F(Q+yx))`.

Independent checks: exact `F(P)` via the memoized minimax recurrence (`scripts/evaluate.py` / `strategy_dag_probe.minimax_values`); exact `e(P)` via subset DP over downsets; `d(P)` via BFS over all legal comparison outcomes; optimal-play membership via DFS keeping all optimal moves. Stober-Weiss 2023 coverage (efficiency threshold `E_thr`) consulted for the distinction between "winning within budget" and "covered by an optimal play."

## 3. What this establishes

1. **Explicit failure of (a)-(c).** At `n=5`, `700` labelled states (`10` unlabelled) satisfy budget-root-reachability, information admissibility, and local viability, yet are **not** on any height-`S(5)=7` optimal strategy. At `n=6` the failure grows to `7500` (`23`). Therefore:
   - (a), (b), (c) are **not** sufficient for (d);
   - the conjecture "every (c)-viable state is (d)-optimal-reachable" is **false**;
   - the `2^{Omega(n log^2 n)}` winning-frontier family is a family of (a)-(c) states, but its membership in (d) is not established by winningness alone.

2. **Lower bound for (d).** The leaves of any optimal strategy are the `n!` total orders, all distinct labelled posets, so `|(d)| >= n! = 2^{Theta(n log n)}`. Hence (d) is **not** polynomially bounded in the labelled setting, and no polynomial-size labelled DAG certificate is possible on leaf count alone. In the quotient, leaves collapse to one class, so this trivial lower bound disappears; the quotient counts (`1,2,5,12,19,134,...`) are the nontrivial quantity.

3. **No all-n superpolynomial lower bound obtained for the nontrivial part of (d).** The winning-frontier construction (`2^{Omega(n log^2 n)}` states) does **not** by itself lower-bound (d), because its states are "winning" (`F(P) <= k-d`) rather than "optimal-move reachable." Converting it to (d) would require showing its installed comparisons can be ordered as globally optimal moves at every ancestor, which is not established. Conversely, no polynomial upper bound for the quotient (d) is proved.

## 4. Exact obstruction

Local viability `d(P)+F(P) <= k` is a **single-state** condition. Optimal-play reachability is a **path** condition: every comparison on the root-to-`P` path must be an optimal move in its state, i.e.

```
F(Q) = 1 + max(F(Q+x<y), F(Q+y<x))
```

for each ancestor `Q`, with `P` as one of the two children. A state can have small `F(P)` and large remaining budget slack while having been reached through a comparison that is not minimax-optimal at an ancestor; then it is winning within budget but absent from every optimal tree. The computed failures are exactly such states: they pass the budget/information/local-viability filters but fail the ancestor-optimality filter.

Stober-Weiss 2023's coverage guarantee (§4.2) is the analogous precise statement for backward tables: coverage holds only for states with efficiency below `E_thr` **and** on optimal forward paths; outside the covered region, absence proves nothing. Our (b) filter is the simpler information cutoff, so it over-approximates (d) even more.

## 5. Complexity implication

- A superpolynomial lower bound for (d) would be a state-space barrier for certificate-complete methods; the trivial `n!` leaf bound gives only `2^{Theta(n log n)}`, below the `2^{Theta(n log^2 n)}` winning-frontier scale.
- A polynomial upper bound for (d) (quotient) would support succinct strategy descriptions; none is proved, and the quotient counts are consistent with (but do not prove) superpolynomial growth.
- Neither outcome is obtained here. The decisive missing piece is a construction/proof connecting ancestor-optimal move sequences to a controlled family of states (or a proof that every (c)-viable state is ancestor-optimal, which is refuted above).
