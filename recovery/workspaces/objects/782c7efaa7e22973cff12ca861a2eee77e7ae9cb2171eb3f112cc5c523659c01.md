**Result type:** decisive falsification of the attempt to turn the average-case entropy balance into a sound deterministic worst-case pruning/move-selection rule using only locally computable aggregate potentials.

## 1. Question

For finite posets P arising in optimal sorting, can the expected-path entropy balance

  E[sum_i (1 - h_2(p_i))] = E[depth] - log_2(n!)

be turned into a sound deterministic worst-case pruning rule using a locally computable potential Phi(P)? Equivalently, is there a locally computable Phi(P) such that, for every non-total P and every incomparable pair {x,y}, if {x,y} is "balanced enough" according to Phi(P), then

  1 + max(F(P+x<y), F(P+y<x)) <= F(P)

(or another explicit sound pruning bound)?

## 2. What the identity does and does not give

The identity is the standard entropy chain rule for a fixed correct decision tree of worst-case depth k on a uniform input: log_2(n!) = sum_v q_v h_2(p_v), E[D]=sum_v q_v, hence k - log_2(n!) = E[k-D] + sum_v q_v(1 - h_2(p_v)). Sergeev's group-insertion theorem gives a tree with k - log_2(n!) = O(n/(log n)^{1/5}) = o(n), so in expectation only o(n) visited comparisons have fixed bias. This is an average over inputs/paths. It imposes no pointwise lower bound on any individual branch and no deterministic choice rule at a state. The task below tests whether any aggregate local potential can nevertheless force a worst-case optimal comparison.

## 3. Candidate rules tested

Exhaustive enumeration of all labelled transitively closed posets on n <= 5 elements (1 + 3 + 19 + 219 + 4231 posets), with exact minimax F(P) and exact linear-extension counts e(P), and independent re-implementation of the recurrence. For each poset P with F(P)>0 and each incomparable pair {x,y}, we recorded:

- e(P), e(P+x<y), e(P+y<x);
- F(P), F(P+x<y), F(P+y<x), and the pair cost 1+max(child F);
- balance metrics: split ratio max(e_child)/e(P), entropy contribution h_2(e_child0/e(P)), and one-ply L0-max = max(ceil log_2 e_child0, ceil log_2 e_child1).

Candidate "balanced enough" rules:

1. Greedy extension-count balance: the pair minimizing max(e_child) (maximal balance by extension counts).
2. One-ply L1 minimizer: the pair minimizing max(ceil log_2 e_child) (the one-ply adversarial information bound of docs/research-one-ply-obstruction.md).
3. Kahn-Saks balanced: any pair with max(e_child)/e(P) <= 8/11 (the known universal existence interval).
4. High-entropy threshold: any pair with h_2(split) >= 0.9 (and the extreme case h_2=1).

A pair is "optimal" if its cost equals F(P).

## 4. Results

- For n <= 4, every candidate rule that selects a most-balanced pair (by any of the metrics above) selects only optimal pairs in every poset. No counterexample occurs.
- At n = 5, counterexamples appear:
  - 60 posets where the greedy extension-count balanced pair is not optimal;
  - 164 posets where the one-ply L1-minimizing pair is not optimal.
- There is no poset at n <= 5 in which every Kahn-Saks-balanced comparison is non-optimal; however, there are many posets containing a balanced (ratio <= 8/11, including ratio exactly 1/2 and entropy 1.0) comparison that is not optimal. Thus the universal statement "every balanced comparison is optimal" is false.

### Explicit counterexample (smallest; n = 5)

Let W = (0, 0, 9, 0, 3) (successor bitmasks; elements 0..4). It is transitively closed.

Independent checks:

- e(W) = 16 (exact subset-DP and brute-force permutation enumeration agree).
- F(W) = 4 (independent memoized recurrence and a separate non-memoized recursion agree).
- All six incomparable pairs and their values:

| pair {x,y} | e(W+x<y) | e(W+y<x) | max ratio | h_2(split) | L0-max | F(child0) | F(child1) | cost | optimal? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| {0,1} | 7 | 9 | 0.562 | 0.989 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 5 | no |
| {0,3} | 7 | 9 | 0.562 | 0.989 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 5 | no |
| {1,2} | 2 | 14 | 0.875 | 0.544 | 4 | 1 | 4 | 5 | no |
| {1,3} | 8 | 8 | 0.500 | 1.000 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 5 | no |
| {2,4} | 8 | 8 | 0.500 | 1.000 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 | yes |
| {3,4} | 2 | 14 | 0.875 | 0.544 | 4 | 1 | 4 | 5 | no |

The comparison {1,3} is maximally balanced by every aggregate metric available from extension counts: split 8/8, ratio 1/2, entropy 1.0, and one-ply L0-max 3 (the minimum possible). Yet its cost is 5 > F(W)=4. The equally balanced comparison {2,4} has cost 4 and is optimal. No threshold on ratio, entropy, or L0-max can include {2,4} while excluding {1,3}, because their aggregate splits are identical. The difference is structural: the children of {1,3} have minimax values 4 and 4, while the children of {2,4} have values 3 and 3, despite both having 8 linear extensions.

This is the obstruction: extension counts (and therefore entropy contributions p_i = e(child)/e(P)) are not a sufficient statistic for the minimax value F. Two comparisons with identical average-case entropy can have different worst-case child values. Hence an average-case entropy identity cannot, by itself, certify a deterministic optimal comparison or a sound pruning bound.

## 5. Relation to prior results

This is the same phenomenon as the state-compression no-go (docs/research-state-compression.md section (a)): two n=5 posets with the same number of linear extensions can have different F. Here the phenomenon occurs inside a single poset, between two comparisons with identical extension-count splits. It is consistent with, and sharpens, the one-ply L1 bound (which is a sound lower bound, not a move selector) and the n=12 non-tightness obstruction (local scalar invariants do not determine m(P)).

## 6. What this does not establish

- It does not prove impossibility for every conceivable locally computable potential; it falsifies the natural aggregate potentials (extension-count ratio, entropy contribution, one-ply L1) and shows why any sound rule must distinguish structurally different children with identical extension counts, which is at least as hard as evaluating a stronger invariant than e(P).
- It does not improve any bound on S(n), does not settle S(12), and makes no complexity-class claim.
- No Lean, repository code, or pull request changes are made.
