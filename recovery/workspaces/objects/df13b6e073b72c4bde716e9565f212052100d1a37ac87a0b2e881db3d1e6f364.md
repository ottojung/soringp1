## 1. Falsifiable finite task

`[definition]` (model: `docs/problem.md`) `S(n)` is the minimum worst-case number of adaptive pairwise comparisons to sort `n` distinct labelled keys (no ties). A state is a transitively closed strict partial order `P`; `e(P)` is the exact number of linear extensions. `F(P)` is the minimax remaining-comparison value.

`[deduction]` `13! = 6,227,020,800`, `C(13)=ceil(log2 13!)=33`, so `S(13) >= 33`. The task is the one-sided Wells admissible heavier-child forward analysis at target height `C=33`, quotiented by poset isomorphism (congruence quotient), **completed to depth 7** with explicit caps and saved JSON.

**Heavier-child Wells levels (one-sided):**
- `S_0 = {∅_{13}}` (antichain).
- At depth `c` (comparisons already made), a move `(u,v)` from representative `P ∈ S_c` is *admissible* iff **both** children satisfy `e(child) <= 2^(C-c-1)`.
- `S_{c+1}` contains the **heavier child** (`max(e1,e2)`) of every admissible move from every representative in `S_c`, quotiented by labelled-poset isomorphism of the transitive-closed successor DAG.

Depth-7 child cap: `2^(33-7)=67,108,864`.

`[deduction]` Soundness (Wells/Peczarski/Stober–Weiss heavier-child argument, cf. `docs/research-n12-nontightness.md` §4): if any `S_c = ∅` under a **complete, audited, sound** enumeration discipline, no height-`C` sorting tree exists and `S(13)>33`. A complete empty level would be an obstruction candidate subject to audit.

`[literature context, not audited here]` For `n=12` Wells' one-sided exploration empties a level; for `n>=13` the published discipline may require inspecting second children / bidirectional or complete-child handling. Therefore a non-empty one-sided level is **inconclusive**, and this computation alone cannot certify `S(13)>33` or `S(13)<=33`. Resource exhaustion/stopped runs are also inconclusive.
