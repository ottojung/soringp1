## Use of TR20-096

The artifact uses TR20-096 appropriately as the primary target:
- It identifies the right theorem chain (Lemma 1–4, Thm 1–3, Cor. 2) and states the asymptotic result `S(n)=log2(n!)+O(n/(log n)^(1/5))` as an upper bound on adaptive comparison sorting, not an exact-`S(n)` evaluator. `[literature/deduction]`
- It distinguishes algorithmic components (group insertion, strategies, LP/feasibility, interpolation) from effectivity of the final asymptotic inequality. This is the right distinction for a constructive/effective-certificate audit.
- It does not treat `o(n)`/big-`O` notation as an implicit finite certificate.
- **Caveat:** this audit did not independently re-extract TR20-096 section/page details from the PDF. The artifact cites local analyses and reports PDF-derived numbering/parameter details; those should be considered **partially rechecked against project context, not primary-source reverified here**.

## Claim-discipline check

The artifact is largely compliant with `AGENTS.md`/`problem.md`:
- **No unconditional separation claim:** it explicitly makes none and frames the question as extractability of an effective finite bound. Correct.
- **No NP/P membership claim from Sergeev:** it states an effective upper bound would not by itself prove `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ NP/P` and would not address `P != PSPACE` / `NP != PSPACE`. Correct and important given `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ PSPACE` is already known.
- **Correct role of an effective certificate:** it says a computable `U_eff(n)=ceil(log2(n!))+f(n)` would narrow the threshold/search window and may support witnesses on the `k >= U_eff(n)` side, but not prove optimality or exact evaluation. Correct.
- **Model fidelity:** it keeps `S(n)` as adaptive comparison-tree height for distinct labelled keys and treats `SORT-BOUND-U` as the unary threshold language. Correct.
- **Evidence labeling:** it uses `[literature]`, `[deduction]`, `[uncertain]`, `[open]` and flags missing/replaced local audit paths. Good practice.

Minor wording nits (not claim violations): “certifiable with resolved parameters…” in the framing could be read as asking for NP-style certification; the body clarifies that naming Thm 3 is not a verifier and that only a fully resolved finite object/countable proof artifact would qualify. The conclusion avoids the NP trap.

## What would count as an effective/constructive certificate (audit standard)

A positive future result should supply:
1. Explicit computable `f` (or algorithm) and `n0` with `S(n) <= ceil(log2(n!)) + f(n)` for all `n>=n0`, or all `n`.
2. Explicit integer/computable schedules and all used Thm 2/Lemma 4 parameters, with hypothesis checks.
3. Explicit bounds for every hidden constant, approximation, rounding, container, positivity/information, and slack term.
4. A sound finite basis for small `a`/`n` and any exceptional cases.
5. A direct verifier artifact: either machine-checkable finite data/proof objects, or a complete finite proof plus reproducible finite-base computation.

For `SORT-BOUND-U`, such a bound narrows the middle window; it is **not** an NP witness for exact threshold answers and **not** a complexity-class separation.

## Recommendation

Accept the artifact’s negative audit conclusion as a **working research status**: TR20-096 is strong asymptotic evidence but not yet an effective finite certificate. Do not treat it as a barrier theorem against all effectivization. If pursued, next work should be a primary-source line-by-line finite extraction/reproof targeting B1–B6, with a reproducible finite base; avoid any claim that a Sergeev upper bound resolves `SORT-BOUND-U` membership below PSPACE or proves separations.
