# Succinct refutation certificates for NO instances of `SORT-BOUND-U`

**Status:** paper-level analysis (definitions, deductions, literature-backed barriers). Not Lean-checked. No repository state outside this report was modified.

**Labels used:** `[definition]`, `[literature]`, `[deduction]`, `[open]`, `[conjecture]`.

## Definitions

- `[definition]` For `n ≥ 0`, `S(n)` is the minimum worst-case height of a deterministic adaptive comparison decision tree that sorts every permutation of `n` distinct labelled keys (docs/problem.md).
- `[definition]` Unary threshold language: `SORT-BOUND-U = { 1^n # 1^k : S(n) ≤ k }`. Input length `N = n + k + 1` (docs/problem.md).
- `[definition]` A **NO instance** of `SORT-BOUND-U` is an input `1^n # 1^k` with `S(n) > k`. This is the complement event in the standard decision sense: the instance is well-formed but not in the language.
- `[definition]` Minimax/comparison game `G(n,k)` (docs/research-certificates-barriers.md, Claim A): state is a transitively closed partial order `P` on `n` labelled elements, starting at the antichain. While `P` is not total and fewer than `k` comparisons have been made, the sorter picks an incomparable pair and the adversary answers `<` or `>`; `P` becomes the transitive closure. Sorter wins iff a total order is reached within `k`. `[deduction]` `S(n) ≤ k` iff the sorter has a winning strategy in `G(n,k)`; equivalently `S(n) > k` iff the adversary has a winning strategy.
- `[definition]` Let `F(P)` denote the minimax remaining comparison count from partial order `P` (docs/research-winning-frontier-barrier.md). Then `S(n)=F(∅_n)` and a NO instance means every first move leads to some adversary answer with `1+F(P') > k`.
- `[definition]` A **succinct polynomial-time-verifiable refutation certificate** (coNP-style witness for the threshold language, relative to `N`) would be a string `w` with `|w| ≤ poly(N)` and a deterministic verifier `V(1^n#1^k,w)` running in time `poly(N)` such that `V` accepts some `w` iff `S(n)>k`.

## Claim

**Falsifiable question (all `n,k` with `S(n)>k`):** does `SORT-BOUND-U` admit succinct polynomial-time-verifiable refutation/adversary-strategy certificates beyond exhaustive game-tree exploration?

**Answer (current state):** `[open]`. No such general succinct certificate is known, and there is no known proof that none exists. However, there are concrete structural obstructions to the natural certificate routes (explicit adversary strategies, exhaustive/state-enumerating refutations, and known potential-function/information-theoretic lower-bound certificates) in the hard regime. Therefore the evidence supports: *no known succinct refutation certificate beyond exhaustive exploration, with identified obstructions*, rather than a constructed succinct witness.

## Analysis

### 1. Minimax/PSPACE characterization
- `[literature]/[deduction]` `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ PSPACE` via the alternating formulation of `G(n,k)`: the ATM stores an `n×n` order/state in `O(n^2)` space and runs `O(k·n^2)=O(N^3)` alternating time, hence `ATIME(poly(N))=APTIME=PSPACE` (docs/research-certificates-barriers.md, Claim A; Chandra–Kozen–Stockmeyer).
- `[deduction]` For a NO instance, adversary-forcing is the universal side of this game: for every sorter move there exists an adversary answer such that, recursively, no total order is reached within the remaining budget. PSPACE membership gives polynomial-space decidability of both YES and NO, but it does **not** yield `NP` or `coNP` membership, because the game may have polynomially many alternations and the obvious witnesses/strategies are not known to compress to polynomial size.
- `[deduction]` A coNP certificate for `SORT-BOUND-U` is exactly a succinct refutation for NO instances. `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ coNP` is open (docs/research-certificates-barriers.md §6).

### 2. Adversary policies as candidate certificates
- `[deduction]` The canonical adversary strategy is a policy mapping each reachable non-total state and sorter-chosen incomparable pair to a comparison outcome that preserves `F(P') > remaining-1`. To certify a NO instance globally, such a policy must be correct against **all** sorter strategies (all comparison choices at all sorter-controlled nodes on plays consistent with the policy).
- `[deduction]` Naive representations are exponential-scale in the worst case:
  - An explicit strategy/game subtree may branch on every sorter move and require specifying answers over a large reachable-state space. The labelled-poset state space is `2^{Θ(n^2)}` (Kleitman–Rothschild; A001035 counts appear as reachable states in the full DP/experiment in docs/research-certificates-barriers.md §5), so an uncompressed state-indexed adversary table is not polynomial in `N`.
  - Even along one line of play, budgets/paths can be large relative to succinctness needs; more critically, global correctness is a universal property over sorter choices, not a single path.
- `[deduction]` Therefore "exhaustive game-tree exploration" / exhaustive strategy enumeration is the default complete refutation method, but not a succinct certificate in input length.

### 3. Lower-bound certificates and the information bound
- `[literature]/[deduction]` The standard counting/information lower bound gives `S(n) ≥ ⌈log2(n!)⌉`: a correct tree must distinguish all `n!` permutations (docs/problem.md, docs/research-certificates-barriers.md). Hence for any instance with `k < ⌈log2(n!)⌉`, the inequality itself is a succinct, polynomial-time-checkable refutation once `n!`/its logarithmic threshold is compared to `k` in the standard arithmetic model for numbers of size `poly(N)`; this is the "easy" NO regime.
- `[deduction]` The hard regime is `k ≥ ⌈log2(n!)⌉` (and necessarily `k < S(n)` for a NO instance). Here the information/counting argument is insufficient, because enough leaves/comparison outcomes exist in principle; refuting `S(n) ≤ k` requires showing no adaptive strategy exploits them to sort within `k`.
- `[literature]` Published exact lower bounds beyond the information bound in difficult cases (e.g. the `n=12` gap and later exact values up to current records) rely on exhaustive/structured exhaustive search over posets or equivalent state spaces, not on a known general polynomial witness (Wells; Peczarski; Stober–Weiß; summarized in docs/literature context of research-certificates-barriers.md §8.2, research-plan.md).

### 4. Structural facts relevant to obstructions
- `[literature]/[deduction]` No potential strictly stronger and generally applicable than linear-extension/counting information is available as a compact exact certificate: `log2 e(P) ≤ F(P)` is the information-theoretic potential but it is not tight in general; `e(P)` counting is #P-complete (Brightwell–Winkler), which is a barrier to greedy/counting routes though not itself a hardness proof for `S(n)` (docs/research-certificates-barriers.md, Claim E).
- `[deduction]` The winning-frontier barrier (docs/research-winning-frontier-barrier.md) shows an exponential multiplicity phenomenon on the *winning* side: at a common depth there can be `2^{Ω(n log^2 n)}` nonisomorphic states still sortable within the remaining budget (including at budgets around/at the optimum in the stated asymptotic sense). While this is not a lower bound on NO-instance certificates, it evidences that the minimax landscape contains enormously many distinct strategically relevant states and that frontier-enumeration methods are structurally explosive. A dual "losing frontier" can likewise be large in general game terms, and no compression theorem is known.
- `[deduction]` Encoding constraints shape but do not resolve the question: `SORT-BOUND-U` is sparse, so many-one NP-hardness of the language would imply `P=NP` (Mahaney), but this does not rule out `coNP` membership or succinct refutations. Binary encoding differences are out of active scope.

## Obstruction or construction sketch

### Concrete structural obstructions (to known/natural succinct-refutation routes)
1. **Universal adversary correctness:** `[deduction]` a refutation must rule out *all* sorter strategies in `G(n,k)`. A raw adversary policy is a global object; local correctness at sampled branches is insufficient unless accompanied by a proof that every sorter choice is covered.
2. **State/object explosion:** `[literature]/[deduction]` reachable/strategically relevant partial orders form an exponential-size domain (`2^{Θ(n^2)}` labelled posets in general; winning-frontier already exponential in a strong sense). No known canonical polynomial-size quotient/DAG captures optimal adversary play for all `n,k`.
3. **No tight succinct invariant:** `[open]/[deduction from literature]` in the regime `k ≥ ⌈log2(n!)⌉`, no polynomial-time-checkable potential or invariant is known that certifies `F(P)>r` tightly enough to serve as a general NO witness. Existing stronger-than-information arguments are instance-specific and search-based.
4. **Verifier gap for compressed strategies:** `[open]` a "program/strategy" description could be short, but verifying it against all `n!`-relevant outcomes or all sorter moves is not known to be polynomial-time in `N` without additional structure.

### Construction sketch (conditional/partial, not a general solution)
- `[deduction]` **Easy NO regime:** if `k < ⌈log2(n!)⌉`, output certificate `(n,k,\text{"info-bound"})`; verifier checks the inequality. This is succinct but applies only outside the hard regime.
- `[open]` **Hypothetical succinct refutation:** exhibit either (a) a polynomial-size adversary strategy representation together with a polynomial-time local-correctness proof (e.g. an inductive invariant implying every allowed response keeps remaining budget below `F`), or (b) a polynomial-size dual potential/cutting-plane-style witness showing every root strategy hits a certified losing state within `k`. Neither form is currently known for general NO instances.
- `[deduction]` **Exhaustive baseline (non-succinct):** fully explore `G(n,k)` (or run the known PSPACE/game decision procedure and record the explored refutation tree) to confirm adversary win; correct but exponential in the relevant parameters and thus not a succinct certificate.

## Falsification

The obstruction/negative assessment would be weakened or refuted by any of:
- An explicit encoding of adversary strategies for all NO instances with `|w|,T(V) \le poly(n+k)` and a correctness proof (establishing `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ coNP`).
- A polynomial-time-checkable invariant stronger than `log2 e(P)` that certifies `S(n)>k` in the hard regime (`k ≥ ⌈log2(n!)⌉`) for general `n`.
- A theorem that optimal adversary play can be confined to polynomially many canonical states/transcripts, with a verifier exploiting that compression.
- A concrete family construction showing NO certificates of polynomial size for all large `n` in the relevant threshold window.

Conversely, confidence in obstruction would increase by: a proof that some natural certificate class (e.g. bounded-width strategy DAGs, fixed invariant families) is insufficient infinitely often; or a formal reduction showing succinct NO certificates collapse complexity classes in a way considered implausible relative to the fixed unary/PSPACE setting (any such claim would require extreme care not to assert open separations).

## Consequence

- `[deduction]` For the active unary target, a general succinct refutation certificate would imply `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ coNP`, a genuine upper-bound/classification advance below PSPACE (cf. problem.md "Achievable in principle"). No such advance is currently established.
- `[deduction]` The absence of a known succinct NO witness aligns with, but does not prove, that exact lower-bound arguments may remain search/exploration-based or require new structural invariants; this identifies "succinct coNP-side certificates / stronger potentials" as a sharp open frontier rather than a resolved method.
- `[deduction]` Because `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ PSPACE` is already known, neither `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ coNP` nor `SORT-BOUND-U ∉ coNP` is forced by the major separation consequences attached to `∉ P` or `∉ NP`; the question is comparatively live but unresolved.
- `[deduction]` Practical/research consequence: attempts to certify NO instances should either (i) target restricted regimes (e.g. information-bound regime) where succinct witnesses exist, or (ii) seek a new compressing invariant/strategy representation and explicitly test verifiability and coverage, since exhaustive adversary exploration is complete but non-succinct.
