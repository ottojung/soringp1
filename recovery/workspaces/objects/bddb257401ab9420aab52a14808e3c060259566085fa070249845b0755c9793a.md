## Question (all n, falsifiable)

For framed sorting-certificate DAGs, when does an **intersection + permutation join** of incoming branches preserve (a) soundness/correctness and (b) **equivalence for optimal strategies** (same minimax value / exact-budget winnability)? Is there an all-n merge condition that supports a polynomial-size completeness candidate, or an obstruction?

## Model and notation

- State = strict partial order `P` on `n` slots (transitive closure), successor bitmasks.
- `F(P)` = minimax remaining comparisons from `P`; `S(n)=F(empty)`.
- A framed DAG node's guaranteed poset is the intersection of all feasible incoming posets (in the node's common frame).
- Depth at a join = `d = max` incoming depths; remaining budget `k-d`.
- Meet of incoming posets: `R = ∧_i P_i` (coordinatewise intersection).

## Merge condition (claims)

**Claim 1 (soundness, always).** [deduction] Any intersection join is sound: `LE(R) ⊇ ⋃_i LE(P_i)`, so `R` overapproximates all inputs reaching the node. Acyclicity/transitivity are preserved. Permutation frames already encoded on edges only relabel slots; they do not affect soundness.

**Claim 2 (budget compatibility).** [deduction] A join is compatible with budget `k` iff `F(R) ≤ k-d`. Because `R` is weaker than each `P_i`, `F(R) ≥ F(P_i)`.

**Claim 3 (lossless / optimal-strategy equivalence).** [deduction + experiment] A join preserves the minimax value of every incoming state (no forgetting loss) iff
`F(R) = F(P_i)` for all `i`. Equivalently, every relation occurring in some `P_i` but not in `R` is **F-redundant at `R`**: `F(R + (u≺v)) = F(R)`.

**Claim 4 (syntactic sufficient conditions).** [deduction + experiment]
1. **Identical posets** (after frame alignment): `P_i` all equal ⇒ `R=P_i`, lossless.
2. **Subset (domination):** if one `P_i`'s relation set is contained in another's, then `R` equals the weaker one; lossless iff the stronger has the same `F`.
3. **Common base:** if all `P_i` are strengthenings of a base `B` (i.e. `B ⊑ P_i` as relation sets), then `B ⊑ R`, so `F(R) ≤ F(B)`. If `F(B) ≤ k-d`, the join is budget-compatible even when not lossless.
4. **Isomorphism:** if `P_i,P_j` are isomorphic as unlabelled posets, a frame permutation can align them exactly, reducing to case (1); lossless.

**Claim 5 (most joins are lossy).** [experiment] For `n=4` (`219` posets), among `24090` pairs, `19609` (`≈81%`) are strict-lossy (`F(R)>max F(P_i)`), while only `471` (`≈2%`) are value-preserving; for sampled `n=5` pairs the lossy fraction is `≈93%`. Thus arbitrary intersection cannot be used in exact-optimal framed DAGs.

## Small-case experiment

For `n≤5`, every value-preserving distinct pair found has its extra (non-common) constraints individually F-redundant at the meet; subsets of those constraints also preserve `F` (no hidden collective loss). Permutation alignment can convert some non-preserving pairs into preserving ones: for `n=4`, `29/200` sampled non-preserving identity pairs became value-preserving under some permutation.

## Winning-frontier family

Construction: `s` disjoint chains of length `L` (`n=sL`), plus a subset of `M=(L-1)s(s-1)` independent extra adjacent-level edges.

- [experiment, small n] For `(s,L) = (2,2),(2,3),(3,2)`, every tested single extra edge is F-redundant relative to the skeleton (`F` unchanged), and pairwise intersections of distinct one-extra-edge posets preserve `F`.
- [deduction] The skeleton `B` is a common base of all `P_A`, so `B ⊑ R` for any meet `R` of a subset; hence `F(R) ≤ F(B)`. The construction gives `F(B) ≤ B_budget = k-t`. Therefore meets remain winning at the same budget.
- Consequently the `2^(Ω(n(log n)^2))` winning states are **not** an obstruction to polynomial framed DAGs under this merge condition: they collapse losslessly/budget-compatibly to the skeleton or to common-extra subsets. This supports the existing `O(n²)`-node adaptive merge-sort certificate as the all-n completeness candidate.

## Outcome

- **Not an obstruction result** for framed DAG compression; rather, a **merge condition** plus a positive collapse observation.
- The condition is semantic (`F`-redundancy / `F(meet)≤remaining budget`), with cheap sufficient syntactic proxies (identity, domination, common base, isomorphism).
- Open: whether there is a polynomial-time way to recognize F-redundant edges without computing `F` (which is the core cost), and whether every optimal strategy has polynomially many non-mergeable states. The winning-frontier family does not settle this.
