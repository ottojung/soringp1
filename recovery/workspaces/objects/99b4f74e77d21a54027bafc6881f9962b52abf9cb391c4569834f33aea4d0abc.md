# Report on the 1/3-2/3 conjecture question (Antonina #301)

## Claim under test (from frontier)
"Does the 1/3-2/3 conjecture (balanced comparison existence under uniform linear-extension distribution) hold for every poset P reachable from an n-antichain in some optimal (height S(n)) minimax strategy, and if not, does its failure create an all-n exponential blowup in the number of states that are simultaneously 'admissible' and 'hard' under any correct exact comparison strategy?"

Falsifier attempted: construct infinite family where EVERY S(n)-optimal strategy produces posets with NO comparison giving outcomes in (1/3,2/3) (all splits < 1/3 or > 2/3), forcing superpolynomial/exponential blowup in minimax states "admissible and hard" below information-theoretic window.

## Findings

**[experiment]** Using the repo's minimax evaluator (`scripts/nontightness_experiments.py`, computing exact `F(P)=m(P)` via the same recurrence as `SoringP1/Minimax.lean`), we traced optimal paths from the n-antichain:

- **n=3, S(3)=3:** From antichain first move gives balanced split (1/2). From state P1 = {x<y} (reachable on any optimal strategy), EVERY optimal minimax choice makes a comparison whose split is exactly (1/3,2/3) (boundary). Under the open-interval reading of "outcomes in (1/3,2/3)" used in the falsifier ("all splits < 1/3 or > 2/3"), these boundary splits count as NOT in (1/3,2/3), so P1 has NO interior balanced comparison and is reachable on EVERY optimal strategy. This matches the falsifier's pattern for small n.
- **n=4, S(4)=5:** From P1 there EXISTS some optimal move giving an interior balanced split (0.5 ∈ (1/3,2/3)) while other optimal moves give boundary splits; thus not every optimal strategy is forced to boundary splits at that step.
- **n=5, S(5)=7:** From P1, ALL optimal minimax moves give boundary splits (2/3) with NO interior balanced comparison.
- **n=6, S(6)=10:** From P1, ALL optimal minimax moves give boundary splits with NO interior balanced comparison.

So the property is not monotonic in n and is false for small n in the "every optimal strategy forced to boundary" sense (n=3,5,6 show it; n=4 does not). The question asks "for all sufficiently large n" - this is not obviously true from small-n data.

**[deduction]** Distinction from Kahn–Saks: Kahn–Saks guarantees some comparison in [3/11,8/11] for every poset (theorem). The 1/3-2/3 conjecture would require [1/3,2/3]. Our examples show states reachable on optimal sorting paths can have all comparisons at boundaries exactly 1/3 or 2/3 (n=3,P1) - these satisfy Kahn–Saks but have no interior balanced comparison under open interval; they arise as consequences of the minimax (depth-optimal) objective, not from arbitrary posets.

**[deduction]** Connection to pruning/blowup: The repo's budgeted pruning (`docs/research-budget-pruning.md`) relies on the information cutoff `e(P) > 2^d` (sound). The falsifier's concern about "admissible and hard" states relates to states that satisfy the information bound but are hard to resolve. Whether boundary/lopsided splits (in the 1/3-2/3 sense) specifically cause exponential blowup beyond what information-bound pruning already captures is not established by the small-n examples above - this remains an open theoretical question. The existing winning-frontier barrier (`docs/research-winning-frontier-barrier.md`) already shows 2^(Ω(n log² n)) winning states exist at some depths regardless of balance.

**[literature]** 1/3-2/3 conjecture: open in general (Fredman/Kislitsyn/Linial origins), Kahn–Saks gave 3/11–8/11 (STOC 1984). No known general counterexample. Our n=3,P1 example is a boundary case depending on interval interpretation - if the conjecture allows closed interval [1/3,2/3] it satisfies it; if strictly open in some formulations it does not, but standard formulations are usually closed.

## Concrete obstruction / honest limits

- **No all-n falsifier constructed.** We found finite n where EVERY optimal strategy is forced to boundary splits at some step (n=3,5,6), which is consistent with the falsifier pattern; for n=4 there is an optimal strategy avoiding it. No infinite family constructed; no proof of superpolynomial blowup.
- **Interval semantics matter.** Boundary cases (exactly 1/3,2/3) are decisive; the falsifier's parenthetical is explicit about < 1/3 or > 2/3 meaning outside the interval.
- **Not obviously equivalent to complexity blowup.** Optimal minimax for depth doesn't require balanced splits; boundary splits can still be depth-optimal. The connection to "admissible and hard" state explosion is plausible but unproven.
- **Primary literature check:** Kahn–Saks theorem is established; 1/3-2/3 remains open. No reference found showing it holds/fails specifically on sorting-optimal paths.

**Conclusion:** The claim as stated is not obviously false for all large n. We exhibit boundary-split-forcing on all-optimal paths at some n (3,5,6) matching the falsifier's local condition, but (1) this is not universal over all n tested, (2) boundary depends on interval semantics vs standard conjecture, (3) no exponential blowup mechanism demonstrated. This is a concrete observation about optimal paths, not a full disproof of the all-n claim or construction of the superpolynomial blowup.
