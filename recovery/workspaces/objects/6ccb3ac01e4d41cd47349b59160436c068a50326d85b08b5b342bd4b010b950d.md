# Exact `S(n)` certificates: read-once, oblivious, branching-program/DAG, and meet obstructions

**Status:** research note (paper analysis; not Lean-checked). Labels:
`[definition]`, `[literature]`, `[deduction]`, `[open]`. No unconditional
separation, no NP/coNP membership, and no NP-hardness claim is made.

**Task (SoringP1 #301, falsifiable):** for exact-height `S(n)` yes-certificates
of `SORT-BOUND-U = {1^n # 1^k : S(n) <= k}`, test whether read-once,
oblivious, branching-program-style, or succinct comparator-DAG certificate
classes have a precise completeness or size obstruction beyond the
permutation-frame/meet (PGF-meet) obstruction already identified for framed
DAGs, or whether a concrete representable subclass exists. Claims are tied to
the adaptive comparison-game quantifier and to `n=12`, `S(12)=30`.

## 1. Adaptive comparison-game quantifier

`[definition]`/`[literature]`. Let `S(n)` be the minimum worst-case number of
adaptive pairwise comparisons needed to sort every permutation of `n` distinct
labelled keys (`docs/problem.md`). A yes-certificate for `(1^n,1^k) \in
SORT-BOUND-U` is any object `C` for which there is a polynomial-time verifier
`V` with

```text
S(n) <= k  <=>  ∃C, |C| <= p(n+k+1) and V(1^n,1^k,C)=1.
```

`[deduction]` The underlying minimax/alternation form of `S(n)<=k` is the
finite comparison game `G(n,k)` (`docs/research-certificates-barriers.md`):
states are transitively closed partial orders `P`; the **sorter** existentially
chooses an incomparable pair; the **adversary** universally answers with one of
the two outcomes; sorter wins if a total order is reached within `k`
comparisons. Thus

```text
S(n) <= k  iff  ∃σ_sorter ∀σ_adversary (height of play <= k and terminal total).
```

A certificate class is **sound for exact `S(n)` height** if every accepted
`(n,k,C)` implies `S(n)<=k`. It is **complete for exact `S(n)`** if for every
`n` there is an accepted certificate with `k=S(n)`. **Polynomial completeness**
for exact `S(n)` would mean such certificates have size `poly(n)` (equivalently
`poly(N)` on informative unary instances, since `N=n+k+1=\Theta(n log n)` when
`k=S(n)=\Theta(n log n)`). Polynomial completeness would imply
`SORT-BOUND-U \in NP` `[deduction]`; it is **not** asserted.

## 2. The baseline PGF-meet obstruction (already known here)

`[literature]`/`[deduction]` from `docs/research-framed-certificate-frontier.md`.
The framed permutation-graph certificate propagates a guaranteed strict partial
order `R`; at a join it takes the **meet/intersection** of feasible incoming
guarantees. This is sound because intersection removes only knowledge, never
adds unjustified order. The script
[`scripts/check_framed_strategy.py`](../scripts/check_framed_strategy.py)
implements this local meet semantics.

**PGF-meet obstruction (soundness/completeness tradeoff for naive joins).**
`[deduction]` A fixed comparator strategy may be correct globally while two
feasible histories arriving at the same program point carry different
disjunctive correlations; intersecting their `R`'s can discard the correlation
needed by later comparisons. Therefore a class that forces immediate
intersection at every join is not complete for arbitrary correct strategies.
`research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` reports an optimal five-comparison
network for four elements as such a counterexample and, conversely, accepts a
quadratic bubble-sort witness by using branch permutations. This obstruction is
about **representation of joins under meet semantics**, not about NP-hardness
and not about the nonexistence of some other polynomial certificate.

**What is proved/constructed beyond the obstruction (paper-level, pending
independent all-`n` audit).** `research-framed-certificate-frontier.md` gives
polynomial framed DAG constructions for adaptive merge sort (`O(n^2)` nodes,
`O(n log n)` path length) and a plausible rank-envelope/pendant-insertion
construction for the Ford–Johnson schedule (`O(n^2)` query nodes). The equal-
halves merge architecture has comparison deficit relative to `log2(n!)`,
so it cannot witness exact `S(n)` for large powers of two `[literature]` via
Sergeev's asymptotic upper bound; DAG sharing cannot repair a comparison
deficit. Ford–Johnson gives an upper-bound certificate, not exact `S(n)`.

## 3. Certificate classes and their relation to the quantifier

### 3.1 Explicit adaptive trees

`[deduction]`/`[experiment]` (`research-certificates-barriers.md`,
`research-strategy-certificates.md`). An explicit correct comparison tree has
at least `n!` reachable leaves and `2n!-1` nodes in reduced form. For
informative unary instances this is `2^{\Theta(N)}` in the explicit tree, hence
not polynomial in `N`; verification is polynomial in tree size, so the natural
verifier is at most `NEXP`, not `NP`. **Obstruction:** explicit-tree
representation size, independent of PGF meet (the tree has no DAG joins).

### 3.2 Decision DAGs / branching programs over poset states

`[definition]` A **strategy DAG** (hash-consed) has nodes = poset states and
edges encoding a comparison plus both outcomes; a verifier can check root,
poset validity, edge feasibility/closure, leaf totality, and height in time
polynomial in `|V|+|E|` and `n` (`research-strategy-certificates.md`, Thm
1.1). `[deduction]` This gives a certificate checker, not certificate
existence: `SORT-BOUND-U` gets only naive `NEXP`/`N2EXP` from explicit DAGs
unless a polynomial size bound is proved.

**State-count facts.** `[experiment]` For `n<=6`, a single optimal policy has
no hash-consing sharing: `DAG(1)=2n!-1` (it is a tree). `[deduction]` In a
reduced deterministic sorting comparison tree, distinct reachable nodes have
distinct labelled poset states, so hash-consing cannot shrink a *single-policy
tree*; sharing requires either DAG joins in the representation or considering
the union of all optimal moves. `[experiment]` The all-optimal DAG `DAG(all)`
shrinks relative to unshared union but grows rapidly (`19,181,1551,65551` for
`n=3..6`), with `log2(DAG(all))/n` increasing on that tiny range — **not** an
asymptotic lower bound.

**Precise obstruction (size/state, beyond PGF meet).** `[open]`/`[deduction]`
For the *plain poset-state DAG* (no permutation frame, no meet of distinct
histories into one abstract annotation except by identical labelled state),
polynomial exact-`S(n)` completeness is open. The obstacle is the same
`∃sorter ∀adversary` quantifier: the DAG must cover all adversary branches of
an optimal sorter strategy, and sharing occurs only when distinct histories
reach the same labelled poset. No theorem here shows `poly(n)` many states
suffice, and no theorem here shows they cannot. The PGF-meet issue is
orthogonal: PGF adds frame+intersection to gain sharing; plain poset DAGs avoid
unsound meets but may forfeit sharing.

### 3.3 Read-once certificates (over comparisons or over state bits)

`[definition]` Call a branching-program/certificate representation **read-once
in comparisons** if every root-to-accepting path tests any comparison pair
`{x,y}` at most once as a query. Call it **read-once in state bits** if it
reads each input/state bit at most once along a path (the usual OBDD/ROBP
read-once notion relative to a chosen variable encoding).

**Obstruction/representability (comparison read-once).** `[deduction]` Any
sound adaptive sorting strategy is read-once in comparisons: repeating
`{x,y}` after its outcome is known gives no information and cannot be needed on
a reduced optimal path. Therefore comparison read-once is **not an obstruction**;
it is a normal-form property of comparison strategies, not a restriction that
separates certificates.

**Obstruction/representability (state-bit read-once, OBDD-like).** `[open]`
An OBDD/ROBP reads a fixed Boolean input once in a fixed variable order. The
comparison game input is not a static bitstring but the adversary's online
answers to existentially chosen queries. Encoding a full strategy as a
read-once BP over a fixed schedule requires fixing query order (obliviousness)
and fixing bit access; exact `S(n)` allows adaptive query choice. Thus
state-bit read-once is a **candidate restriction**, but I do not prove
completeness or incompleteness for exact `S(n)`: it is neither justified as
complete nor ruled out here beyond the adaptivity mismatch.

### 3.4 Oblivious / fixed-schedule comparator DAG certificates

`[definition]` An **oblivious** certificate fixes a comparator schedule or a
fixed query order independent of adversary answers (a sorting-network-like
skeleton, possibly with framed/meet annotations if one tries to prove
correctness). `[literature]` Oblivious comparator networks and adaptive
decision trees are different models (`docs/problem.md`,
`research-certificates-barriers.md`).

**Completeness obstruction relative to `S(n)`.** `[deduction]` A fixed
oblivious comparator network of depth `d` can witness the upper bound “there is
an oblivious sort in depth/size terms,” but it does **not** in general witness
the adaptive comparison number `S(n)` unless its comparison count/height is
proved equal to `S(n)`. The class of oblivious certificates is therefore
**not the right completeness target for exact `S(n)`**: completeness for exact
`S(n)` would require, for every `n`, an oblivious schedule whose adaptive-
equivalent worst-case comparison bound equals `S(n)`. That is a strong
structural claim not established here. Conversely, using oblivious certificates
only as upper bounds is sound but incomplete for exact optimality whenever the
oblivious optimum differs from the adaptive optimum.

**Beyond PGF meet.** `[deduction]` The meet obstruction affects DAG joins that
merge histories; a plain oblivious network has fixed wires/comparators and no
adaptive-history meet in the PGF sense. Its obstruction is **model mismatch**
(adaptivity removed), not meet unsoundness.

### 3.5 Succinct comparator-DAG / framed PGF certificates

`[definition]` A **framed comparator DAG** is an acyclic graph of comparisons
with branch permutations and meet/intersection at joins, as checked by
`check_framed_strategy.py`. It is succinct if its explicit size is `poly(n)`.

**Known obstructions.**
- **PGF meet incompleteness for naive arbitrary joins** `[deduction]`, §2.
- **Comparison deficit cannot be fixed by sharing** `[literature]`/`[deduction]`,
  §2: an architecture using more comparisons than `S(n)` cannot become an exact
  `S(n)` certificate by compressing states.
- **Adaptive quantifier coverage** `[deduction]`: a sound exact certificate must
  justify all adversary outcomes along an optimal sorter strategy; local
  polynomial annotations are sound only when the meet/forgetting rule preserves
  enough information for future comparisons (`research-framed-certificate-frontier.md`,
  §4 gives necessary analytic conditions via an auxiliary walk, not a
  polynomial-time test).

**Concrete representable subclasses (sound, not known exact-complete).**
`[deduction]`/paper-level:
1. **Path/straight-line framed schedules with explicit permutations** — the
   bubble-sort witness is the trivial example (`check_framed_strategy.py`),
   size `O(n^2)`, complete for the bubble upper bound, not for `S(n)`.
2. **Adaptive merge-sort framed DAG** — `O(n^2)` comparison nodes, `O(n log n)`
   depth, sound by ordered-prefix/two-queue meet invariant; not exact-optimal
   for large powers of two.
3. **Rank-envelope pendant insertion / Ford–Johnson-framed DAG** — plausible
   `O(n^2)` query-node certificate for the Ford–Johnson upper bound, pending
   independent audit of recursive frames, boundaries, and odd elements; not
   equal to `S(n)` for all `n`.
4. **Single-policy explicit tree** — representable but exponential (`2n!-1`).

These subclasses show representability/soundness for specific upper bounds;
they do **not** establish polynomial completeness for exact `S(n)`.

## 4. The `n=12`, `S(12)=30` tie-in

`[literature]` Wells showed `S(12)=30` while the information lower bound is
`C(12)=\lceil log2(12!)\rceil=29` (`docs/research-n12-nontightness.md`,
`docs/research-one-ply-obstruction.md`, `docs/literature.md`).

**Consequence for certificate classes.** `[deduction]`
- Any sound exact-height certificate for the true threshold at `n=12` must use
  budget `k>=30`. A claimed complete exact class that could produce a valid
  height-29 certificate for `n=12` would be **unsound**. A class that can only
  produce height `>=30` at `n=12` is consistent with the known value but this
  consistency is not completeness.
- `n=12` is the first global information-bound non-tightness; local one-ply
  information underestimates are common and do not by themselves force global
  non-tightness (`research-one-ply-obstruction.md`, H1). Therefore `n=12`
  obstructs **information-tight height bounds/certificates**, not framed DAG
  syntax as such.
- For PGF-meet/framed classes, `n=12` is relevant through the comparison count:
  a framed design whose root strategy needs 30 comparisons may still be sound
  and possibly succinct; a design that assumes 29 is impossible. The open
  question is whether the actual optimal `n=12` strategy has a polynomial
  framed/rank-envelope DAG representation — **open**; no such representation is
  exhibited here.

## 5. Falsifiable summary

| Class | Sound checker | Exact-`S(n)` complete? | Precise obstruction beyond/relative to PGF meet |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Explicit comparison tree | poly in tree | yes (trivial) | size `>=2n!-1` = `2^{Theta(N)}` on unary informative instances |
| Plain poset-state strategy DAG | poly in DAG | open | no meet unsoundness; potential state explosion / no poly sharing theorem |
| Comparison read-once strategy | same as strategy | normal form | no obstruction (reduced strategies are read-once in comparisons) |
| State-bit read-once / fixed-order BP | model-dependent | open | adaptivity/online adversary mismatch; not proved in/out for exact `S(n)` |
| Oblivious comparator DAG/network | model-dependent | not a complete exact-`S(n)` target unless adaptive optimum matched | model mismatch (adaptivity removed), not meet |
| Framed PGF meet DAG | poly in graph (`check_framed_strategy.py`) | open | naive immediate meet can be incomplete for arbitrary joins; comparison deficit cannot be shared away; all-adversary coverage/forgetting |
| Merge-sort framed subclass | poly | no (comparison deficit) | architecture deficit vs `S(n)` for large powers of two |
| Ford–Johnson/rank-envelope subclass | poly if audited | no (upper bound) | equals `F_FJ(n)`, not `S(n)` in general |
| Any info-bound-tight height-29 class at `n=12` | N/A | no (unsound there) | `S(12)=30 > 29` |

## 6. Next decisive checks

`[open]` Concrete falsifiers/provers:
1. Exhibit or rule out a polynomial-size framed PGF meet DAG for a known exact
   small case at its true `S(n)` (especially `n=12`, height 30), with the
   checker. Absence for searched subclasses is evidence only against those
   subclasses.
2. Formalize a read-once/state-bit BP model against the online comparison game
   and prove either a simulation (representable subclass) or an explicit
   non-simulation tied to adaptivity.
3. For all-optimal poset DAGs, measure or bound states on *some* exact
   `S(n)`-optimal root strategy for `n>=7` (distinct from all reachable or all
   optimal unions), as in `research-certificates-barriers.md` §8.
4. Audit Ford–Johnson framed permutations and boundaries as a fixed subclass
   certificate of `F_FJ(n)` (upper bound), keeping exact-`S(n)` claims separate.
