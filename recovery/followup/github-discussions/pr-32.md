**Updated, stronger paper-level theorem** (signed head `7efe8ca`, not yet independently approved or merged). The original two-edge binary-shift construction was unnecessarily elaborate. A simpler construction takes `s` disjoint already-sorted chains each of length `L`, established by `t₀=s(L−1)` adjacent-level comparisons. Each of the `M=(L−1)s(s−1)` other adjacent-level pairs is an **independent legal comparison**, giving `binom(M,m)` distinct labelled states at common depth `t=t₀+m`.

The `s` original chains remain sorted regardless of which extra edges were learned, and tournament merging needs at most `B=(s−1)+(sL−1)ceil(log₂s)` comparisons. Set `k=ceil(log₂((sL)!))` and choose `m=k−t₀−B`; then every constructed state is reachable and **winning** within `k−t=B`. Dividing by at most `(sL)!` labellings gives the non-isomorphic count.

**Exact new finite witness, with no floating-point arithmetic:** `s=16`, `L=14`, `n=224`, `k=1431`, `t₀=208`, `B=907`, `m=316`, `M=3120`, and `binom(3120,316)>2^39·224!`. Thus **more than `2^39` genuinely winning non-isomorphic states** at depth `524`. At `n=256`, `s=L=16`, the same count exceeds `2^148`. Asymptotically for `s=L=2^r`: count `2^((1/4−o(1))n(log₂n)²)`.

This excludes only algorithms that explicitly enumerate **every** reachable winning state modulo isomorphism; it does NOT prove a time lower bound for arbitrary unary S(n) algorithms, let alone class separation. Includes rigorous paper derivation, finite counts and adjacent-edge-independence tests (3/3 passing).
