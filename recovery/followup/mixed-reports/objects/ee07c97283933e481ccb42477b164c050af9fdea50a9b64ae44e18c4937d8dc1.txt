The “independent merge obligations” idea is not yet a defensible pruning rule. Disjoint subsets do not imply independent sorting tasks: cross-subset comparisons can reveal information about both, and the exact direct-sum argument in `research-series-decomposition.md` applies to **ordinal sums**, not arbitrary disjoint subsets. The existing correlated-subset counterexample also warns against assuming additivity without a product of independent extension sets.

I checked one small case—the union of two 2-element chains has minimax cost 3—but it does not test the proposed sum bound. I found no proof or concrete counterexample establishing that bound, so I would not dispatch it as a useful research front yet.
