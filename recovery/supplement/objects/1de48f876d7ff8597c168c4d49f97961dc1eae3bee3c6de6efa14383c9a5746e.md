# Poset canonicalization for the minimax sorting DP

**Status:** research note (2026-10-20). Literature survey and complexity
analysis. Everything below is a **deduction**, **literature result**, or
**conjecture** unless explicitly marked. No Lean file and no complexity-class
separation is claimed.

## Precise question

The `S_n` quotient of the minimax state space is exact
(`docs/research-state-compression.md`), but canonicalization is the
bottleneck: `n=9` takes 305 s with 183k classes because the current
invariant brute-forces permutations inside colour classes. The question is:

> What is the cheapest complete invariant or canonical form for posets
> reachable in sorting search, with a provable per-state time bound?

## Source and model

*Literature:* The primary sources are Babai (2019) on quasi-polynomial
canonization, Luks (1980) on bounded-degree canonization, McKay (1981) on
nauty, Miyazaki (1995) and STOC 2018 on exponential lower bounds for
individualization-refinement, and Heitzig-Reinhold (2000) / Lygeros-Zimmermann
(2003) on poset enumeration via orderly generation.

*Model:* A state is a strict partial order `P` on `n` labelled keys. The
minimax recurrence evaluates `F(P)` over isomorphism classes `[P]`. The
canonical form must be a complete invariant: `canon(P) = canon(Q)` iff
`P` is isomorphic to `Q`.

## Finding 1 (literature): Poset isomorphism is GI-complete

Poset isomorphism reduces from graph isomorphism via comparability graphs.
For posets of height at most 2 and interval dimension at most 2, isomorphism
is in P (`O(n^2)` time). For height 3, it is GI-complete. Therefore:

- There is **no known polynomial-time algorithm** for general poset
  canonization.
- Poset canonization is **at least as hard** as graph canonization.
- The best known general bound is **quasi-polynomial**: `exp((log n)^O(1))`
  per poset (Babai 2019).

## Finding 2 (literature): Nauty-style individualization-refinement

The standard practical approach is nauty's individualization-refinement
paradigm:

1. Run colour refinement (1-WL) to get a stable partition.
2. If discrete, read off the canonical labelling.
3. Otherwise, individualize a vertex in a target cell, refine, recurse.
4. Take the lexicographically smallest leaf encoding.

**Worst case:** Exponential. Miyazaki (1995) constructed graphs on which
nauty takes exponential time. STOC 2018 proved exponential lower bounds
for *any* individualization-refinement algorithm with k-realizable
refinement operators.

**Average case:** For most posets, the search tree is small. The key
parameter is the size of the individualization-refinement tree, which
depends on the automorphism group structure.

## Finding 3 (deduction): The staircase family is easy for canonicalization

The staircase family `P_m` (`docs/research-time-exponent.md`) has trivial
automorphism group. For such **rigid posets**:

- Nauty's search tree has depth 1 (no backtracking needed).
- Colour refinement alone may give discrete classes.
- Canonization is **polynomial time** per poset.

The `Theta(n^2)` incomparable pairs make the *minimax search* hard (many
candidate moves), but not the *canonicalization*. Rigid posets are the
easiest case for nauty.

## Finding 4 (deduction): Sparse posets and colour refinement

Posets reachable in `k` comparisons have at most `k` relations in the
transitive reduction. For `k = O(n log n)`, this is `O(n log n)` edges.

**Colour refinement cost:** `O(n(n+m)) = O(n^2 log n)` per poset for sparse
posets.

**Key question:** Does colour refinement give discrete classes for sparse
posets?

- For random sparse graphs, 2-WL gives discrete classes with high
  probability (STACS 2025).
- For adversarial sparse graphs, this is not guaranteed.
- **Conjecture:** For posets with `O(n log n)` relations, colour refinement
  plus individualization-refinement runs in polynomial time per poset with high
  probability over the uniform distribution on posets.

This is **not proved**. The worst case remains exponential.

## Finding 5 (deduction): Transitive reduction as canonical form

The transitive reduction (Hasse diagram) of a poset is a DAG. Canonizing
the poset is equivalent to canonizing its transitive reduction:

- The poset is the transitive closure of its reduction.
- Two posets are isomorphic iff their reductions are isomorphic as DAGs.
- The reduction has at most as many edges as the poset has relations.

For sparse posets, the reduction has `O(n log n)` edges. Canonizing a
sparse DAG is potentially easier than canonizing a dense one, but no
polynomial-time algorithm is known for general sparse DAGs.

## Finding 6 (deduction): Total bound for the minimax DP

The quotient DP explores `A000112(n) = 2^{Theta(n^2)}` states. For each state,
we canonicalize the poset and its `O(n^2)` successors.

**Total cost:** `O(A000112(n) * n^2 * C(n))` where `C(n)` is the
canonization cost per poset.

| Canonization method | `C(n)` | Total cost |
|---|---|---|
| Brute-force (current) | `O(n! * n^2)` worst | `2^{Theta(n^2)} * n!` |
| Nauty-style IR | `poly(n)` typical, `exp(n)` worst | `2^{Theta(n^2)} * poly(n)` typical |
| Babai quasi-poly | `exp((log n)^O(1))` | `2^{Theta(n^2)}` |

**Key insight:** The total bound is dominated by the state count
`2^{Theta(n^2)}`, not the canonization. Even with polynomial-time
canonization, the total is `2^{Theta(n^2)}`.

## Finding 7 (deduction): The bottleneck is state count, not canonization

The current bottleneck is canonization because the brute-force approach
is `O(n!)` per poset. Replacing it with nauty-style IR reduces the
per-state cost from `O(n!)` to `O(poly(n))` for most posets.

**Practical impact:** This should extend the quotient DP from `n=9` to
`n=10-12`, where the known obstruction `S(12)=30` lives.

**Theoretical impact:** None on the asymptotics. The total remains
`2^{Theta(n^2)}` regardless of the canonization method.

## What was learned

1. **Poset canonization is GI-complete.** No polynomial-time algorithm is
   known. The best bound is quasi-polynomial (Babai 2019).

2. **Nauty-style IR is the practical solution.** It reduces per-state cost
   from `O(n!)` to `O(poly(n))` for most posets. The worst case is
   exponential, but rare.

3. **The staircase family is easy.** Trivial automorphism group means
   no backtracking. The `Theta(n^2)` incomparable pairs do not hurt
   canonicalization.

4. **The bottleneck is state count.** Even with poly-time canonization,
   the total is `2^{Theta(n^2)}`. To improve asymptotically, we need a smaller
   state space, not faster canonization.

5. **Open question:** Is there a complete invariant cheaper than the full
   canonical form? The linear-extension count is falsified
   (`docs/research-state-compression.md`). No other candidate is known.

## Next unresolved obstacle

**State space reduction, not canonization, is the asymptotic bottleneck.**
The `S_n` quotient gives `A000112(n) = 2^{Theta(n^2)}` states. To improve the
asymptotic bound, we need either:

- A smaller exact quotient (are all `A000112(n)` classes actually reachable
  in optimal play? experiment says yes for `n <= 9`).
- A decomposition theorem (disjoint unions, series-parallel compositions).
- A different state representation that does not require canonicalization.

The canonization problem is **practically solved** by nauty-style IR, but
the **theoretical** bound remains `2^{Theta(n^2)}` due to the state count.
