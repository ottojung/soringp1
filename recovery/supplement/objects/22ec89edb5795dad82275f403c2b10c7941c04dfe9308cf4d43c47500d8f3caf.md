# Succinct strategy and negative-proof certificates for unary `SORT-BOUND-U`

**Status:** research note (paper analysis plus reproducible experiments for
`n ≤ 6`). **Not** Lean-checked. Every item is labelled `[literature]`,
`[deduction]`, `[experiment]`, `[conjecture]` or `[open]`. No new Lean files,
no commits, no PRs. This note is **unary-only**; binary variants are deferred.

This note responds to the 2026-10-19 directive: study whether unary
`SORT-BOUND-U ∈ P`, `∈ NP`, or below `PSPACE`, separating **strategy existence**
from **small certificates**, and testing structural restrictions against small
examples and known obstructions.

---

## 0. Model, encoding, and input-size accounting (fixed throughout)

`[literature]`/definition. From `docs/problem.md`:

- `S(n)` = minimum height (worst-case number of pairwise comparisons) of a
  **deterministic adaptive comparison decision tree** sorting `n` distinct
  labelled keys.
- `SORT-BOUND-U = { 1^n # 1^k : S(n) ≤ k }` — both parameters **unary**.

`[deduction]` **Unary accounting.** The input is `1^n # 1^k`, length `m = n+k+1`.
Hence `n ≤ m` and `k ≤ m`, so `poly(m) = poly(n)` and `poly(m) = poly(k)`. The
certificate bar is therefore **`poly(n)` bits**, and the poset state (`n×n`
ternary matrix) fits in `O(n²) = O(m²)` space. This is exactly why the
alternating-game bound gives `PSPACE` (Claim A of
`research-certificates-barriers.md`): an ATM stores the poset in `O(n²)=O(m²)`
space and plays `k ≤ m` rounds, so `ATIME(poly(m)) = APTIME = PSPACE`
(Chandra–Kozen–Stockmeyer `[literature]`).

`[deduction]` **Informative regime.** For a yes-instance `S(n) ≤ k` we have
`k ≥ S(n) ≥ ⌈log₂ n!⌉ = Θ(n log n)`, so `m = Θ(n log n)` and `n! = 2^{Θ(m)}`.
For a no-instance with `k < ⌈log₂ n!⌉` the information bound already decides it
(§5). The only instances needing a nontrivial certificate have
`k ∈ [⌈log₂ n!⌉, n(n-1)/2]`, i.e. `m` from `Θ(n log n)` up to `Θ(n²)`.

---

## 1. Precise question

1. **(YES certificate / NP)** Is there a `poly(n)`-bit, `poly(n)`-time-checkable
   witness for `S(n) ≤ k`, for every yes-instance? I.e. is `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ NP`?
2. **(NO certificate / coNP)** Is there a `poly(n)`-bit, `poly(n)`-time-checkable
   witness for `S(n) > k`, for every no-instance? I.e. is `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ coNP`?
3. **(P)** Is `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ P` (a `poly(n)`-time decision procedure)?

The current bound is `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ PSPACE` `[deduction]` (Claim A). All three
questions ask for a strict improvement.

---

## 2. The potential/Lyapunov certificate framework (new reframing)

`[deduction]` Define a **descent potential** `Φ: Posets → ℕ` by:

- `Φ(total order) = 0`;
- **descent:** for every non-total `P` there is an incomparable pair `{x,y}` with
  `max(Φ(P+x<y), Φ(P+y<x)) ≤ Φ(P) − 1`;
- **budget:** `Φ(∅) ≤ k`.

Following the descent comparisons from the antichain reaches a total order within
`Φ(∅)` comparisons (each step drops `Φ` by ≥ 1, and `Φ = 0` only at a total
order). Hence `S(n) ≤ Φ(∅)`.

`[deduction]` **D1 (F is a descent potential).** `F(P) = 1 + min_{x‖y}
max(F(P+x<y), F(P+y<x))` satisfies `F(total)=0` and, for non-total `P`, the
minimising pair gives `max(F(children)) = F(P) − 1`. So `F` is a descent
potential with `F(∅) = S(n)`. (This is the kernel-checked `adv univ`, A24
`S_eq_adv_univ`.)

`[deduction]` **D2 (F is the minimal descent potential).** If `Φ` is any descent
potential then `Φ(P) ≥ F(P)` for all `P`. *Proof:* induction on `F`. For total
`P`, `Φ=0=F`. For non-total `P`, descent gives a pair with `max(Φ(children)) ≤
Φ(P)−1`; by IH `F(child) ≤ Φ(child)`, so `max(F(children)) ≤ Φ(P)−1`, whence
`F(P) = 1 + min max(F(children)) ≤ 1 + (Φ(P)−1) = Φ(P)`. ∎

`[deduction]` **D3 (potential ⟺ decision).** A descent potential with
`Φ(∅) ≤ k` exists **iff** `S(n) ≤ k`. (`⟸`: take `Φ = F`. `⟹`: follow descent,
`S(n) ≤ Φ(∅) ≤ k`.) Consequently `F` is the unique *minimal* descent potential
(D2), and any descent potential with `Φ(∅) = S(n)` must agree with `F` at the
antichain while dominating it everywhere — **the potential certificate is
precisely as hard as the decision problem.**

`[deduction]` **Dual (ascent potentials, NO direction).** An **ascent potential**
`Ψ` has `Ψ(total)=0` and **ascent:** for every non-total `P` and *every*
incomparable `{x,y}`, `max(Ψ(P+x<y), Ψ(P+y<x)) ≥ Ψ(P) − 1` (the adversary can
always keep `Ψ` high). By a symmetric induction `Ψ ≤ F`, so `F` is the *maximal*
ascent potential; an ascent potential with `Ψ(∅) > k` exists iff `S(n) > k`. The
information bound `Ψ₁ = ⌈log₂ e(P)⌉` is an ascent potential (`max e(child) ≥
e(P)/2`), giving `S(n) ≥ ⌈log₂ n!⌉`, but it is not tight.

**Why this matters for complexity.** A `poly(n)`-bit, `poly(n)`-time-checkable
descent potential with `Φ(∅) ≤ k` is exactly an NP certificate for `S(n) ≤ k`;
a `poly(n)`-bit, `poly(n)`-time-checkable ascent potential with `Ψ(∅) > k` is
exactly a coNP certificate for `S(n) > k`. By D2/D3, a *tight* one must encode
`F`-equivalent information. So the certificate question **reduces to** the
existence of compact (`poly(n)`-size, `poly(n)`-time-checkable) proofs of tight
sorting bounds.

## 3. The two natural certificate formats are both obstructed

### 3.1 Explicit single strategies are exponentially large

`[deduction]` **Theorem 3.1 (no poly-size explicit strategy).** A single
deterministic sorting strategy, unfolded, is a binary tree (distinct reachable
nodes have distinct posets — Theorem 2.6 of `research-strategy-certificates.md`)
with `≥ n!` reachable leaves, hence `≥ 2·n! − 1` nodes. Under the unary encoding
(§0), `n! = 2^{Θ(m)}`, so `2·n! − 1 = 2^{Θ(m)}` is **exponential in the input
length**. Therefore **no `poly(n)`-bit explicit strategy certificate exists**,
and `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ NP` (if true) requires a fundamentally non-explicit witness.

### 3.2 Potentials are poly-sized but their descent proof is the bottleneck

`[deduction]` **Theorem 3.2 (verification bottleneck).** Given a potential `Φ`
as a `poly(n)`-bit formula, checking the descent property requires verifying
`∀ non-total P ∃{x,y} …` over all `2^{Θ(n²)}` posets — **exponential** — unless
the certificate also carries a `poly(n)`-size, `poly(n)`-time-checkable *proof*
of descent. A potential is a valid NP/coNP certificate **only if** its
descent/ascent property has such a compact proof.

`[deduction]` **The tightness–complexity tradeoff.** The candidates:

| potential | poly-time? | descent/ascent? | value at ∅ | certifies |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Φ₀ = #incomparable pairs` | yes | descent ✓ | `n(n-1)/2` | `S(n) ≤ n(n-1)/2` (trivial) |
| `Ψ₁ = ⌈log₂ e(P)⌉` | no (`e` is #P-complete, Brightwell–Winkler `[literature]`) | ascent ✓ / descent ✗ | `⌈log₂ n!⌉` | `S(n) ≥ ⌈log₂ n!⌉` only |
| `F` (minimax) | no (exponential in practice) | both ✓ | `S(n)` | exact, but not poly-checkable |

`[deduction]` The *only* poly-time-computable descent potential available is
`Φ₀`, which is loose by `~n²/2 − n log n`. Tightening it toward `F` requires
`#P`-complete quantities (`e(P)`) or optimality arguments. Hence **no
poly-time-computable tight descent potential is known**, and a poly-size
*potential* certificate for near-optimal `k` would have to be non-constructive
(a compact proof of a tight sorting upper bound).

## 4. Experiment: the information-bound potential is not a descent potential

`[experiment]` `scripts/unary_potential_certificates.py` tests descent for the
three potentials over all labelled posets (`A001035`). Reproduce:
`python3 scripts/unary_potential_certificates.py 5 6`.

```
n=5:  S(5)=7   Phi_0(∅)=10  Phi_1(∅)=7
      Phi_0 : DESCENT OK
      Phi_1 : NOT descent (120 failures)  witness e=8, F=4, Phi_1=3
      F     : DESCENT OK
      F <= Phi_0 everywhere: True     Phi_1 <= F everywhere: True

n=6:  S(6)=10  Phi_0(∅)=15  Phi_1(∅)=10
      Phi_0 : DESCENT OK
      Phi_1 : NOT descent (5760 failures) witness e=16, F=5, Phi_1=4
      F     : DESCENT OK
      F <= Phi_0 everywhere: True     Phi_1 <= F everywhere: True
```

`[experiment]` Findings:
1. `Φ₀` is a genuine descent potential (valid certificate primitive) but its
   budget `n(n-1)/2` is loose; it certifies only the trivial regime.
2. `Φ₁ = ⌈log₂ e⌉` — the "natural" tight-looking potential — is **not** a descent
   potential: 120 failures at `n=5`, 5760 at `n=6`, growing. So the information
   bound cannot serve as a YES certificate, confirming the `n=12` obstruction is
   already visible as a local descent failure at `n=5`.
3. `F` is the unique tight descent potential (`F(∅)=S(n)`), and `F ≤ Φ₀`
   (minimality, D2) and `Φ₁ ≤ F` (information bound) hold everywhere.

`[deduction]` This is the computational face of D2/D3: the gap between the best
poly-time potential (`Φ₀`, loose) and the tight potential (`F`, exponential)
is exactly the gap that a compact certificate would have to bridge.

## 5. Barriers (rigorous, unary-specific)

`[deduction]` **B1 (exponential explicit certificates).** By Theorem 3.1, any
single-strategy witness is `2^{Θ(m)}` bits. NP membership requires a non-explicit
witness. *(This sharpens Theorem 2.1/2.6 of `research-strategy-certificates.md`
with the unary input-length accounting.)*

`[deduction]` **B2 (verification bottleneck).** By Theorem 3.2, a potential
certificate is only as good as its compact descent proof. For near-optimal `k`
that is a compact proof of a tight sorting upper bound — not known.

`[literature]`+`[deduction]` **B3 (sparsity / Mahaney).** `SORT-BOUND-U` is sparse
(`≤ (m+1)²` strings of length `≤ m`). By Mahaney's theorem it is not NP-hard
under poly-time many-one reductions unless `P=NP` (Ogiwara–Watanabe for Turing
reductions). This blocks the *hardness* route for the unary language; it does
**not** decide NP or P membership.

`[literature]`+`[deduction]` **B4 (non-tightness is structural).** The `n=12`
obstruction (`S(12)=30 > 29=⌈log₂ 12!⌉`, Wells) is not scalar: sortability is
not monotone in `e(P)` (`n=6` witness, `research-n12-nontightness.md`), and no
function of `e(P)`, efficiency, or slack predicts tightness. Hence no
potential that is a *function of `e(P)`* can be tight, and the only known proofs
of `S(n) > k` for `k ≥ ⌈log₂ n!⌉` are exhaustive searches.

## 6. Status and next obstacle

| Statement | Status |
|---|---|
| `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ PSPACE` (game/ATM) | `[deduction]` Claim A |
| explicit single-strategy certificate is `poly(n)` | **impossible**, Thm 3.1 (`2^{Θ(m)}`) |
| `F` is the unique tight descent/ascent potential | `[deduction]` D1–D3 |
| `Φ₀` descent, loose; `Φ₁` not descent; `F` tight | `[experiment]` §4 |
| poly-time computable *tight* descent potential | `[open]`; none known |
| compact proof of a near-optimal sorting upper bound | `[open]`; would give NP |
| `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ NP` | `[open]`; obstructed by B1+B2 |
| `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ coNP` | `[open]`; obstructed by B2+B4 |
| `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ P` | `[open]`; widely doubted, B4 |
| `SORT-BOUND-U` many-one NP-hard ⟹ `P=NP` | `[literature]` Mahaney |

**Bottom line.** Under the unary encoding the certificate bar is `poly(n)` bits.
The potential/Lyapunov framework (D1–D3) shows that a tight YES/NO certificate is
*exactly* a compact proof of a tight sorting bound, and that the minimal descent
potential is the minimax value `F` itself. The two natural formats are both
obstructed: explicit strategies are provably exponential (B1), and potentials are
poly-sized but their descent proof is a global statement with no known compact
tight form (B2, B4). `PSPACE` remains the best known bound; `NP`, `coNP` and `P`
are open and share the same obstruction.

**Smallest compelling next step.** Either
(a) find a `poly(n)`-size, `poly(n)`-time-checkable proof of a near-optimal
sorting upper bound (would give `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ NP`), or
(b) prove that no compact tight descent potential can exist (would separate from
NP). Both are open and likely hard; (a) is the more concrete target, and the
Kahn–Saks/Kahn–Kim constant-factor potential (`≈ 2.17·log₂ n!`, `#P`-complete
to compute) is the natural "loose but valid" starting point to tighten.
