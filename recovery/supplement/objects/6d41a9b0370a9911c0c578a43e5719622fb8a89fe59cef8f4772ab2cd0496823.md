# Discriminative witnesses for the surplus parameterization of unary S(n)

**Status:** research note. Separates literature facts, deductions, experiments
and conjectures. No Lean files, no commits. Exploration front:
"Parameterized complexity of S(n) via surplus" (unary encoding only).

## 1. Precise question

For the adaptive worst-case comparison-sorting number S(n) and the
information lower bound L(n) = ceil(log2 n!), the **surplus** is
s(n) = S(n) - L(n). The threshold language SORT-BOUND-U = {1^n # 1^k : S(n) <= k}
is parameterized by s = k - L(n). The parameterized complexity question:

> Is SORT-BOUND-U fixed-parameter tractable in s? I.e., is there an algorithm
> deciding S(n) <= k in time f(s) * poly(n + k)?

Competing hypotheses:
- **H1 (bounded surplus):** s(n) is bounded by a constant → FPT trivial.
- **H2 (unbounded, structured):** s(n) grows predictably → FPT plausible.
- **H3 (unbounded, erratic):** s(n) non-monotonic/unpredictable → parameter
  meaningful but structure complex.
- **H4 (FPT possible):** ∃ f(s)·poly(n) algorithm.
- **H5 (FPT impossible):** no such algorithm exists.

This note reports only discriminative finite witnesses.

## 2. Surplus table and monotonicity (discriminates H1/H2/H3)

**Experiment.** Exact surplus s(n) = S(n) - L(n) for n = 0..22 using literature
values (Peczarski 2012, Stober-Weiss 2022). Script:
`scripts/surplus_discriminators.py`.

| n | S(n) | L(n) | s(n) | 2^L/n! | note |
|---|------|------|------|--------|------|
| 0-11 | — | — | 0 | — | tight |
| 12 | 30 | 29 | **1** | 1.1208 | RISE |
| 13-19 | — | — | 1 | — | |
| 20 | 62 | 62 | **0** | 1.8955 | **DROP** |
| 21 | 66 | 66 | **0** | 1.4442 | DROP |
| 22 | 71 | 70 | **1** | 1.0503 | RISE |

**Discriminative findings:**

1. **Surplus is non-monotonic.** It is 0 for n≤11, rises to 1 at n=12, drops
   back to 0 at n=20 and n=21, then rises again at n=22. This refutes any
   simple "surplus grows monotonically" hypothesis and supports H3 (erratic).

2. **Max surplus for n≤22 is 1.** No n≤22 has surplus ≥ 2. H1 (bounded) is
   **not refuted** by known data — it remains plausible that s(n) ∈ {0,1} for
   all n, which would make FPT trivial (hardcode two answers).

3. **The n=20 anomaly is the strongest discriminative witness.** Surplus drops
   to 0 at n=20 despite being 1 for n=12..19. The slack ratio 2^L(20)/20! =
   1.8955 is the **largest** in the table, yet the bound is tight. Conversely,
   n=22 has the **smallest** slack ratio (1.0503) and is not tight. This
   inverts the naive "small slack → non-tight" heuristic.

## 3. Local vs global surplus (discriminates local-gap propagation)

**Experiment.** For every poset P on n elements, compute local surplus
m(P) - ceil(log2 e(P)). Script: `scripts/surplus_discriminators.py`.

| n | total posets | surplus=0 | surplus=1 | max local gap |
|---|-------------|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| 5 | 4,231 | 4,111 (97.16%) | 120 (2.84%) | 1 |
| 6 | 130,023 | 114,213 (87.84%) | 15,810 (12.16%) | 1 |

**Discriminative finding:**

4. **Local gaps exist even when global surplus is 0.** At n=6, 12.16% of posets
   have local gap 1, yet S(6) = L(6) (global surplus 0). This shows that local
   information-bound failures do **not** propagate to global non-tightness. Any
   FPT algorithm must handle the fact that local gaps are common but globally
   "absorbed."

## 4. FPT hardness witness (discriminates H4 vs H5)

**Experiment.** Budgeted DFS CanSort(antichain, k) with memoization, measuring
call counts for different surplus values s = k - L(n). n=6, S(6)=10, L(6)=10.

| s | k | CanSort calls | time | result |
|---|---|--------------|------|--------|
| 0 | 10 | **18,696** | 1.285s | True |
| 1 | 11 | 840 | 0.044s | True |
| 2 | 12 | 903 | 0.021s | True |
| 3 | 13 | 998 | 0.006s | True |
| 4 | 14 | 1,200 | 0.007s | True |
| 5 | 15 | 1,315 | 0.006s | True |

**Discriminative finding:**

5. **Even s=0 requires exponential-in-n search.** At n=6, the tight case (s=0)
   requires 18,696 CanSort calls — this is the worst case, not s>0. The
   information-bound pruning does **not** collapse the True probe at d=S(n).
   This is a finite witness that deciding S(n) <= L(n) is not trivially easy:
   it requires searching a state space of size 2^{Theta(n^2)} (labelled posets).
   If FPT with s=0 were possible, we would need f(0)*poly(n) time, but the
   best known algorithm is 2^{O(n log^2 n)} (budgeted DFS). This supports H5
   (FPT impossible) but does not prove it.

6. **Counterintuitively, s=0 is harder than s>0** in call count. With s=1, the
   extra budget gives the search more room, reducing calls to 840. This
   discriminates the hypothesis that "larger surplus → harder decision." The
   parameter s does **not** measure difficulty monotonically.

## 5. Merge family surplus (discriminates family-specific behavior)

**Experiment.** Exact minimax on Merge(a,b) = disjoint union of a-chain and
b-chain. Script: `scripts/surplus_discriminators.py`.

| (a,b) | n | e(P) | L(P) | M(a,b) | gap |
|-------|---|------|------|--------|-----|
| (1,1)..(1,6) | 2-7 | — | — | — | 0 |
| (2,4) | 6 | 15 | 4 | 5 | **1** |
| (2,6) | 8 | 28 | 5 | 6 | **1** |
| (3,5) | 8 | 56 | 6 | 7 | **1** |
| (4,5) | 9 | 126 | 7 | 8 | **1** |
| (4,6) | 10 | 210 | 8 | 9 | **1** |
| (5,5) | 10 | 252 | 8 | 9 | **1** |
| (5,6) | 11 | 462 | 9 | 10 | **1** |
| (6,6) | 12 | 924 | 10 | 11 | **1** |

**Discriminative finding:**

7. **Merge family has surplus 0 or 1, never more.** Gaps appear at specific
   (a,b) pairs where the merge cost M(a,b) exceeds ceil(log2 C(a+b,a)). This is
   a controlled family with bounded surplus, showing that **some families have
   O(1) surplus**. But the gaps are real and predictable via the merge cost
   formula (Stockmeyer-Yao 1980, Li-Sun-Zhang 2016).

## 6. Slack ratio vs surplus (discriminates scalar predictors)

**Observation.** The slack ratio 2^L(n)/n! does **not** predict surplus:

| n | 2^L/n! | surplus | tight? |
|---|--------|---------|--------|
| 5 | 1.0667 | 0 | yes |
| 11 | 1.6812 | 0 | yes |
| 12 | 1.1208 | 1 | **no** |
| 20 | 1.8955 | 0 | yes |
| 22 | 1.0503 | 1 | **no** |

n=5 has smaller slack than n=12 yet is tight; n=20 has the largest slack and
is tight. No function of the slack ratio predicts surplus. This confirms the
H1 falsification in `docs/research-n12-nontightness.md` §5.

## 7. What was learned, failed ideas, next hypotheses

**Learned (discriminative):**

- Surplus is **non-monotonic** (drops at n=20, 21). This is a finite witness
  that the parameter is erratic, not structured. H3 supported.
- Max surplus for n≤22 is **1**. H1 (bounded) is not refuted. The FPT question
  is only meaningful if surplus can exceed 1 for some larger n.
- **Local gaps are common** (12.16% at n=6) even when global surplus is 0.
  Local gap does not imply global gap.
- **s=0 is the hardest case** for the budgeted DFS (18,696 calls at n=6),
  refuting "larger surplus = harder." This is the strongest FPT hardness
  witness: even the tight case requires exponential search.
- **Merge family** has O(1) surplus, showing some families are easy.

**Failed ideas:**

- "Surplus grows monotonically" — falsified by n=20 drop.
- "Larger surplus = harder decision" — falsified by call count inversion.
- "Slack ratio predicts tightness" — falsified by n=5/n=20 counterexamples.

**Next hypotheses to test:**

1. Does any n > 22 have surplus ≥ 2? This is the decisive question for H1 vs
   H2/H3. If surplus is always 0 or 1, FPT is trivial. If it reaches 2, the
   parameter becomes meaningful.
2. Is there a poly-time algorithm for deciding S(n) <= L(n) (the s=0 case)?
   The n=6 witness (18,696 calls) suggests not, but this is not a proof.
3. Can the non-monotonicity at n=20 be explained by a structural property
   (e.g., a specific poset family that becomes sortable exactly at n=20)?

## 8. Honest novelty assessment

- **Not new:** S(n) values for n≤22 (Peczarski, Stober-Weiss); the merge cost
  formula M(a,b) (Stockmeyer-Yao, Li-Sun-Zhang); the information-bound
  tightness for n≤11 and n=20 (Wells, Peczarski).
- **Original contributions of this note:** (i) the first explicit surplus
  table s(n) = S(n) - L(n) for n=0..22; (ii) the identification of n=20 as a
  non-monotonicity witness; (iii) the FPT hardness witness showing s=0 is the
  hardest case for budgeted DFS (18,696 calls at n=6); (iv) the local-vs-global
  surplus gap quantification (12.16% local gaps at n=6 with global surplus 0).
- **Bottom line:** The surplus parameter is **erratic** (non-monotonic) and
  **possibly bounded** (max 1 for n≤22). The FPT question is only meaningful
  if surplus exceeds 1 for some n. The strongest evidence against FPT is that
  even s=0 requires exponential search, but this is not a proof of
  parameterized hardness.
