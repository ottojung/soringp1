## Audit result: partial, with no all-\(n\) lower bound

The common-knowledge obstruction is a valid **necessary condition for sharing**, but it does not establish the requested superpolynomial lower bound. I found no proof or counterexample resolving whether the required incompatible pairs occur on a single optimal strategy for every sufficiently large \(n\). In particular, an incompatible pair at depth about \(S(n)/2\) is not presently established.

### (a) Precise sharing condition

Let \(Q_1,Q_2\) be the actual history posets at depth \(t\), after identifying their labels by permitted frames. A shared node with annotation \(P\) must satisfy
\[
P\subseteq Q_1\cap Q_2.
\]
It must sort every linear extension of \(P\) in at most \(k-t\) further comparisons. Writing \(F(P)\) for the exact minimax sorting number of \(P\), such a common annotation is possible **iff**
\[
F(Q_1\cap Q_2)\le k-t.
\]
Indeed, \(F\) is monotone under weakening a poset, so \(P\subseteq Q_1\cap Q_2\) implies \(F(P)\ge F(Q_1\cap Q_2)\); conversely, the intersection itself is an eligible annotation. Allowing frames, the condition becomes: there exist permitted relabellings \(\sigma_1,\sigma_2\) for which
\[
F\bigl(\sigma_1(Q_1)\cap\sigma_2(Q_2)\bigr)\le k-t.
\]
Thus incompatibility means that **every** permitted pair of relabellings has intersection minimax value greater than \(k-t\). The cited comment uses the weaker, readily stated necessary test \(e(\text{intersection})>2^{k-t}\); passing that test certifies incompatibility, but failing it does not prove compatibility.

### (c) Winning-frontier barrier

The \(2^{\Omega(n\log^2 n)}\) winning-state count does **not** combine by itself with incompatibility to yield a DAG lower bound. It counts states, not pairwise incompatibilities among states on one fixed optimal strategy. A valid combination would require a large clique (or chromatic lower bound) in the incompatibility graph of that strategy’s same-depth histories, accounting for all allowed relabellings. That is precisely the missing result.

### (d) Certificate and complexity implications

This is distinct from state counting: the relevant states must occur on a **single optimal strategy**, and the obstruction must rule out sharing under the certificate grammar’s permitted frames and forgetting. Even a superpolynomial lower bound for this framed-DAG grammar would not rule out other succinct certificates or prove a complexity lower bound for computing \(S(n)\). Polynomial-size framed certificates for every exact optimum would imply unary `SORT-BOUND-U ∈ NP`; neither that completeness claim nor its negation is established here.

The depth budget uses **exact** \(k=S(n)\). Sergeev’s \(\log_2(n!)+o(n)\) bound is only an asymptotic upper bound and cannot substitute for exact \(S(n)\) in the obstruction.
