## C1 — PROVED

Let \(x\) be comparable with every other element. Partition \(P-\{x\}\) into \(D=\{y:y<x\}\) and \(U=\{y:x<y\}\). Transitivity gives \(d<u\) for every \(d\in D,u\in U\). Hence in every linear extension of \(P\), the \(D\)-elements form an initial segment, then \(x\), then the \(U\)-elements. Deletion of \(x\) is therefore a bijection \(\operatorname{Ext}(P)\to\operatorname{Ext}(P-x)\), so \(e(P)=e(P-x)\). Comparisons involving \(x\) are predetermined, and comparisons among \(P-x\) have the same outcomes in \(P\) and \(P-x\); thus optimal trees for the two problems simulate each other. So \(m(P)=m(P-x)\). Iterating is immediate.

## C2 — PROVED

Assume every maximal deletion is non-core. For each maximal \(x\), choose a universal \(y_x\) of \(P-x\). Since \(P\) is a core, \(y_x\) is not universal in \(P\), so it is incomparable with \(x\): if it were comparable, maximality of \(x\) forces \(y_x<x\), making it universal in \(P\).

Suppose some \(y_x\) is nonmaximal. Choose maximal \(w>y_x\). Then \(w\neq x\) (else \(y_x<x\)), and \(w\) is incomparable with \(x\) (else \(w<x\), giving \(y_x<x\)). Consider \(y_w\). It cannot equal \(x\), because \(y_x\in P-w\) is incomparable with \(x\), so \(x\) is not universal in \(P-w\). Since \(x\) is maximal in \(P\), hence in \(P-w\), and \(y_w\) compares with \(x\), we get \(y_w<x\). Also \(y_x<w\). Since \(y_x\) is universal in \(P-x\), it compares with \(y_w\). If \(y_w<y_x\), then \(y_w<w\), contradicting the fact that \(y_w\) is incomparable with \(w\) (otherwise it would be universal in \(P\)). If \(y_x<y_w\), then \(y_x<x\), contradicting incomparability. Equality is also impossible: if \(y_w=y_x\), then \(y_x\) is universal in \(P-w\) and below \(w\), hence universal in \(P\). Thus every \(y_x\) is maximal.

A core has at least two maxima; with one maximal element that element is universal. Three maxima \(x_1,x_2,x_3\) are impossible: \(y_{x_1}\) is maximal and distinct from \(x_1\), hence is another maximum, but it must compare with the third maximum, while distinct maxima are incomparable. Thus exactly two maxima \(x,w\). Then \(y_x=w\) and \(y_w=x\). Hence every \(z\in P-\{x,w\}\) satisfies \(z<w\) and \(z<x\). The top \(\{x,w\}\) is a 2-antichain, and \(|P|\ge4\) makes \(P-\{x,w\}\) nonempty. Therefore \(P=(P-\{x,w\})(+)\{x,w\}\), a nontrivial ordinal sum, contradiction.
